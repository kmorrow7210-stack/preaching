# **COLD OPEN: THE BLANK CHECK**

*\[Walk to the front. No introduction. No greeting. Drop them into the story.\]*

Let’s talk about the First World War.

For forty years before 1914, Europe was a mess. Every power wanted more land, more colonies, more control over the trade routes. Britain had the biggest empire on the planet. France had claimed most of North Africa. Germany had only been united as a single nation since 1871, but they looked at a map and decided, 

*Hey, we want a seat at the table too.*

So Kaiser Wilhelm II took the throne in 1888 and started swinging. He built a navy to rival Britain’s. Doubled the size of his army. Before long, he was pushing into Africa, pushing into the Pacific, muscling into the Ottoman Empire’s backyard.

Eyebrows raised across the rest of Europe. So they started looking for allies. Making deals. France allied with Russia. Britain allied with France. Germany allied with Austria-Hungary. Every nation picked a partner—not because they liked each other, but because they were afraid of each other.

By 1914, the whole continent was a powder keg. If one country did the wrong thing, these alliances could bring the whole thing down.

And then on June 28th, 1914, a nineteen-year-old assassin put two bullets into the heir of the Austro-Hungarian throne. Archduke Franz Ferdinand.

Austria-Hungary wants war with Serbia. So they look at the Kaiser and say, *Are you with us?* Wilhelm doesn’t hesitate. He writes what historians call the **blank check**—unconditional support. Whatever you want to do, Germany’s got your back. He’s betting that Russia won’t step in.

Russia steps in.

Then France mobilizes. Germany invades Belgium to get to France. Britain enters because they have a treaty with Belgium. Within six weeks, all of Europe is about to explode.

Nobody thought this war was going to last. They thought the boys would be home by Christmas. But Christmas came and went. Then another. Then another. Then another.

They spent four years fighting in the trenches from the English Channel to Switzerland. They’d never been in a war like this. It began with French cavalry on horseback. Before long, they had machine guns, poison gas. Entire towns lost every young man they had in a single afternoon. Seventeen million dead before it was over.

And the Kaiser—the guy who wrote the blank check, the man who lit the match—by the fall of 1918, he couldn’t hold it together. His navy mutinied. His soldiers refused to fight. Revolutions breaking out in the German cities. His own chancellor walked out and announced to the press that the Kaiser was done.

Nobody asked Wilhelm first. He finds out he’s no longer the emperor the same way the German people do—from the news. Next morning, he’s on a train to the Netherlands. A five-hundred-year dynasty. Gone.

———

Two days later. November 11th, 1918\.

In Compiègne, France, there’s a railroad car sitting on the tracks. At one time, this was a dining car on the Orient Express. White linen, fancy silverware. But now it’s the field office for the supreme commander of the Allied forces.

The German delegates sit down to hear their terms.

Surrender the fleet. Surrender the submarines. Surrender the air force. Surrender the heavy artillery. An army that fielded thirteen million men—capped at a hundred thousand. The Rhineland occupied. And when the papers are signed, Foch refuses to shake their hands.

But that’s just the wound. Seven months later came the Treaty of Versailles. And that’s the twisting of the knife.

Particularly Article 231\. The war guilt clause. It said Germany must accept **complete responsibility** for starting the war. Not shared responsibility. Every bit of it—on them.

The head of the German delegation reads the terms and says to the Allied leaders: *“We can sense the full force of hatred that confronts us here. You demand we confess we were the only guilty party of the war. Such a confession in my mouth would be a lie.”*

But they signed it anyway. They didn’t have a choice.

Thirteen percent of their territory—gone. Forty-eight percent of their iron production—gone. Reparations set at $33 billion in 1918 dollars. A number so large that economists said out loud: it could never be paid.

Their economy collapsed. Hyperinflation hit so hard that people carried wheelbarrows of cash to buy a loaf of bread.

**The German empire took a mortal wound that day. And it died in a dining car.**

# **SCRIPTURE READING & PRAYER**

*\[Transition from the cold open. Do not resolve the dining car yet.\]*

Last week we saw the war in heaven. Spoiler alert—the dragon lost. Michael and the angels kicked him out. The dragon’s been disbarred. No more access to the courtroom of heaven. He lost his role as the accuser.

But he’s not done. He’s mad. And the last thing we see in chapter 12 is him standing on the shore of the sea.

Now, here’s a textual note. Some Bibles may read, *“I stood on the sand of the sea,”* like that’s John standing there. But the oldest manuscripts read *he* stood—third person. The dragon. He plants himself on the shore and stares out at the water. This is a defeated general calling for reinforcements.

He couldn’t kill the child. He couldn’t destroy the woman. So he does what every tyrant does when he loses power.

**He builds a system.**

For the first twelve chapters of Revelation, we’ve had the vantage point from heaven. Now in chapter 13, we’re transported back to earth. And what John sees next is what war looks like when it shows up in your city, in your economy, in your workplace. The dragon doesn’t show up in person. But he’ll sure send delegates.

# **THE COMPOSITE BEAST**

This thing has seven heads and ten horns. It’s wearing pieces of every empire that ever came against God’s people. John describes it: body of a leopard, feet of a bear, mouth of a lion.

Now, readers of this letter would have read Daniel 7\. Their ears would have perked up. Because Daniel sees four beasts rise from the sea—but he sees them one at a time. Spread across centuries. The lion was Babylon. The bear was Persia. The leopard was Greece. And the fourth beast was so terrible there wasn’t even a name for it.

It was Rome. A Roman Empire that Daniel hadn’t seen yet.

John sees one beast wearing all four. He’s not saying that Rome is the worst empire in a long line. He’s saying that Rome is the **concentration** of all of them.

Think about the Axis Powers in World War II. You had Imperial Japan ruling the Pacific. You had Fascist Italy trying to resurrect the Roman Empire. You had Nazi Germany rolling across Europe. Separate war machines. Separate dictators. But they signed a pact and merged into one global terror.

Every anti-God empire that ever rose from the chaos—Babylon, Persia, Greece—poured their blueprints into one creature.

**John is looking at the spirit of empire itself.**

———

## **THE COUNTERFEIT KINGDOM**

And the dragon gives this thing his power. Gives it his throne. Gives it his authority.

You catch that? That’s the same language the Father uses for the Son. *“All authority in heaven and earth has been given to me.”* The dragon has no originality. He’s running a counterfeit operation. He’s building a fake Trinity. The dragon plays the Father. The sea beast plays the Son. And we’re about to meet the third person.

But first—verse 3\.

# **THE MORTAL WOUND**

One of the heads has a mortal wound. But the wound heals. And the whole earth marvels and follows the beast.

Let’s go back to that dining car.

The German Empire was mortally wounded there in 1918\. The Kaiser’s gone. The army’s gutted. The economy obliterated. By every measure, the head was dead.

But twenty-two years later, a corporal who fought in the trenches of that same war—his name was Adolf—had that same dining car dragged out of the museum the French had built around it. He put it on the exact same tracks. The exact same clearing. He sat down in Foch’s chair—the same chair where Germany’s surrender was accepted—and he made France sign their own surrender in the same car, on the same spot. Walked out without speaking. Same insult.

Then he destroyed every monument in that clearing. Every stone. Every plaque. Every marker of Germany’s defeat. Except one. A statue of Foch. Left standing there alone, staring out over nothing.

**The head took a mortal wound. And it got back up.**

And the world watched it happen and couldn’t look away. They looked at that war machine and said, *“Who is like the beast? Who can fight against it?”*

———

## **THE STOLEN HYMN**

That question—“Who is like the beast?”—John’s audience caught it instantly. Because they grew up singing a different version.

Exodus 15\. The Song of the Sea. Israel standing on the far shore with Pharaoh’s chariots at the bottom of the ocean, singing: *“Who is like you, O LORD, among the gods?”*

The beast stole the hymn. Changed one word. Replaced the LORD with itself. And the world just sang along.

That’s what empire does. It doesn’t just demand obedience. It wants your *worship.* And it gets it—not always at swordpoint, but by being so big, so loud, so apparently invincible that you forget there’s anything else worth singing about.

———

## **THE THIRD REICH**

And they named what they built. They called it the *Third Reich.*

*Reich* is a German word that means “realm” or “empire.” The *First* Reich was the Holy Roman Empire—Charlemagne, crowned in 800 AD. A Christian empire that lasted a thousand years. The *Second* Reich was the German Empire that fell in 1918—the one that died in that dining car.

And now Hitler claimed his regime was the *Third*—the next resurrection of the same empire. He promised it would last a thousand years. A **Thousand-Year Reich.**

You hear that language? A kingdom that rises, falls, and rises again. A thousand-year reign. That’s not just political propaganda. That’s a man borrowing the vocabulary of scripture to dress up a counterfeit kingdom.

———

## **THE MARVEL**

And the world marveled.

*Blitzkrieg*—“lightning war.” That’s what they called the German strategy. Instead of trench warfare—the slow, grinding slaughter of the first World War—this was something nobody had ever seen. Tanks, aircraft, and infantry moving together at a speed that broke every military model on the planet. Poland fell in five weeks. Denmark in six hours. France—one of the great powers of Europe—fell in six weeks.

People marveled at the technology. The Stuka dive bombers had sirens bolted to their landing gear—they called them *Jericho trumpets*—so that when they dropped out of the sky, the sound alone sent people running before a single bomb hit the ground. The tanks rolled in formations so precise they looked choreographed. The V-2 rockets were the first man-made objects to reach space. The world looked at that machinery and said, *How is this possible? Who can match this?*

They marveled at the spectacle. The Nuremberg rallies packed two hundred thousand people into a single event. Searchlights shooting straight into the night sky—Albert Speer called it a “cathedral of light.” Columns of soldiers marching in perfect unison. Everything designed to make you feel small and make the Reich feel eternal.

They marveled at the speeches. Hitler didn’t just talk—he performed. He’d start slow, almost conversational, then build until he was screaming and the crowd was screaming with him. Propaganda films showed him descending from the clouds in an airplane—like some counterfeit messiah arriving from heaven—and crowds wept. They reached out to touch his car as it passed.

They didn’t fight. They didn’t question. They just marveled.

# **THE CYCLE**

And John’s audience had already lived this cycle.

They survived Caligula—the emperor so deranged he put a statue of himself in the temple. Then he was assassinated. They thought the nightmare was over.

But the beast just grew a new head. Along came Nero—the guy who used Christians as human torches until he stabbed himself in the throat. Rome plunged into civil war. It looked like the empire was going to collapse. Mortal wound.

Then it got back up. A general named Vespasian took over. The empire healed. And by the time John writes Revelation, there’s a man named Domitian on the throne—so cruel, so wicked that the early church called him “the Bald Nero.”

*\[Beat.\]*

Which would be a great name for an Italian restaurant, by the way.

*\[Let the laugh land.\]*

To them, it felt like the ghost of Nero had come back from the dead to finish the job.

That’s what the beast does. It dies. But it doesn’t stay dead. It takes a mortal wound and puts on a new outfit.

———

## **THE SYSTEM**

And look at how it operates while it’s up and running.

Verse 5—it’s given a mouth to speak blasphemies. Verse 7—it’s given authority over every tribe, tongue, people, and nation.

Now catch that four-part formula. *Every tribe, tongue, people, and nation.* You’ve heard that before. Revelation 5:9. That’s the song the elders sing to the Lamb: *“You purchased people for God from every tribe and tongue and people and nation.”* The beast is running the Lamb’s setlist with the serial numbers filed off.

And verse 8—all who dwell on earth will worship it. Everyone whose name has *not* been written **before the foundation of the world** in the book of life of the Lamb who was slain.

*Before the foundation of the world.* The Lamb’s death was not Plan B. The cross was not an emergency response. Before the beast ever crawled out of the sea, before the dragon ever fell from heaven, before Genesis 1:1—the Lamb was already slain and your name was already written.

**The kingdom was prepared before the beast was ever formed.**

# **THE LEASH AND THE QUESTION**

But here’s what most people miss. Verse 5—the beast exercises authority for **forty-two months.**

That number shows up everywhere in this book. It’s the same as 1,260 days. Same as Daniel’s “time, times, and half a time.” It’s three and a half years—a seven cut in half. That’s not a countdown on somebody’s calendar. That’s a theological statement. The beast gets half a week. God keeps the other half. Evil’s time is real, but it’s rationed.

**The beast is on a leash. And the leash has a length.**

It has a pre-appointed lifespan. The beast doesn’t get to decide when it’s done. God does. There’s no bunker hidden from the Almighty. There’s no Thousand-Year Reich that actually lasts a thousand years. The clock is already ticking the moment the beast opens its mouth.

But that raises the real question, doesn’t it? Okay, the beast is on a leash. It won’t last forever. 

**But what do we do in the meantime?**

John answers it. One sentence. Verse 10\.

**“Here is a call for the endurance and the faith of the saints.”**

That word *endurance*—the Greek is *hypomonē.* It doesn’t mean gritting your teeth. It doesn’t mean hiding in a basement until the beast’s time runs out. It means you see the beast for what it is, and you refuse to be impressed.

The text doesn’t say “here is a call for the *survival* of the saints.” It says endurance. And faith.

The danger isn’t that the beast kills you. It might. The danger is that you **marvel.** That you get so impressed by the size of the machine that you stop asking who built the original.

# **EVERY COUNTERFEIT PROVES THE REAL THING EXISTS**

Here’s what I need you to see.

Everything the beast has—everything—is borrowed.

The throne? Handed down from the dragon. A copy of the Father’s delegation to the Son.

The mortal wound that heals? Fake resurrection. The Lamb was actually slain. The beast just *looks* like it died.

The worship? Stolen from Exodus 15\.

The authority over every tribe, tongue, people, and nation? Lifted straight from the Lamb’s song in chapter 5\.

**This is not an invasion. It is a plagiarism.**

And here’s why that matters. **Every counterfeit proves the real thing exists.**

Nobody counterfeits a three-dollar bill. You only forge what has value. The fake resurrection only works because there’s a real one. The stolen hymn only works because the original is worth stealing.

So when you see the beast’s machinery running—in any era, in any empire—the right response is not fear. It’s recognition. *Oh. There it is again. The copy. Which means the original is still out there.*

And you look at the Lamb. The real one. Standing as though slain. Alive.

## **POLYCARP: THE ANCIENT REFUSAL**

Sixty years after John wrote this letter, one of his own students proved it.

After Jerusalem fell in AD 70, the center of gravity for the church shifted to Ephesus. That’s where John pastored. About thirty-five miles north was Smyrna—one of the seven churches that received this letter.

In Smyrna there was a young man named Polycarp. He converted young and grew up in the faith in the 80s and 90s—right in John’s orbit. Their relationship started as teacher and student. Over the years it became something deeper. Father and spiritual son. John mentored him, trained him, and eventually appointed him Bishop of Smyrna. Polycarp became a sort of living archive of everything John taught.

When Revelation was first unrolled and read aloud in that church, Polycarp was probably in his twenties. Already a leader. Standing in the room.

Remember what the letter to Smyrna said? Revelation 2:10.

*“Do not fear what you are about to suffer. Be faithful unto death, and I will give you the crown of life.”*

Polycarp heard his spiritual father speak those words over his church. And he carried them for sixty years.

At eighty-six years old, the Romans dragged him into the stadium. The crowd screaming. The proconsul offered him the deal. “Swear by the genius of the emperor. Say ‘Caesar is Lord,’ and you can go home.”

Just marvel at the beast, Polycarp. Just sing along.

This old pastor looked the Roman Empire in the face and said:

**“Eighty-six years I have served Him, and He has done me no wrong. How then can I blaspheme my King and my Savior?”**

The proconsul threatened wild beasts. Polycarp didn’t move.

He threatened fire. Polycarp told him to light it.

As the flames rose, Polycarp didn’t scream. He prayed. He gave thanks. He sang.

The beast said, “Marvel at me.” Polycarp said, “I’ve seen the real thing.”

———

## **CHURCHILL: THE MODERN REFUSAL**

Seventeen centuries later, the beast put on a new uniform.

Summer of 1940\. France has fallen in six weeks. The mortal wound from 1918 has healed. The beast is staring across the English Channel with the largest war machine the world has ever seen.

And a man with a cigar and a bulldog jaw stood before the House of Commons.

He didn’t promise glory. While the beast was offering the world a Thousand-Year Reich if they’d just submit, Churchill said: *“I have nothing to offer but blood, toil, tears, and sweat.”*

He looked at the propaganda machine. He looked at the dive bombers. He looked at a system that demanded total surrender. And he said:

*“We shall fight on the beaches. We shall fight on the landing grounds. We shall fight in the fields and in the streets. We shall never surrender.”*

Polycarp said it with a hymn in the flames. Churchill said it with a growl over the radio. Two thousand years apart. Same war. Same refusal.

**You cannot negotiate with the beast. You cannot out-politic it. You cannot cut a deal. You either marvel—or you stand.**

# **THE DINING CAR — ONE LAST TIME**

Let me take you back to that dining car one last time.

The beast took a mortal wound in 1918\. Got back up in 1940\. The whole world marveled.

But then the beast took another wound. The Thousand-Year Reich lasted twelve. The head healed—and then it died again. Because the counterfeit never holds. The copy always degrades.

Beast after beast after beast. Rising from the chaos. Taking a mortal wound. Getting back up. Dying again. An endless cycle of borrowed power running out.

But there is a Lamb who was slain before the foundation of the world. His wound was not fake. His death was not theater. And when He got back up, He didn’t need propaganda films. He didn’t need dive bombers. He didn’t need anybody’s compliance.

He walked out of a tomb. And He is still standing.

**The beast keeps dying and getting back up. The Lamb died once and will never die again.**

## **ALTAR**

So here’s my question.

What are you marveling at?

What system, what power, what voice has gotten so big and so loud that you’ve stopped singing the real song? What has crept in and replaced “Who is like the LORD?” with “Who can fight against the beast?”

Some of you have never given your life to the Lamb. You’ve been singing along with whatever empire was loudest your whole life. You’ve marveled at the counterfeit because you didn’t know the original existed. He exists. He’s here. His name is Jesus. Come to Him today.

Some of you know the Lamb. You gave Him your life. But somewhere along the way, you got impressed by the machine. The system got loud and you went quiet. You didn’t deny Him—you just stopped singing.

Polycarp was eighty-six. He’d been carrying his pastor’s words for sixty years. And when the fire came, he had one more song in him.

**You’ve got one more song in you too.**

**Come sing it.**
