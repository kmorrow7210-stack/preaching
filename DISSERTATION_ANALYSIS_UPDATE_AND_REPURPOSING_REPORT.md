# Dissertation Analysis, Update Map, and Repurposing Report

## Quick Verdict

The dissertation has a strong center: Pentecostal preaching is not only communication, inspiration, or pulpit style. It is a Spirit-empowered theological practice that forms the health, identity, and mission of a local congregation.

That center is worth keeping.

The dissertation as written should not be commercially published as a lightly edited book. It is too tied to D.Min. requirements, too autobiographical in the wrong places, too source-summary heavy, and too uneven in method language. But the argument underneath it is very publishable. The best path is to turn the dissertation into a minister-facing book, training curriculum, or content series on theological Pentecostal preaching as spiritual formation.

The new voice should not make the dissertation sound less like you. It should make it sound more like the writer you have become: clearer, firmer, more text-driven, less apologetic, less explanatory around the point, and more willing to let the theological claim carry the paragraph.

## What The Dissertation Is Really Saying

The stated research question is whether theologically sound Pentecostal preaching produces increased spiritual health in a local congregation.

The deeper claim is stronger:

Pentecostal congregations become spiritually thin when the pulpit keeps Pentecostal atmosphere but loses Pentecostal theology.

The dissertation is not merely about sermon improvement. It is about congregational formation. The pulpit is treated as one of the primary places where a church receives its theological imagination. If the pulpit is shallow, the church becomes shallow. If the pulpit is doctrinally faithful, Spirit-attentive, and pastorally local, it can help form healthier disciples.

The best version of the argument has four movements:

1. The pulpit forms a people.
2. Pentecostal preaching has often retained energy while losing theological density.
3. The Fivefold Gospel gives Pentecostal preaching a coherent theological spine.
4. A focused season of theological Pentecostal preaching corresponded with measurable spiritual growth in one rural congregation.

That is the book, article, course, and video series hiding inside the dissertation.

## Old Voice vs. Revelation Paper Voice

### The Revelation Paper Voice

The Revelation paper argues by movement. It names the issue early, follows the text closely, and introduces scholarly detail only where the detail sharpens the passage's function. It repeatedly uses contrast to create clarity:

- not creaturely force, but God's verdict
- not coercion, but witness
- not autonomous victory, but received victory
- not ordinary conquest, but the Lamb's shape

That voice is precise, restrained, and confident. It does not hurry to explain every background detail. It lets the argument control the sources.

### The Dissertation Voice

The dissertation has real substance, but the older voice often circles the point before landing it. It frequently gives biography, source summary, and background information before it tells the reader why that material matters. The personal sections are honest and compelling, but they are too long for the argument they are serving. The literature review is useful, but it often reads like a series of source reports rather than a guided argument.

The old draft says, "Here is everything I found, and here is how I got here."

The new voice should say, "Here is the theological hinge, here is how the evidence moves, and here is why it matters for the church."

## What I Changed In The Rewrite

The voice rewrite does five things:

1. It names the argument earlier and more directly.
2. It compresses autobiography so the story serves the thesis.
3. It turns literature-review summary into argumentative synthesis.
4. It keeps Pentecostal theology as the controlling center.
5. It treats the research design honestly, including limitations.

The rewrite is not a citation-ready final dissertation. It is a new main-body voice draft. The original source, appendices, data tables, and bibliography were left untouched.

## Source Update Map

The dissertation cannot really be retroactively updated as a completed research project, so the right move is not to pretend it is a new 2026 study. The right move is to mark where a future article, book, or revised edition would need new sources.

### 1. Chapter 1: Ministry Context

Update the Winston County demographic section. The original uses older county data. Current Census QuickFacts gives a cleaner and more authoritative baseline, including 2025 population estimate, age profile, racial makeup, broadband access, education, median household income, and poverty rate.

Useful source:

- [U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts: Winston County, Alabama](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/winstoncountyalabama/POP010220)

Best use:

Replace the long dated demographic paragraph with a tighter "ministry context" paragraph and move detailed data to a table or appendix.

### 2. Chapter 2: Practical Theology

Add a recent Pentecostal practical theology source. James Seager's 2023 article is a strong fit because it argues for a distinct classical Pentecostal method of practical theology and centers what Christ and the Spirit are saying or doing in experience.

Useful source:

- [James Seager, "Towards a distinct classical Pentecostal method of practical theology," Practical Theology 16.6 (2023)](https://research.bangor.ac.uk/en/publications/towards-a-distinct-classical-pentecostal-method-of-practical-theo/)

Best use:

Place this after Browning, Anderson, and Osmer. It would let you say: "General practical theology gives the method; Pentecostal practical theology gives the pneumatological posture."

### 3. Chapter 3: Pentecostal Homiletics

Add Taylor S. O. Drake's 2024 article on Pentecostal homiletic methodology. This is probably the cleanest single new source for the dissertation's core. It directly addresses how Pentecostals produce sermons and connects worldview, hermeneutics, doctrine-making, worship, and sermon practice.

Useful source:

- [Taylor S. O. Drake, "Towards a Pentecostal Homiletic: A Re-Enchanted Methodology," Religions 15.1 (2024)](https://doaj.org/article/e7f3fa24ffb64779bb720c0a5307e608)

Best use:

Use Drake to update "Bridging the Gap," "Pentecostal Distinctives," and the concluding implications. He gives you vocabulary for what your dissertation was reaching toward: Pentecostal preaching as a re-enchanted, Spirit-attentive, communal homiletic.

### 4. Chapter 3: Preaching As Event

Add newer work on preaching as an affective, embodied, divine-human event. The dissertation already argues this, but newer homiletical language could make it sharper.

Useful source:

- [Proclamation and Power: Toward a Phenomenology of Preaching and Its Affects](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/15/4/392)

Best use:

Use this in the "Preaching, Leadership, and Pastoral Care" section to strengthen the claim that preaching affects congregations at more than the cognitive level.

### 6. Chapter 4 and 5: Methodology

This is where a revised dissertation would need the most technical cleanup.

Fix or verify:

- "T-Wilcoxon test" should likely be clarified as Wilcoxon signed-rank test.
- The project says a control group would be used, but the final analysis appears to report only the intervention group.
- The sermon implementation date says March and April 2001, which is almost certainly wrong.
- The Fivefold Gospel list duplicates Spirit baptism in one place and omits healing.
- SAI table labels are inconsistent: acceptance/awareness and realistic acceptance appear with different wording.
- SPF results say seven categories in one place and eight in another.
- The age bracket shifts between 15-54 and 18-54.
- Table 6 duplicates "Confession" and omits one expected discipline.

Best use:

If this becomes a public article or book, do not overclaim the data. Say: "In one congregational case study, a focused preaching intervention corresponded with measurable growth in several spiritual-health and spiritual-practice indicators." That is strong and honest.

## How To Improve It As A Dissertation

### 1. Rebuild The Thesis Around Formation

Current form:

"What effect does theological preaching have on the health of a local congregation?"

Stronger form:

"This project argues that Spirit-empowered theological preaching functions as a formative practice in Pentecostal congregational life, strengthening spiritual awareness, Pentecostal identity, and participation in spiritual disciplines."

That version is more specific and more publishable.

### 2. Cut The Autobiography By At Least Half

Your story matters. It explains why this project is yours. But in dissertation form, the personal narrative should be controlled by the argument. The strongest parts are:

- early loss and Pentecostal encounter
- theological awakening through ministry and seminary
- pastoral failures that taught you the need for formation
- Houston as the concrete ministry site
- desire to train ministers

The rest can be moved to a memoir, preface, or appendix.

### 3. Turn Chapter 3 From Source Summary Into Argument

The literature review should not feel like a bookshelf tour. It should feel like a courtroom brief.

Recommended structure:

- Preaching as theological event
- Preaching as practical theology
- Preaching as pastoral leadership and care
- Pentecostal preaching as Spirit-Word encounter
- Pentecostal distinctives as congregational formation
- Why this study is needed

Every source should answer one question: What does this source contribute to the claim?

### 4. Strengthen The Method Section

The method chapter needs tighter research language. It should clearly identify:

- study type
- sample
- intervention
- instruments
- procedure
- statistical test
- limitations
- ethical/privacy protections
- why SAI and SPF fit the research question

Right now the method is understandable, but not polished.

### 5. Treat Limitations As Strength, Not Embarrassment

The small, rural, racially homogeneous congregation is a limitation. It is also the truth of the context. Name it clearly.

The strongest claim is not universal proof. It is practical-theological evidence from one Pentecostal congregation.

### 6. Fix The Tables And Timeline

Before this can go anywhere public, fix the obvious issues:

- "WELEYAN-PENTECOSTAL"
- "Data Anaylysis"
- "waves" should likely be "waives"
- 2001 implementation date
- December 2023 vs. 2022 graduation timeline
- duplicate Spirit baptism sermon slot
- seven-vs-eight SPF result inconsistency
- duplicated "Confession" in Table 6

These are not deep flaws, but they signal carelessness if left unfixed.

## What This Says About You

### As A Pastor

You are fundamentally a shepherd, not a platform-builder. The dissertation shows a pastor who wants to know whether the people are actually becoming healthier. That is not ego work. That is shepherd work.

You are relationally driven, deeply shaped by local ministry, and drawn toward people who need care, belonging, and spiritual direction. The weakness is that your empathy can make you over-explain or soften the sharp edge of the argument. The strength is that your theology keeps returning to actual people, not abstractions.

### As A Preacher

You believe preaching is an event with God. That is the best thing about the dissertation. You do not treat the sermon as content delivery. You treat it as a Spirit-Word encounter that should form the church.

The older draft reveals a preacher still trying to defend that instinct academically. The newer Revelation paper reveals a preacher-scholar who can now state the claim with more precision. You no longer have to prove that Pentecostal preaching matters by talking louder on the page. You can argue it.

### As A Writer

You are a real writer, but the dissertation is not your strongest writing form. It is a record of a writer who had substance before he had full control of his academic voice.

Your older writing explains too much, leaves too many transitions visible, and sometimes uses source summary as a substitute for argument. Your current writing is better because it trusts the reader more and trusts the claim more. The Revelation paper shows you have learned how to make a paragraph move.

Bluntly: the dissertation sounds like a pastor trying to earn academic permission. The Revelation paper sounds like a theologian-preacher making an argument.

That is growth.

### As A Content Creator

The dissertation points toward a strong creator lane, but not a broad lifestyle or commentary lane. Your best lane is:

**pastoral-theological translation for serious church leaders and thoughtful laypeople.**

You can take dense theological work and turn it into a usable ministry question:

- What is the pulpit forming?
- What doctrine is missing?
- What kind of people is this sermon making?
- Are we mistaking emotional force for spiritual health?

That is a commercially viable lane because it is not generic. It has a clear audience: pastors, ministers-in-training, Pentecostal leaders, sermon makers, and serious church people who know something is thin but do not yet have language for it.

## Final Recommendation

Do not try to update the dissertation as a dissertation.

Use it as source material.

The best next artifact is a short minister-facing book proposal or workshop outline built around this thesis:

**Pentecostal preaching is not faithful because it is loud, spontaneous, or emotionally charged. It is faithful when the Spirit makes Scripture and theology live in the gathered church for the formation of healthier disciples.**

That is the sentence everything else can grow from.
