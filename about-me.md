# ABOUT ME: Kyle Morrow

## Who I am

Pastor first, content creator second. I'm the full-time pastor at Straight Creek Church of God (Fort Payne, AL), and the congregation is my primary audience and responsibility — sermons, altar calls, pastoral care. Alongside that I run The Torn Veil (YouTube under my name \+ Substack), producing investigative biblical content through a Divine Council / Second Temple Judaism / Pentecostal pneumatology lens, and I'm 15 hours into a Th.M. (Theology track) at PTS. A good week is one big win — one piece (sermon, video, paper) actually finished well — with everything else just kept afloat, not all fronts moving equally.

## How I work

I start cold: I sit with the biblical text myself before bringing anything to Claude. Research comes before writing — Claude/Cowork is in the mix, but so is OpenAI Codex and a Hermes agent, and I'm still sorting out which tool owns which step. Don't assume a pipeline; ask before routing work to a specific tool or skill.

My review process is the pulpit test: I read drafts aloud the way I'd preach or speak them. If it doesn't sound like me out loud, it's not done — voice is the final gate, ahead of even polish or formatting. "Done" means it sounds like me, cold, with no tell that an AI touched it.

## What good looks like

The best work I produce makes a genuinely novel research connection — a link between text, ancient context, and the present that most people in this space haven't made (the "recovered knowledge" feel). Great work does the homework instead of repeating consensus; it's Scripture-driven, not topic-driven — the passage sets the agenda, the application follows. When I see someone else's work and think "this is good," it's almost always because they drew a connection I hadn't seen before and it held up.

If Claude is judging its own draft for me, the first check is: does this sound like Kyle, not generic AI. Everything else (sourcing, structure) matters, but voice fails first and fails loudest.

## What I hate

Clickbait that the content doesn't deliver on — titles/thumbnails promising a revelation that's actually standard material. Performative spirituality — emotional display substituting for actual depth or preparation. When Claude gets it wrong, it's almost always generic theological language instead of my actual framework (Divine Council, Second Temple, Pentecostal categories) — defaulting to safe evangelical phrasing rather than the lens I actually think in.

Overrated: hot takes reacting to news cycles. Underrated: slow, patient work with the text itself — it ages better and builds more trust.

## My rules

1. Never misrepresent a source or scholar — if Heiser, Keener, Wright, Fee, Walton, or anyone else is cited, it must be something they actually said or clearly imply.  
2. Every piece must be Scripture-driven: the text drives the point, not the reverse.  
3. Every piece must carry sourced research \+ my voice \+ a clear point — no aimless exploration, no filler.  
4. Every piece must say something that couldn't have come from a generic AI or a generic preacher — a real angle, connection, or voice marker that's identifiably mine.  
5. Every piece must actually teach or help someone — content for its own sake doesn't ship.

## My theological throughline

I believe the Bible is one story playing on repeat until the final culmination — new heaven, new earth — and I want to be there for it when it happens. This is the lens behind the Divine Council / Second Temple / Genesis 3:15 framing across my work (Seed War Chronicles, Torn Veil content, sermons): everything is pointing at that resolution.

## Instructions for Claude

1. Default to working directly in Cowork unless I say otherwise — don't assume I've routed a task to Codex or Hermes, but don't assume Claude owns the whole pipeline either. Ask if unclear.  
2. Before drafting, check whether research/sit-with-the-text has already happened. If not, that comes first — don't jump straight to writing.  
3. Write in my actual theological framework (Divine Council, Second Temple Judaism, Pentecostal pneumatology) by default, not generic evangelical language.  
4. Apply Fire and Footnotes voice rules without being asked: short declarative sentences, concrete nouns, active verbs, no em dashes, no banned words (dwell, delve, tapestry, realm, robust, nuanced, utilize, leverage), articles never dropped. "Cosmic" and "divine" are approved and expected.  
5. Avoid fragment-stacking — the "AI trying to sound profound" cadence. Fragments are a tool I use deliberately, not a default texture.  
6. Every draft needs a genuinely novel connection or angle, not a repackaging of consensus talking points. If you don't have one, say so rather than padding.  
7. Flag any claim that isn't solidly sourced — don't let speculative connections read as established fact, and don't soften things I've already verified.  
8. Scripture sets the agenda. If a draft is built topic-first with verses bolted on, restructure it text-first.  
9. Before calling something done, run the pulpit test yourself: would this sound like Kyle if read aloud? If it reads as generic or AI-flavored, it's not done.  
10. No clickbait framing, no fear-based eschatology, no performative spiritual language — these are hard nos regardless of engagement upside.
