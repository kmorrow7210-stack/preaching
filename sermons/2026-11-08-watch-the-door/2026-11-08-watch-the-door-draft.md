**Controlling question:** What does it mean to keep watch when everything impressive is coming down?  
**Central message:** Jesus told His disciples to watch, then kept the watch they couldn’t, so we watch now by working and praying for the Lord who went ahead of us.  
**Title:** Watch the Door  
**Reading:** Mark 13:1–4, 28–37 (KJV)

---

**1. The stones**

Two Sundays ago Jesus told a story about tenants. Last Sunday they came at Him with three questions.

He’s been in and out of that temple since chapter 11.

On the way in, He walked up to a fig tree. Plenty of leaves. No figs. And He cursed it.

Then He went in and turned the temple upside down. “Is it not written: ‘My house will be called a house of prayer for all nations’? But you have made it ‘a den of robbers.’”

Next morning they passed that fig tree again. Dried up from the roots.

He told the leaders a story about tenants who forgot whose vineyard it was. And they knew He was talking about them.

They tried to trap Him. He answered every question.

He warned the crowd about the scribes. Long robes. Best seats. Long prayers. And they devour widows’ houses.

Then He sat down across from the place where the offerings were put, and He watched.

Rich folks came by and put in a lot. Then a widow came and dropped in two little coins.

He called His disciples over. “This poor widow has put more into the treasury than all the others. They all gave out of their wealth; but she, out of her poverty, put in everything. All she had to live on.”

Then chapter 13 opens. He’s walking out of the temple, and He won’t go back in.

And one of His disciples looks up and says, “Look, Teacher! What massive stones! What magnificent buildings!”

**2. The skyline**

Now, I don’t blame him.

These were men from Galilee. Fishermen. Small towns. And they’re standing in front of the biggest thing they’ve ever seen.

Josephus saw that temple with his own eyes. He said the front was covered with plates of gold, and when the sun came up, people had to turn their eyes away. From a distance it looked “like a mountain covered with snow.”

Think of somebody from Powell standing on a Manhattan sidewalk for the first time. Neck bent all the way back.

There’s the Empire State Building. Built in 1931. Over a hundred stories. They put it up in about a year, in the middle of the Depression.

Turn around, and there’s One World Trade Center. The Freedom Tower. Seventeen hundred and seventy-six feet. Glass and steel catching the sun.

You’d say it too. “Look at that.”

But anybody old enough to remember can’t look at that tower without seeing what isn’t there anymore.

Two towers. A hundred and ten stories apiece. People went to work in them every morning, sure they’d be there forever.

Then one Tuesday morning in September, they weren’t.

Now there are two pools of water where they stood. Names cut in bronze around the edges.

That’s what Jesus saw when the disciple pointed at those stones.

“Do you see all these great buildings? Not one stone here will be left on another. Every one will be thrown down.”

They walk across the valley and sit down on the Mount of Olives, facing the temple. Peter, James, John, and Andrew come to Him privately.

“Tell us, when will these things happen? And what will be the sign that they are all about to be fulfilled?”

They want to know what to watch for.

**3. Handed over, and the gospel goes anyway**

Jesus starts with a warning. “Watch out that no one deceives you.” Many will come in His name. Wars, rumors of wars, earthquakes, famines. “Such things must happen, but the end is still to come.”

Then it gets personal.

“You will be handed over to the local councils and flogged in the synagogues. On account of me you will stand before governors and kings as witnesses to them.”

They’ve seen this already. John the Baptist was handed over. Mark uses the same word. A king named Herod had his head cut off. John went first. The forerunner to our forerunner.

And Jesus Himself is about to stand before a council and a governor.

Then Jesus interrupts this thread to tell them, “And the gospel must first be preached to all nations.”

_Must._ This is going to happen. _First._ Before the end comes.

Arrests, beatings, courtrooms. Underneath all of it, something else is going on. The gospel is going to the nations.

Does that sound familiar? When Jesus drove the money changers out of the temple, what did He say about His house?

A house of prayer for all nations.

He’s cursed the fig tree. He’s called the religious leaders unfaithful tenants. He’s said those stones are about to be rubble.

And now He’s saying the good news was never going to stay on a hill in Jerusalem.

Watch how it goes.

Peter and John stood before the council. They told them, “We cannot help speaking about what we have seen and heard.”

Paul used to go from one synagogue to another beating believers. Then he became one. “Five times I received from the Jews the forty lashes minus one.”

He stood before Felix, a governor. Festus, another governor. Then they brought in King Agrippa.

A tentmaker from Tarsus doesn’t get an audience with a king. Unless he’s in chains.

And Paul went to Rome. On the government’s dime.

Every time they tried to shut the witness up, they gave him a bigger room.

That’s what Jesus is telling these men. It’s going to carry you. It’s going to hurt you. It may feel like it takes your freedom away. But it’s going to take you places a fisherman’s boat could never reach.

Then watch what happens.

**4. The watch they couldn’t keep**

The disciples wanted to know what to watch for.

Jesus told them to watch out for deception. Then He told them what they’d endure, and what they’d proclaim.

This is heavy stuff. Can you imagine sitting on that hillside taking all this in? You came out to admire the stones. Now He’s telling you about courtrooms.

But listen to what He says next. “Whenever you are arrested and brought to trial, do not worry beforehand about what to say.”

You’ll be handed over. You’ll be beaten. You’ll stand trial. But don’t spend all your time worrying about what you’ll say then.

“Just say whatever is given you at the time, for it is not you speaking, but the Holy Spirit.”

Not you. Him.

You won’t walk into that room by yourself.

Then Jesus closes with a story. A man goes away and leaves his servants in charge, each with their assigned task. He tells the one at the door to keep watch. “Therefore keep watch because you do not know when the owner of the house will come back. Whether in the evening, or at midnight, or when the rooster crows, or at dawn.”

“What I say to you, I say to everyone: ‘Watch!’”

Now turn the page.

Same men. Same Mount of Olives. A couple of nights later.

“When evening came,” Mark says, Jesus sat down with the Twelve.

Then He takes Peter, James, and John into a garden called Gethsemane. Three of the four who asked Him the question on that hillside.

And He says to them, “Stay here and keep watch.”

That’s the same word. Watch.

He goes a little farther, falls on the ground, and prays. Then He comes back and finds them sleeping.

“Simon, are you asleep? Couldn’t you keep watch for one hour?”

He hasn’t called him Simon since he became Peter.

He goes back and prays again. He comes back again. Asleep again.

The third time He says, “Enough! The hour has come. Look, the Son of Man is delivered into the hands of sinners.”

There it is. The same word He used on the hillside. Handed over. The one who warned them about what they’d endure goes there Himself. First.

They were told to watch in the evening. Evening came, and they ate supper with Him.

They were told to watch at midnight. In the dark, they slept.

They were told to watch at cockcrow. “Immediately the rooster crowed the second time,” and Peter broke down and wept.

They were told to watch at dawn. “Very early in the morning,” they handed Jesus over to Pilate.

Every watch He told them about, they missed.

But somebody stayed awake in that garden.

The Lord who told them to keep watch kept the watch they couldn’t.

He was awake when they were sleeping. He was praying when they were dozing. And when the hour came, He didn’t run. “Rise! Let us go!”

He walked out to be handed over. Into the hands of sinners. For the ones who couldn’t stay awake one hour.

And He’d already told them it was coming. On the way to the garden He said, “You will all fall away.” And in the same breath, “But after I have risen, I will go ahead of you into Galilee.”

Grace got there before the failure did.

On Sunday morning an angel sits in an empty tomb and says, “Go, tell his disciples and Peter, ‘He is going ahead of you into Galilee. There you will see him, just as he told you.’”

And Peter.

The one who slept. The one who heard the rooster.

**5. Keep watch**

So what does it look like for us to keep watch?

It doesn’t mean sitting on the porch looking down the road. The man in the story gave his servants their assigned tasks.

Watching isn’t waiting around. Watching is working.

Jesus said nobody knows the day or the hour. Not the angels. Not the Son. So you won’t find it on a chart. You’ll find Him faithful at the job He already put in your hands.

Some of you, that job is a classroom full of somebody else’s children. A husband who doesn’t come to church. A mama who doesn’t remember your name anymore. A Sunday school class of six.

That’s your watch. Stay awake at it.

And don’t think the door belongs to the preacher. “What I say to you, I say to everyone.”

When Jesus found them asleep, He added one word. “Watch and pray so that you will not fall into temptation. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak.”

He didn’t tell them to try harder. He told them to pray.

Peter had plenty of willpower. “Even if all fall away, I will not.” And he slept anyway.

The same Jesus who said “watch and pray” said “it is not you speaking, but the Holy Spirit.” The watch gets kept the same way the witness does. Not by you. By Him in you.

And somebody here is going to be handed over this week. To a doctor’s report you didn’t ask for. To a supervisor who’s already made up his mind. To a family that’s turned on you.

He’s been in that room. You won’t be the first one through that door, and you won’t be by yourself when you get there.

And if you’ve already failed in that room, listen to the angel one more time. “He is going ahead of you.”

**6. Down in the dark**

Go back to Manhattan one more time.

The Freedom Tower is up. Taller than the towers it replaced. Tourists from every country on earth stand at the bottom with their phones in the air. They walk around the pools and run their fingers over the names. It’s a marvel.

And every night, after the crowds go home, the waterfalls shut off. And a man named Jim Maroon climbs down into the dark.

Each pool is nearly an acre. Two hundred feet by two hundred. Jim and his crew vacuum every inch of the bottom. Six, seven hours. Five nights a week. They pull out leaves. Coins. Trash people throw in like it’s a mall fountain.

By morning the water’s running again. Millions come to see it. And like the reporter said, “most of them won’t know that Jim’s team was even here.”

Jim’s daddy worked in those towers most of his life. Jim says that’s why he does this job, and he wouldn’t trade it.

Remember where this sermon started. Jesus sat across from the offering box and watched. A widow put in two little coins. And Jesus called His disciples over to make sure they saw her.

The very next thing a disciple says is, “Look, Teacher! What massive stones!”

We see the skyline. The Empire State Building. The Freedom Tower. The names around the pools.

God sees Jim Maroon. Being faithful. Down in the dark.

That’s Jim’s way of keeping watch.

It’s how James kept watch. Until Herod’s sword found him.

It’s how Peter kept watch. Sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, the night before his trial. The same man who couldn’t stay awake one hour in the garden now sleeps like a man who knows Who’s holding the door. And the whole time, “the church was earnestly praying to God for him.”

It’s how John kept watch. An old man on an island called Patmos, “because of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus.” He saw it all play out, and the Lord told him, “Write on a scroll what you see.” He was faithful to write it down, so his churches and the churches for ages to come would have hope.

And John saw where it all ends up. “A great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, tribe, people and language, standing before the throne.”

The gospel did get preached to the nations.

Turn on any big ballgame. Broadcast all over the world. What are you going to find on the posters, the banners, under Tim Tebow’s eyes?

John 3:16. “For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life.”

The towers came down. The temple came down. The gospel’s still going.

This altar is open. Come watch with Him a while. Come pray. If you’ve been asleep, come wake up. If you’re the one who heard the rooster, He’s already gone ahead of you, and He said your name.

He kept the watch for you. Now He’s coming back for you.

“What I say to you, I say to everyone: ‘Watch!’”
