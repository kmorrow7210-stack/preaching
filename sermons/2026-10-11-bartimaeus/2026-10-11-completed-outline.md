# Completed outline: Bartimaeus, Mark 10:46–52

Built on Kyle's outline from Sunday morning (points 1–2 are his, lightly extended). Body quotes are NIV, checked against `mark-8-9-niv.md` and `mark-10-niv.md`. The refrain and closing lines come from the adopted [[2026-10-11 Stand plan - Bartimaeus (draft)]].

## Big idea
**You can know who He is and still miss His road.**
Real sight is more than knowing who Jesus is. It's seeing where He's going and following Him there.

**Second thread (new):** people who can't see His road end up standing in the way of the people trying to get to Him.

## Summary (Kyle's)
Between the blind man at Bethsaida, who sees in stages and is sent home, and Bartimaeus, who sees immediately and follows Jesus on the road, Mark puts three passion predictions, the disciples' repeated misunderstandings, the question "What do you want?", and the rich man's departure. Together they show the difference between recognizing Jesus as Messiah and truly seeing what it means to follow Him.

---

1. Bartimaeus
   1. Blind
   2. Sat by the roadside – *hodos* – alongside the road. He starts *beside* the way.
   3. Heard Jesus is coming
   4. Cries out, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!" (10:47) – the edge becomes the road
      1. First time in Mark anyone calls Jesus "Son of David." A king's title. The man who can't see anything can see who Jesus is.
      2. Tease, don't teach yet: "Mark has been setting this moment up for three chapters. Hang on, I'm going to show you." *(Move the three predictions to point 4 so you only walk through them once.)*
2. Crowd tells him to hush
   1. Perhaps they were traveling to Jerusalem and didn't want to be interrupted.
   2. Perhaps they were afraid of what calling a man "Son of David" might stir up.
   3. Regardless, they were trying to keep him from reaching Jesus.
      1. NIV: "Many **rebuked** him and told him to be quiet" (10:48). Hold on to that word "rebuked." We'll come back to it.
   4. But I love his response.
      1. He doesn't quiet down. He shouts all the louder: "Son of David, have mercy on me!"
   5. Transition: To see why this blind man matters so much, we have to go back to the *first* blind man.

3. The other blind man: Bethsaida (8:22–26)
   1. Tell his story
      1. "Some people brought a blind man and begged Jesus to touch him" (8:22).
      2. Jesus "took the blind man by the hand and led him outside the village" (8:23).
      3. Spit on his eyes, put His hands on him: "Do you see anything?"
      4. "I see people; they look like trees walking around" (8:24).
      5. "Once more Jesus put his hands on the man's eyes… and he saw everything clearly" (8:25).
      6. "Jesus sent him home, saying, 'Don't even go into the village'" (8:26).
   2. Jesus didn't miss. This isn't a weak healing. I believe Mark is showing us a picture.
      1. Just before: "Do you have eyes but fail to see?" (8:18)
      2. Just after: Peter's confession (8:27–29). Peter is about to see Jesus partly. Trees walking.
   3. Notice the differences

      | | Bethsaida (8:22–26) | Bartimaeus (10:46–52) |
      |---|---|---|
      | Name | Unnamed | Named, and his father too |
      | How he got to Jesus | Others brought him and begged | Nobody brought him. He cried out himself, and others tried to stop him |
      | Where | Led out of the village, away from people | On the road, in front of the crowd |
      | What Jesus asked | "Do you see anything?" | "What do you want me to do for you?" |
      | How | Spit and two touches | A word: "Go, your faith has healed you" |
      | Faith mentioned? | No | "Your faith has healed you" |
      | Speed | In stages | "Immediately" |
      | Afterward | Sent home | "Followed Jesus along the road" |
      | Where in the story | Galilee, start of the journey | Jericho, last stop before Jerusalem |

   4. The point: these two men are bookends. Everything between them is about the disciples, who are stuck at "trees walking." They can see Jesus. They can't see Him clearly yet.

4. Between the bookends: three times Jesus tells them where the road goes

   | | Prediction | How the disciples respond | What Jesus teaches |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | 8:31 | 8:32–33 Peter rebukes Him | 8:34–38 Take up your cross |
   | 2 | 9:31 | 9:32–34 Don't understand, afraid to ask, argue about who's greatest | 9:35–37 Be last, servant of all, welcome a child |
   | 3 | 10:33–34 | 10:35–41 James and John ask for the best seats | 10:42–45 Serve; "to give his life as a ransom for many" |

   1. Prediction 1 – Caesarea Philippi
      1. "On the way" (8:27) – *hodos* again. This whole section happens on the road to Jerusalem.
      2. Peter gets the title right: "You are the Messiah" (8:29).
      3. Jesus: the Son of Man must suffer, be killed, and rise (8:31).
      4. "Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him" (8:32). *There's that word again.*
      5. "Get behind me, Satan!… merely human concerns" (8:33).
      6. Teaching: "deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me" (8:34).
      7. **Refrain #1:** Peter saw the title. He couldn't see the cross. *You can know who He is and still miss His road.*
      8. Connecting stories (mention, don't preach): the mountain and the valley you preached last week (9:2–29).
   2. Prediction 2 – passing through Galilee
      1. "The Son of Man is going to be delivered into the hands of men. They will kill him, and after three days he will rise" (9:31).
      2. "They did not understand what he meant and were afraid to ask him about it" (9:32).
      3. Then: "What were you arguing about on the road?" Silence, "because on the way they had argued about who was the greatest" (9:33–34).
      4. Teaching: "Anyone who wants to be first must be the very last, and the servant of all" (9:35). He takes a child in His arms (9:36–37).
      5. Connecting stories
         1. John: "we told him to stop, because he was not one of us" (9:38). Jesus: "Do not stop him" (9:39). The disciples are starting to decide who gets near Jesus.
         2. The children (10:13–16). *Save this for point 6.*
         3. The rich man (10:17–22)
            1. "What must I do to inherit eternal life?" A road he builds himself, out of what he can do. *(Say "I believe.")*
            2. "Jesus looked at him and loved him" (10:21). Keep this in the same breath. Grief, not scorn.
            3. "Go, sell everything… Then come, follow me."
            4. "He went away sad, because he had great wealth" (10:22).
            5. "How hard it is for the rich to enter the kingdom of God!" (10:23)
   3. Prediction 3 – on the road up to Jerusalem
      1. "Jesus leading the way, and the disciples were astonished, while those who followed were afraid" (10:32).
      2. The most detail yet: condemned, handed to the Gentiles, mocked, spit on, flogged, killed, and three days later He'll rise (10:33–34).
      3. And the very next thing: James and John. "Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we ask" (10:35).
      4. Jesus: **"What do you want me to do for you?"** (10:36). *Plant this. It's the same question He'll ask Bartimaeus.*
      5. "Let one of us sit at your right and the other at your left in your glory" (10:37).
      6. "You don't know what you are asking" (10:38). Turn to 15:27: "They crucified two rebels with him, one on his right and one on his left." That's who ends up at His right and left. The Son of David's road runs through a cross.
      7. The other ten get indignant (10:41). Nobody's seeing clearly.
      8. Teaching: "whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant… For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many" (10:43–45).
      9. **Refrain #2:** James and John saw the glory. They couldn't see the suffering. *You can know who He is and still miss His road.*
   4. Three times He tells them. Three times they miss it. Three times He calls them in and teaches again. That's not a slow class. That's a patient Teacher.

5. Why is Jesus saying these things?
   1. **Because they have the right title with the wrong picture.** They pictured a Messiah who takes a throne. He's a Messiah who takes a cross. Each prediction is Jesus correcting their eyesight, the way He touched the Bethsaida man a second time.
   2. **Because His road becomes our road.** After every prediction comes teaching about following: carry a cross (8:34), be last and servant of all (9:35), serve and give yourself (10:43–45). He isn't just telling them where *He's* going. He's telling them where *following* goes.
   3. **Because the kingdom turns the line around.** Look at who's close to the kingdom in these chapters: a child (10:14–15), the last, the servant, a blind beggar. And look who struggles: the rich man (10:23), and the disciples asking for seats. The ones with nothing to bring receive it. The ones holding on to something walk away.
   4. Relatable picture (optional, 30 seconds): A couple can know exactly who they're marrying on the wedding day and still have no idea what "in sickness and in health" is going to ask of them. Knowing *who* isn't the same as knowing *the road*.

6. Mark 10:13: "the disciples rebuked them." Where have we seen that?
   1. "People were bringing little children to Jesus… but the disciples rebuked them" (10:13).
   2. "When Jesus saw this, he was indignant… 'Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them, for the kingdom of God belongs to such as these'" (10:14).
   3. "Anyone who will not receive the kingdom of God like a little child will never enter it" (10:15). He takes them in His arms and blesses them (10:16).
   4. Now trace the word "rebuke" through the road:
      1. Peter rebukes Jesus for talking about the cross (8:32).
      2. The disciples rebuke the people bringing children (10:13).
      3. The crowd rebukes Bartimaeus (10:48).
      4. Same instinct in 9:38: "we told him to stop." And twice Jesus answers, "Do not stop him" (9:39), "do not hinder them" (10:14).
   5. **The pattern:** people who can't see His road end up standing in the way of people trying to get to Him.
   6. Bartimaeus is like those children. He has nothing to offer. He can't earn anything. All he can do is receive. The children had somebody to carry them to Jesus. The Bethsaida man had somebody to bring him. Bartimaeus had nobody, so he used the only thing he had: his voice.
   7. And right after the children comes the rich man, who has everything to offer and can't receive it.
   8. This brings us back to Bartimaeus.

7. Back to Jericho: Jesus stopped (10:49–52)
   1. "Jesus stopped and said, 'Call him'" (10:49). He's on His way to the cross, and He stops for the man the crowd wanted gone. That's 10:45 walking down the road: He came to serve.
   2. The same crowd that said "Hush" now says, **"Cheer up! On your feet! He's calling you."** *(Mark this. It's the church's application in point 8.)*
   3. "Throwing his cloak aside, he jumped to his feet and came to Jesus" (10:50). The rich man held on to his wealth. Bartimaeus threw aside about all he had. *(Say "aside." Don't say he left it for good.)*
   4. "What do you want me to do for you?" (10:51) – the same question as 10:36.
      1. James and John: "Give us seats." Bartimaeus: "Rabbi, I want to see."
      2. **James and John wanted a seat. Bartimaeus got up out of his.**
   5. "Go," said Jesus, "your faith has healed you." "**Immediately** he received his sight and followed Jesus along the road" (10:52).
      1. Same word, *hodos*. He started beside the road and ends on it.
      2. The rich man was told "Go" and went away. Bartimaeus was told "Go" and went *with Him*. **One goes away. The other goes with Him.**
      3. The Bethsaida man was sent home. Bartimaeus follows Jesus on the road, and the next verse (11:1) is Jerusalem. He's following Him toward the cross.
   6. **Peter saw who Jesus was. Bartimaeus followed Him where He was going.**
   7. **The first thing clear sight did was put him on the road.**

8. Application: make it land
   1. **A word to the church first: Are we the crowd?**
      1. Somebody is always trying to get to Jesus, and they're usually a little loud, a little messy, or a little inconvenient. Kids who don't sit still. The person who looks rough coming in the door. The one who doesn't know when to stand or sit. The person who keeps coming back to the altar about the same thing.
      2. Jesus took the crowd's line from "Hush, sit down" to "Cheer up! On your feet! He's calling you."
      3. This week: be the voice that says "He's calling you" to one person. Name situations from your own congregation, not people.
   2. **Then three questions, asked once each:**
      1. Where are you sitting? Beside the road, or on it? (The worn out, the shut-in, the one who used to be faithful, the one who feels life passing by.)
      2. What do you want from Him? A seat, or sight? (Position, recognition, a comfortable church, versus "Lord, I want to see.")
      3. What are you holding that would make you go away sad?
   3. **And for anybody who's been told to hush** (the diagnosis, the past, a voice that says you used up your chances, even church people): cry out all the more. He still stops.
   4. Ask Him for sight. Not for a seat. Not a better spot beside the road.
   5. **Closing line (protected):** *You can know who He is and still miss His road. Or you can ask Him for sight, get up off the side of it, and follow Him down it.*
   6. "If you'll stand with me."

9. Altar: three invitations (from the stand plan)
   1. Never come, or drifted off the road: "Cheer up! On your feet! He's calling you" (10:49). Sinner's prayer.
   2. You know Him but can't see the road: "Son of David, have mercy on me. Lord, I want to see."
   3. Something in your hands: Bartimaeus threw his cloak aside. *(Say this is application, not something Mark spells out.)*

## Watch your words
- Bethsaida man: healed in stages, then saw "everything clearly" (8:25), then sent home (8:26). Don't make him disobedient. The contrast is Mark's arrangement, so present it as your reading.
- Bartimaeus: "immediately" (10:52). Cloak thrown "aside."
- "Rebuke" in 8:32, 10:13, and 10:48 is the same Greek verb (*epitimaō*), and "do not stop him" (9:39) and "do not hinder them" (10:14) are the same Greek phrase (*mē kōlyete*). Check these in an interlinear before saying "the same Greek word" from the pulpit. Saying "the same word in our English Bibles" for "rebuked" is safe in the NIV.
- *Hodos* in this section, by my count: 8:27, 9:33, 9:34, 10:17, 10:32, 10:46, 10:52 (seven). Worth a quick check before saying "seven."
- The rich man: keep "Jesus looked at him and loved him" (10:21) in the same breath.
