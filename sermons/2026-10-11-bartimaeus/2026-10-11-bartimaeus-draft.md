# **Mark 10:46–52**

# **Right King, Wrong Road**

---

## **Opening: “Shut up, I am busy”**

On the night of April 14, 1912, the Titanic was crossing the North Atlantic. It was the biggest ship in the world, and people said it couldn’t sink. Up in the radio room, a young operator named Jack Phillips was buried in work. Passengers were paying to send messages to family and friends back on shore, and he was tapping them out as fast as he could.

Not far away, another ship, the Californian, had stopped for the night because it was surrounded by ice. Her radio operator reached out to warn the ships nearby, and his signal came through so loud in Titanic’s radio room it nearly hurt Phillips’s ears. And Jack Phillips answered him: “Shut up, shut up, I am busy.” So the other operator listened a little longer, then turned off his radio and went to bed.

About ten minutes later, the Titanic struck an iceberg. In the early hours of the morning, Phillips sent out call after call for help, and the closest ship on the ocean didn’t hear a word. More than fifteen hundred people died that night.

Now, I want to be fair to Jack Phillips. He stayed at his post sending distress calls almost to the very end, and he went down with the ship. He wasn’t a bad man. He was a busy man. He was so busy with what was in front of him that he couldn’t see what was coming, and he told the one voice that could have helped to be quiet.

Church, not every voice that interrupts you is a nuisance. And this morning, on the road out of Jericho, there’s a crowd in a hurry, and a man on the side of the road they’re about to tell to hush.

---

# **1\. Beside the Road**

## **A. Jericho and the Road to Jerusalem**

Jericho is the last stop before Jerusalem, about fifteen miles away and nearly all of it uphill. Jesus is leaving town with His disciples and "a large crowd" (10:46). Everybody’s moving. Everybody’s on the road going somewhere.

## **B. Sitting Beside the Hodos**

And Mark says Bartimaeus was "sitting by the roadside begging." The word for road is hodos: the road, the way. Bartimaeus is beside the hodos. Not on it. Beside it.

Have you ever been there? Life is going right past you. Everybody else is moving, and everybody else has somewhere to be. And you’re sitting on the side of the road with your hand out, hoping somebody notices.

## **C. Recognizing the King**

Then he hears it’s Jesus of Nazareth, and he starts shouting: "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me\!" (10:47).

Notice what he calls Him. Son of David. That’s a king’s title. It’s the first time in the whole Gospel of Mark that anybody calls Jesus that. The man who can’t see anything can see who Jesus is.

---

# **2\. Hush**

## **A. The Crowd’s Attempt to Silence Him**

"Many rebuked him and told him to be quiet" (10:48).

Maybe they were in a hurry. It’s a crowd on the way to Jerusalem, and they don’t want to be held up by a beggar. Maybe they were nervous, because shouting "Son of David" on the road to Jerusalem is king talk, and king talk gets you noticed. Either way, they were standing between that man and Jesus.

Hold on to that word *rebuked*. We’re going to see it again.

## **B. Refusing to Be Silenced**

But I love what he does. He doesn’t quiet down. "He shouted all the more, ‘Son of David, have mercy on me\!’"

Somebody here has been told to hush. Maybe it was a diagnosis. Maybe it’s a voice in your head that says you already used up your chances. Maybe, God help us, it was church people.

Bartimaeus says, I don’t care who’s telling me to hush. He’s passing by, and I’m not letting Him go by without me.

Now hold that cry right there. Mark has been building to this moment for three chapters, and to see why it matters, we have to go back to the first blind man.

---

# **3\. Two Blind Men**

## **A. The First Blind Man at Bethsaida (Mark 8:22–26)**

Bartimaeus isn’t the first blind man in this part of Mark. He’s the second. Mark puts one blind man at the front of these chapters and one at the back, like bookends on a shelf.

Turn back to Mark 8:22. At Bethsaida, "some people brought a blind man and begged Jesus to touch him." Jesus takes him by the hand, leads him out of the village, spits on his eyes, lays hands on him, and asks, "Do you see anything?"

And the man says, "I see people; they look like trees walking around" (8:24).

Jesus didn’t miss. He wasn’t having an off day. "Once more Jesus put his hands on the man’s eyes… and he saw everything clearly" (8:25). Then Jesus sent him home.

## **B. Key Contrasts Between the Two Blind Men**

Look at the differences:

* That man was brought by his friends. Bartimaeus had nobody, so he cried out himself.  
* That man was healed in stages, with two touches. Bartimaeus was healed with a word, immediately.  
* That man was sent home. Bartimaeus followed Jesus down the road.

## **C. The Disciples at "Trees Walking"**

Just four verses before Bethsaida, Jesus asked His disciples, "Do you have eyes but fail to see?" (8:18).

I believe Mark is showing us a picture. Everything between these two blind men is about the disciples, and for three chapters they’re at "trees walking." They can see Jesus. They just can’t see Him clearly yet. This is a message about following Jesus, told in the language of seeing.

---

# **4\. Three Times on the Road**

Mark 8:27: "On the way he asked them, ‘Who do people say I am?’"

On the way: there’s hodos again. Everything in this part of Mark happens on the road, and the road goes to Jerusalem. Three times on that road, Jesus tells them what’s waiting there. And every time it goes the same way: He tells them. They miss it. He teaches them again.

## **A. The First Prediction: Peter’s Rebuke (Mark 8:27–38)**

* **The Declaration:** The first time, Peter gets the title exactly right: "You are the Messiah" (8:29). Praise God, Peter. Gold star.  
* **The Prediction:** Then Jesus says the Son of Man "must suffer many things… and that he must be killed and after three days rise again" (8:31).  
* **The Rebuke:** And "Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him" (8:32). There’s that word again. Peter puts his arm around Jesus: "Lord, can I talk to you a minute? This is not good for morale."  
* **The Teaching:** And Jesus says, "Get behind me, Satan\! You do not have in mind the concerns of God, but merely human concerns" (8:33). Then He teaches them: "Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me" (8:34).  
* **The Lesson:** Peter saw the title. He couldn’t see the cross. You can know who He is and still miss His road.

## **B. The Second Prediction: Arguing Over Greatness (Mark 9:30–37)**

* **The Prediction:** The second time, Jesus tells them, "They will kill him, and after three days he will rise" (9:31).  
* **The Reaction:** And Mark says "they did not understand what he meant and were afraid to ask him about it" (9:32).  
* **The Argument:** Then Jesus asks, "What were you arguing about on the road?" Nobody says a word, "because on the way they had argued about who was the greatest" (9:34).  
* **The Teaching:** So Jesus sits down, calls the Twelve, and teaches them: "Anyone who wants to be first must be the very last, and the servant of all" (9:35). He tells them. They miss it. He teaches them again.

## **C. Interlude: The Rich Young Ruler (Mark 10:17–22)**

Then a man runs up and falls on his knees: "Good teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?" (10:17). He’s kept the commandments since he was a boy. I believe he’s walking a road he built himself, out of what he can do.

And Mark says, "Jesus looked at him and loved him." Then Jesus tells him, "Go, sell everything you have… Then come, follow me."

And "he went away sad, because he had great wealth" (10:22). Remember that word *go*. We’re going to hear it again.

## **D. The Third Prediction: James and John’s Request (Mark 10:32–45)**

* **The Prediction:** The third time, they’re on the road up to Jerusalem, "with Jesus leading the way" (10:32). He tells them everything: condemned, handed over, mocked, spit on, flogged, killed, and three days later He’ll rise (10:33–34).  
* **The Request:** And the very next thing out of anybody’s mouth is James and John: "Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we ask." And Jesus asks them, "What do you want me to do for you?" (10:36). Remember that question. "Let one of us sit at your right and the other at your left in your glory."  
* **The Reality:** Jesus says, "You don’t know what you are asking." Turn over to Mark 15:27: "They crucified two rebels with him, one on his right and one on his left." That’s who ends up at His right and His left. The Son of David’s road runs through a cross.  
* **The Teaching:** Then the other ten get mad, not because James and John got it wrong, but because they asked first. So Jesus calls them in one more time: "For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many" (10:45).  
* **The Lesson:** James and John saw the glory. They couldn’t see the suffering. You can know who He is and still miss His road. Three times He tells them. That’s not a slow class. That’s a patient Teacher. He kept telling them because they had the right King and the wrong picture of His road.

---

# **5\. Rebuked**

## **A. Standing in the Way of Others**

Now back up to 10:13. "People were bringing little children to Jesus… but the disciples rebuked them."

There’s that word again.

* Peter rebuked Jesus for talking about the cross.  
* The disciples rebuked the people bringing children.  
* And the crowd rebuked Bartimaeus.

When you can’t see His road, you’ll end up standing in someone else’s way.

## **B. The Kingdom Belongs to the Helpless**

And Jesus was indignant. "Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them, for the kingdom of God belongs to such as these… anyone who will not receive the kingdom of God like a little child will never enter it" (10:14–15).

Those children had somebody to carry them to Jesus. The Bethsaida man had friends to bring him. Bartimaeus had nobody. All he had was his voice. He’s like those children: nothing to offer, nothing to earn, just hands open to receive. And the rich man had everything to offer and couldn’t receive a thing.

That brings us back to Jericho.

---

# **6\. Jesus Stopped**

## **A. The Call of Jesus**

"Jesus stopped and said, ‘Call him’" (10:49).

He’s on His way to the cross, and He stops for the man the crowd wanted gone. That’s 10:45 walking down the road: He didn’t come to be served, but to serve.

And listen to the crowd now: "Cheer up\! On your feet\! He’s calling you." It’s the same crowd. They went from "Hush" to "He’s calling you."

## **B. Casting Aside the Cloak**

"Throwing his cloak aside, he jumped to his feet and came to Jesus" (10:50).

For a blind beggar, that cloak may have been about everything he owned. He threw it aside to get to Jesus.

## **C. The Question: Sight vs. Status**

Then Jesus asks him, "What do you want me to do for you?" (10:51).

It’s the same question He asked James and John. They said, "Give us seats." Bartimaeus said, "Rabbi, I want to see." James and John wanted a seat. Bartimaeus got up out of his.

## **D. Going Away vs. Following**

"Go," said Jesus, "your faith has healed you."

It’s the same word the rich man heard: *go*. But "immediately he received his sight and followed Jesus along the road" (10:52). That’s hodos, the same road. He started this story beside the road, and he ends it on the road.

The rich man heard "go" and went away. Bartimaeus heard "go" and went with Him. One goes away. The other goes with Him.

And look where the road goes. The very next verse, 11:1, they’re coming into Jerusalem. Bartimaeus doesn’t follow Jesus to a throne. He follows Him toward Calvary. Peter saw who Jesus was. Bartimaeus followed Him where He was going. The first thing clear sight did was put him on the road.

---

# **7\. Landing & Application**

## **A. Moving Past Blurry Sight**

We’ve seen two blind men this morning. One saw in stages, and one saw immediately. Here’s what I believe Mark wants us to see.

The man at Bethsaida is a picture of where the disciples were: trees walking. And church, that’s what the journey can look like for us. We see Him. We know He’s the Anointed One, the Messiah. We’ll sing it, shout it, and testify to it. But when suffering comes, it’s a different story.

We stop praying, not because we don’t believe He’ll answer, but because we’re afraid of what He’ll say. We serve, but it isn’t serving. It’s a means to an end: a position, a seat, somebody noticing. That’s not ministry. That’s pragmatism. We see Jesus. We just can’t see His road. Trees walking.

## **B. Receiving the Second Touch**

But when we look past the naysayers, when we quit letting the crowd tell us to hush, when we cry out in desperation, "Son of David, have mercy on me\!", He stops. Our eyes are opened. The beggar’s cloak gets thrown aside. And we get up and follow Him down the road.

If you’re at trees walking this morning, that isn’t failure. Jesus put His hands on that man again. He told His disciples about the cross three times. He hasn’t run out of second touches. But don’t settle for blurry, and don’t stop at seeing. The point of sight is to see where He’s going and follow Him there.

## **C. Becoming an Encouraging Community**

And church, one more thing. Somebody is always trying to get to Jesus, and they’re usually a little loud, a little messy, a little inconvenient. Let’s not be the crowd that says "Hush." Let’s be the crowd that says, "Cheer up\! On your feet\! He’s calling you."

## **D. Three Final Questions**

So let me ask you three questions, and I’ll only ask them once:

1. **Where are you this morning:** trees walking, beside the road, or on it?  
2. **What do you want from Him:** a seat, or sight?  
3. **What are you holding** that would make you go away sad?

Ask Him for sight. Not for a seat. Not for a better spot beside the road. "Rabbi, I want to see." You can know who He is and still miss His road. Or you can ask Him for sight, get up off the side of it, and follow Him down it.

# **8\. Altar Call & Closing Prayer**

## **A. Altar Invitation**

This altar is open. If you’ve never come to Jesus, or you’ve drifted off the road, hear what the crowd told Bartimaeus: "Cheer up\! On your feet\! He’s calling you." He stopped for a blind beggar, and He’ll stop for you.*Lord, I have sinned. I need forgiveness. I’m asking you to save me, and I put my trust in you.*

If you know Him, but things are blurry right now, if you’re at trees walking, come ask for the second touch.*"Son of David, have mercy on me. Lord, I want to see."*

If there’s something in your hands that would make you go away sad: Bartimaeus threw his cloak aside to get to Jesus. Come lay down whatever you’ve been holding on to. Come now.

## **B. Closing Prayer**

Lord, thank you that you stop for people on the side of the road. Open our eyes. Don’t let us just know who you are. Let us see where you’re going and follow you there. Whatever we’re holding, help us throw it aside. Whatever voice has told us to hush, let us cry out louder. And when you ask, "What do you want me to do for you?", let our answer be, "Lord, I want to see."In Jesus’ name. Amen.
