## [~3:00] 1. Trees walking

I told you I'd show you why we jumped ahead, and here it is. Bartimaeus isn't the first blind man in this part of Mark. He's the second. Mark puts one blind man at the front of these chapters and one at the back, like bookends on a shelf.

Here's the thing. This is a message about following Jesus. But Mark tells it in the language of seeing. All through these chapters, the question isn't whether people can see Jesus. It's whether they can see where He's going.

Go back with me to Mark 8:22. Some people bring a blind man to Jesus at Bethsaida. Jesus takes him by the hand, leads him out of the village, spits on his eyes, lays His hands on him, and asks, "Do you see anything?" (8:23). And the man says, "I see people; they look like trees walking around" (8:24).

I love this part right here. Jesus didn't miss. Jesus is not having an off day. He puts His hands on the man's eyes one more time, and Mark says "his eyes were opened, his sight was restored, and he saw everything clearly" (8:25). Then Jesus sends him home (8:26).

Now think about this. Just four verses before that, Jesus asked His own disciples, "Do you have eyes but fail to see?" (8:18). Then Mark tells us about a man who sees in stages. I believe Mark is showing us something on purpose. The disciples are about to spend three chapters at "trees walking." They can see Jesus. They just can't see Him clearly yet.

Hold on to that picture of trees walking. We're about to watch twelve men see Jesus that way all the way to Jericho.

## [4:00] 2. Right title, wrong road · HARD CAP: if you pass 11:00, jump to movement 3

Mark 8:27: "Jesus and his disciples went on to the villages around Caesarea Philippi. On the way he asked them, 'Who do people say I am?'"

Notice "on the way." The Greek word is *hodos*. It means the way, the road. Mark uses it seven times between here and the end of chapter 10. Everything in this part of Mark happens on the road, and the road is going to Jerusalem.

So on the road, Jesus asks, "Who do you say I am?" And Peter gets it right: "You are the Messiah" (8:29). Praise God, Peter. Gold star.

Then verse 31. Jesus "began to teach them that the Son of Man must suffer many things and be rejected... and that he must be killed and after three days rise again." And Mark says "Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him" (8:32). I can just see it. Peter puts his arm around Jesus. "Lord, can I talk to you a minute? This is not good for morale." And Jesus turns, looks at the rest of the disciples, and says, "Get behind me, Satan! You do not have in mind the concerns of God, but merely human concerns" (8:33).

Peter saw the title. He couldn't see the cross. He knew who Jesus was. He just didn't want the road Jesus was on.

**You can know who He is and still miss His road.**

And church, it doesn't happen once. Mark shows us the same pattern three times.

- **First time:** Jesus tells them He'll die (8:31). Peter rebukes Him (8:32). Jesus calls the crowd together and teaches: "Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me" (8:34).
- **Second time:** Jesus tells them again (9:31). Mark says "they did not understand what he meant and were afraid to ask him about it" (9:32). Then they get to Capernaum, and Jesus asks what they were arguing about on the road. Nobody says a word, "because on the way they had argued about who was the greatest" (9:34). Jesus sits down, calls the Twelve, and says, "Anyone who wants to be first must be the very last, and the servant of all" (9:35).
- **Third time:** Jesus tells them in more detail than ever: condemned, handed over, mocked, spit on, flogged, killed, and three days later He'll rise (10:33–34). And the very next thing that happens is James and John asking for the best seats (10:37). We'll come back to that. Jesus calls them together again (10:42).

Three times He tells them where the road goes. Three times they miss it. And three times He calls them in and teaches them again. They can see Jesus. What they can't see is the road.

That's not a slow class. That's a patient Teacher. I'm preaching to myself here. Some of us have needed God to tell us the same thing three times, and I'm glad He didn't quit on us after the first one.

## [11:00] 3. Beside the road

Now come to Jericho. Jericho is the last stop before Jerusalem, about fifteen miles away and nearly all of it uphill. Jesus is leaving town with His disciples and "a large crowd" (10:46). Everybody's moving. Everybody's on the road going somewhere.

And Mark says Bartimaeus was "sitting by the roadside begging" (10:46). Beside the *hodos*. Not on the road. Beside it.

Have you ever been there? Life is going past you. Everybody else is moving. Everybody else has somewhere to be. And you're sitting on the side of the road, hand out, hoping somebody notices.

Then he hears it's Jesus of Nazareth, and he starts shouting: "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!" (10:47).

Notice what he calls Him. "Son of David." That's a king's title. It's the first time in the whole Gospel of Mark that anybody calls Jesus that. The man who can't see anything can see who Jesus is.

"Many rebuked him and told him to be quiet" (10:48). Hush. Sit down. You're embarrassing everybody. Jesus has important things to do.

[Optional: That's the same word Mark used back in 10:13, when the disciples rebuked the people bringing their children to Jesus. People keep trying to keep folks away from Jesus, and Jesus keeps making room.]

"But he shouted all the more, 'Son of David, have mercy on me!'"

Somebody here has been told to be quiet. Maybe it was the diagnosis. Maybe it was a voice in your head that says you already used up your chances. Maybe it was church people. Bartimaeus says, I don't care who's telling me to hush. He's passing by, and I'm not going to let Him go by without me.

And watch this: "Jesus stopped" (10:49). He's on His way to Jerusalem, on the most important walk in history, and He stops for a blind beggar on the side of the road. He says, "Call him." And the same crowd that was telling him to hush now says, "Cheer up! On your feet! He's calling you."

"Throwing his cloak aside, he jumped to his feet and came to Jesus" (10:50). For a blind beggar, that cloak may have been about everything he owned. And he throws it aside to get to Jesus.

## [16:00] 4. "What do you want?" · THE HINGE

Jesus asks him a question: "What do you want me to do for you?" (10:51).

Church, we've heard that question before. Back up to verse 35. James and John come to Jesus and say, "Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we ask." That's like your kid saying, "Daddy, say yes, and then I'll tell you what it is."

And Jesus asks them, "What do you want me to do for you?" (10:36). It's the same question He asks Bartimaeus.

They say, "Let one of us sit at your right and the other at your left in your glory" (10:37).

Now let's be fair to James and John. They know who He is. They've walked with Him the whole way. They've seen demons go and the storm go quiet. Both of them were on the mountain last week when His clothes turned whiter than anyone could bleach them (9:3). They believe He's the King and that He's coming into His glory. They're right about that.

But Jesus says, "You don't know what you are asking" (10:38).

Now look at this. Mark lets us see that right hand and left hand one more time. Turn over to Mark 15:27: "They crucified two rebels with him, one on his right and one on his left." James and John asked for His right and His left in glory. Mark shows us who actually ended up at His right and His left: two men on crosses. That's His road. The Son of David's road runs through a cross.

James and John saw the glory. They couldn't see the suffering.

**You can know who He is and still miss His road.**

So Jesus calls them together one more time and gives us the center of this whole section. Mark 10:45: "For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."

Then they walk into Jericho, and Jesus asks a blind man the same question. "What do you want me to do for you?"

James and John said, "Give us seats." Bartimaeus said, "Rabbi, I want to see."

The men who had walked with Him the whole way asked for the wrong thing. The blind man on the side of the road asked for the right one.

**James and John wanted a seat. Bartimaeus got up out of his.**

## [22:00] 5. Two men hear "Go"

There's one more man in this chapter, and Mark wants us to put him right next to Bartimaeus.

Mark 10:17. A man runs up to Jesus, falls on his knees, and says, "Good teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?" Jesus pushes back right away: "Why do you call me good? No one is good, except God alone" (10:18). Jesus won't even let that word slide.

Now this man can see a road. He's been walking it since he was a boy. Jesus lists the commandments, and he says, "Teacher, all these I have kept since I was a boy" (10:20). But listen to how he asked the question: "What must I do?" I believe it's a road he built himself, out of what he can do and what he has kept.

But I love verse 21. "Jesus looked at him and loved him." Don't miss that. Jesus isn't against this man. He loves him. And then He spells out exactly what His road costs: "One thing you lack. Go, sell everything you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me."

"At this the man's face fell. He went away sad, because he had great wealth" (10:22). I believe he saw Jesus' road clearly, and he walked away from it.

Now listen to Bartimaeus. Jesus tells him, "Go, your faith has healed you" (10:52). It's the same word Jesus gave the rich man: go.

But watch what happens. "Immediately he received his sight and followed Jesus along the road."

One is invited to follow, and he leaves. The other is told to go, and he follows. **One goes away. The other goes with Him.**

The rich man held on to everything he had and walked away. Bartimaeus threw aside the little he had and came running. And notice how the rich man walks off: sad. Walking away from Jesus never made anybody happy.

Bartimaeus started this story beside the road, and he ends it on the road. Same word, *hodos*. Same road the disciples have been walking for three chapters. And look where it goes. The very next verse, 11:1, they're coming into Jerusalem. That's the road Jesus said would end in a cross (10:33–34). Bartimaeus doesn't follow Jesus to a throne. He follows Him toward Calvary.

**Peter saw who Jesus was. Bartimaeus followed Him where He was going.**

Bartimaeus saw clearly. **The first thing clear sight did was put him on the road.**

## [27:00] 6. Ask Him for sight · LANDING AND ALTAR. Protect this time.

So let me ask you three questions this morning, and I'll only ask them once.

Where are you sitting? Beside the road, or on it?

What do you want from Him?

And what are you holding that would make you go away sad?

Here's what I want you to do. Ask Him for sight. Not for a seat. Not for a better spot beside the road. Ask Him for what Bartimaeus asked for: "Rabbi, I want to see." Because when He opens your eyes, you won't just see who He is. You'll see His road, and you'll get up and follow Him on it.

**PROTECTED: You can know who He is and still miss His road. Or you can ask Him for sight, get up off the side of it, and follow Him down it.**

If you'll stand with me. **[KYLE: musician's name]**, if you want to come and play.

This altar is open. I want to pray for you.

**If you've never come to Jesus, or you've drifted off the road,** hear what the crowd told Bartimaeus: "Cheer up! On your feet! He's calling you." He stopped for a blind beggar, and He'll stop for you. You can pray this right where you are: *Lord, I have sinned. I need forgiveness. I'm asking you to save me, and I put my trust in you.* It doesn't have to sound impressive. It just has to come from the heart.

**If you know Him, but you can't see the road right now,** if you've been sitting beside it, worn out, told to be quiet, watching everybody else go by, come pray what Bartimaeus prayed. "Son of David, have mercy on me. Lord, I want to see." He's still stopping on the road.

**If there's something in your hands that would make you go away sad,** this is application, not something Mark spells out: Bartimaeus threw his cloak aside to get to Jesus. Come lay down whatever you've been holding on to.

Come now.

*Closing prayer:* Lord, I thank you that you stop for people on the side of the road. Open our eyes. Don't let us just know who you are. Let us see where you're going, and follow you there. Whatever we're holding, help us throw it aside. Whatever voice has told us to be quiet, let us cry out louder. And when you ask, "What do you want me to do for you?", let our answer be, "Lord, I want to see." In Jesus' name. Amen.

God bless you this morning. It's been good to be in God's house. Shake some hands. Hug some necks.

## Check before the pulpit

- All body quotes are NIV; the reading is KJV (10:46–52).
- Don't give the healing in stages to Bartimaeus. The Bethsaida man saw clearly in the end (8:25) and was sent home (8:26). He didn't follow.
- Bartimaeus was healed "immediately" (10:52), not "suddenly." He threw his cloak aside; don't say he left it for good.
- "Son of David" first appears in Mark at 10:47–48. The only other place in Mark is 12:35–37. Checked.
- The 10:37 / 15:27 link (right and left) is Mark's design, and commentators point it out widely. The 10:13 "rebuked" link is optional.
- Said as inferences: the disciples as the "trees walking" stage, the rich man's "road he built himself," and Bartimaeus as Mark's picture of discipleship.
- The rich man: Mark says Jesus "loved him" (10:21). If you call his road "self-righteousness" from the pulpit, say it as your reading, and keep 10:21 in the same breath so it lands as grief, not scorn.
- "Go" in 10:21 and 10:52 is Mark's everyday word for "go." Use it as an echo Mark sets up between two scenes, not as a hidden meaning.
