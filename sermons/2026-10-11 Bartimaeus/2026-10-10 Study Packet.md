## 1. The shape Kyle asked for

```
 Bartimaeus beside the road (10:46-51) ── freeze on "What do you want?"
        │
        ▼  rewind
 Bethsaida: "men as trees, walking" (8:22-26)
        │
        ▼  the road between
 Prediction 1 → Peter rebukes → "Get behind me" (8:27-38)
 Prediction 2 → argue who is greatest "on the road" (9:30-37)
 Rich man hears "Go" and leaves (10:17-31)
 Prediction 3 → "What do you want?" → thrones (10:32-45)
        │
        ▼  back to the roadside
 Bartimaeus sees "immediately" and follows "on the road" (10:51-52)
```

Why this order works: the listener meets Bartimaeus first, so the whole middle of the sermon is the answer to a question the congregation is already holding. "How did this blind beggar end up seeing what the twelve couldn't?" The question Jesus asks him in 10:51 is the hinge: it is the same question he asked James and John fifteen verses earlier (10:36) [T]. Freezing the scene on that question, then rewinding, sets up the payoff when the sermon comes back.

**Mark 9:2-29 was preached Oct 4.** When the walk reaches chapter 9, nod to last week ("you remember the mountain and the boy") and keep moving.

## 3. Key Greek terms, in plain English

1. ***para tēn hodon* → *en tē hodō*: "beside the road" → "on the road" (10:46, 52).** Bartimaeus starts the story sitting beside the road and ends it following on the road [T]. *Hodos* ("road, way") appears seven times from 8:27 to 10:52: 8:27; 9:33, 34; 10:17, 32, 46, 52 [T]. Mark's whole middle section happens "on the road" to Jerusalem. Reading the road as a picture of discipleship is a reading, not a word Mark defines [I], but the early church called itself "the Way" (Acts 9:2, *hodos*) [T].

2. ***anablepō*: "to see again, to look up" (8:24; 10:51, 52).** In Mark this is the sight verb of both blind healings [T]. Elsewhere it only means "looked up": Jesus looking up to heaven (6:41; 7:34) and the women looking up at the stone (16:4) [T]. The Bethsaida man "looked up" and saw men like trees (8:24). Bartimaeus asks "that I might receive my sight," *hina anablepsō* (10:51), and "immediately he received his sight," *aneblepsen* (10:52). Same word, two endings.

3. ***tēlaugōs*: "clearly" (8:25).** Only here in the New Testament [C]. It means shining far off, seeing at a distance. The Bethsaida man ends up seeing everything clearly [T]. Don't say his sight stayed blurry; the ruled-out list is right about that.

4. ***thelō*: "want" (10:36, 51).** "What do you want me to do for you?" Jesus asks James and John, *ti thelete poiēsō hymin* (10:36), and Bartimaeus, *ti soi theleis poiēsō* (10:51) [T]. Same question, same verb, fifteen verses apart. James and John want thrones. Bartimaeus wants to see. The verb also opens the road: "Whoever wants (*thelei*) to be my disciple" (8:34) [T].

5. ***hypage*: "Go" (8:33; 10:21; 10:52).** This is a three-step thread; the prep note had two of them [T].
   - To Peter: *Hypage opisō mou, Satana*, "Get behind me, Satan" (8:33). Literally, "Go behind me." Get back in follower position.
   - To the rich man: *hypage*, "go, sell whatever thou hast" (10:21). He goes, but away (10:22).
   - To Bartimaeus: *Hypage*, "Go thy way" (10:52). He is free to go anywhere. He goes after Jesus.

6. ***ēkolouthei*: "was following" (9:38; 10:52).** The same Greek form, imperfect tense, appears in both verses [T]. In 9:38 John complains about an exorcist "because he was not following us." In 10:52 Bartimaeus "was following him." The disciples worried about who was following *them*. The beggar followed *Jesus*. (Follow, *akoloutheō*, appears six times in this section: 8:34; 9:38; 10:21, 28, 32, 52.)

7. ***Huie Dauid*: "Son of David" (10:47, 48).** The first time anyone in Mark calls Jesus by this title [T]. Peter got the right name in 8:29 ("Thou art the Christ"). A blind beggar shouts the royal name on the road to the royal city, right before the crowds shout about "the kingdom of our father David" (11:10) [T].

8. ***epitimaō*: "rebuke, charge to be quiet" (10:48).** "Many charged him that he should hold his peace" uses the same verb as Peter rebuking Jesus (8:32) and Jesus rebuking Peter (8:33) [T]. It shows up six times in the section (8:30, 32, 33; 9:25; 10:13, 48), so don't call it unique [T].

9. ***sesōken*: "hath made thee whole," literally "has saved you" (10:52).** The verb *sōzō* covers healing and salvation together [C]. Same words to the bleeding woman, "thy faith hath made thee whole" (5:34) [T].

10. ***apobalōn to himation*: "casting away his garment" (10:50).** He throws off his cloak. Mark doesn't say he left it for good, so don't preach that he never went back for it (ruled-out list). The contrast with the rich man, who "had great possessions" (10:22), is a reading [I].

## 4. Historical background

**Jericho to Jerusalem.** The road climbs from Jericho, far below sea level near the Jordan, up through the Judean wilderness to Jerusalem, roughly 15 miles and more than 3,000 feet of climb [C; V exact figures before giving numbers]. This is the road of the Good Samaritan parable (Luke 10:30) [T]. At Passover it was crowded with pilgrims, which fits the "great number of people" (10:46) [I].

**A beggar's cloak.** The Law treats a poor man's cloak as his covering by night: if it is taken in pledge, it must go back before sundown (Exod 22:26-27) [T]. For a blind beggar it may have been bedding, coat and collection cloth all at once [I; V before saying beggars spread cloaks for coins]. Throwing it off means getting up without the one thing he owns.

**Son of David as a healer.** In Jewish tradition, Solomon, the son of David, was remembered as a healer and an exorcist. Josephus tells of a man named Eleazar driving out a demon with a ring and incantations credited to Solomon, in front of Vespasian (*Antiquities* 8.45-49) [C; V wording]. Some scholars argue this is why a blind man would cry to the "Son of David" for healing [C; V before naming who argues it, often credited to Dennis Duling]. *Psalms of Solomon* 17 shows the other expectation: a Son of David who would "shatter unrighteous rulers" and purge Jerusalem of Gentiles [C; V wording]. Both ideas hang in the air when Bartimaeus shouts. Jesus is going to Jerusalem as the Son of David, but to give his life, not to take a throne (10:45). That fits Kyle's Divine Council frame: the true King walks into the enemy's city on the enemy's terms and wins by dying [I].

**Bethsaida and Caesarea Philippi.** Bethsaida was a fishing town on the north shore of Galilee and the hometown of Peter, Andrew and Philip (John 1:44) [T]. Jesus takes the blind man "out of the town" before healing him and tells him not to go back into it (8:23, 26) [T]. The next scene moves north to Caesarea Philippi (8:27), a city with a shrine to the Greek god Pan at a cave at the foot of Mount Hermon [C]. Heiser reads this region as contested spiritual ground (*The Unseen Realm*, Lexham, 2015) [C on Heiser's view; V chapter]. Mark doesn't comment on the location, so treat it as background in a sentence, not a point of the sermon [I].

## 5. What Mark is doing (the design)

**Two blind men bracket the road [C].** Mark 8:22-10:52 opens and closes with blind men healed, and those are the only two blind healings in Mark [T]. Between them, Jesus predicts his death three times, the disciples misunderstand three times, and Jesus teaches three times about following him [C]:

| | Prediction | Misunderstanding | Teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8:31 "must suffer... be killed... rise again" | 8:32 Peter rebukes him | 8:34-38 deny yourself, take up your cross |
| 2 | 9:31 "delivered into the hands of men" | 9:32-34 they don't understand; argue who is greatest | 9:35-37 last of all, servant of all; the child |
| 3 | 10:33-34 condemned, mocked, spat on, scourged, killed | 10:35-41 James and John ask for thrones | 10:42-45 not so among you; ransom for many |

**The Bethsaida man pictures the disciples [C, widely held; still preach it as a reading].** He sees, but not clearly, and needs a second touch. Right after, Peter sees who Jesus is ("the Christ," 8:29) but not what the Christ must do (8:32). Mark ties sight to understanding in so many words just before Bethsaida: "Having eyes, see ye not?" (8:18) [T].

**Watch the ruled-out list here.** The Bethsaida man saw everything clearly after the second touch (8:25) and was sent home (8:26). Bartimaeus has no stages. He sees "immediately" (10:52) [T].

**Who follows, and who goes away [T on the words; I on the contrast].**
- The rich man runs up, kneels, calls Jesus "Good Master," hears "Go... and come, follow me," and goes away sad (10:17-22).
- Bartimaeus is called, jumps up, calls Jesus "Rabboni," hears "Go," and follows (10:49-52).

The rich man had everything and kept it. The blind man had a cloak and threw it off.

**10:45 at the center.** "For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many" (KJV) [T]. This is what Peter couldn't see at Caesarea Philippi. It is the last thing Jesus says before Jericho.

## 6. Main idea

**Kyle's open choice from the Oct 9 note** (still open):
- **A.** "You can know His name and still miss His road."
- **B.** "Real sight gets up off the roadside and follows Jesus on His road."
- **C.** "Peter saw who Jesus was. Bartimaeus saw where Jesus was going, and went with Him."

**How each fits the new order:**
- **A** suits a sermon that spends most of its time with the disciples on the road. It works as the refrain after each prediction.
- **B** suits opening and closing on Bartimaeus. It's the strongest altar line.
- **C** names the contrast plainly and works best as the big-idea turn near the end.

**Recommendation:** use C once as the turn ("What I'm saying this morning is...") and B as the refrain and the altar line.

**Contrast that frames the sermon:** beside the road or on the road.

**Title options** (from the Oct 9 note): "Beside the Road" / "What Do You Want?" / "On the Road." Opening on Bartimaeus favors **"Beside the Road,"** since that's where the church first sees him and where some of them are sitting.

## 7. Proposed outline

About 40 minutes. At Kyle's measured pace (about 110 manuscript words per minute), that is **about 4,400 written words**. Minute marks are starting points.

**Opening (0:00-3:00).** Series placement: "Last week we were on the mountain and at the foot of it with a boy and his daddy (Mark 9). This morning we jump to the end of the road, then walk it backward." Reading: Mark 10:46-52 (KJV). Title. Prayer. "You can be seated."

**1. Beside the Road (3:00-9:00, about 650 words). 10:46-51a.**
- Jericho, the crowd, the climb to Jerusalem. Bartimaeus, son of Timaeus, sitting *para tēn hodon*.
- He hears it's Jesus of Nazareth and shouts a name no one in Mark has used yet: Son of David.
- "Many charged him that he should hold his peace." He cried "the more a great deal."
- Jesus stood still. "Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee." The cloak.
- Freeze the scene: "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" "Hold that question. We've heard it before. Let's go back and find out where."
- Optional story slot (under 2 minutes), per the Oct 9 note.

**2. Trees Walking (9:00-14:00, about 550 words). 8:22-26.**
- Rewind to Bethsaida. They bring him; Jesus takes him by the hand and leads him out of town.
- Spit, hands, "Do you see anything?" "I see men as trees, walking."
- Second touch: he "saw every man clearly" (*tēlaugōs*). Sent home.
- Plain statement: this is the only healing in Mark that comes in two touches. Mark is getting us ready for what happens next.

**3. Right Name, Wrong Road (14:00-20:00, about 650 words). 8:27-38.**
- "On the road" (8:27), the first *hodos*. "Whom say ye that I am?" "Thou art the Christ."
- Prediction one. Peter rebukes Jesus. Jesus rebukes Peter: *Hypage opisō mou*, "Get behind me," back in line.
- Peter has the right name and the wrong road. Like the Bethsaida man: he can see, just not clearly yet.
- Refrain.

**4. Arguing on the Road (20:00-24:00, about 450 words). 9:30-38.**
- Nod to last week (the mountain and the boy) and move on.
- Prediction two. "They understood not." And on the road they argued about who was greatest (9:34).
- The child in his arms. Then John: "he was not following us" (9:38), the same Greek form Mark will use for Bartimaeus.

**5. Two Men Hear "Go" (24:00-30:00, about 650 words). 10:17-31, 32-45.**
- The rich man: runs, kneels, "Good Master." Jesus "beholding him loved him." *Hypage*. He goes away grieved.
- Prediction three, the most detailed: mocked, scourged, spat on, killed.
- James and John: "Master, we would that thou shouldest do for us whatsoever we shall desire." Jesus: "What would ye that I should do for you?" Here is the question from the roadside.
- They ask for thrones, one on the right and one on the left. (Forward look: 15:27, two men crucified on his right and left. One line, not a detour.)
- 10:45 at the center. This is what the road is for.

**6. On the Road (30:00-36:00, about 650 words). Back to 10:51-52.**
- Unfreeze the scene. Same question, different answer: "Lord, that I might receive my sight."
- *Hypage*: go wherever you want. And "immediately he received his sight, and followed Jesus in the way."
- Same Greek form as 9:38. The disciples were worried about who was following them. The beggar followed Jesus.
- The turn: "What I'm saying this morning is..." (big idea C).
- Where is that road going? Jerusalem, a cross, and 10:45. He follows anyway.

**Close and altar (36:00-41:00, about 550 words).** "If you'll stand with me, I'm closing."
- Return to the roadside and the frozen question: "What do you want Him to do for you?"
- Altar groups: (1) the one sitting beside the road, near Jesus every Sunday but not following; (2) the one being told to hush, who needs to cry out "the more a great deal"; (3) the one holding a cloak, the thing that has been your security, ready to throw it off.
- Refrain (B). Closing prayer reusing the road, the cloak and the question.

**Protected lines for the ending:** the frozen question returning, the refrain, and the three altar groups. If time runs long, cut from movement 4, not from the ending.

**Local road for the rhetorical question:** the Oct 9 note's placeholder is Highway 75. Kyle supplies the real road.

## 8. Illustration options

All options. One story at most, in the optional slot.

1. **Learning to see (fits movement 2 only).** In 1991, Shirl Jennings, a man from Oklahoma who had been nearly blind since early childhood, had cataract surgery at about 50. His eyes worked afterward, but his brain could not make sense of what he saw. He could not tell a cat from a dog without touching it. Oliver Sacks wrote about him under the name "Virgil" in "To See and Not See," *The New Yorker*, May 10, 1993 [V: all details before preaching]. **Use:** Bethsaida only, to show that seeing and understanding are different. Do **not** use it for Bartimaeus. The ruled-out list says Bartimaeus had no stages.
2. **The roadside spot (fits movement 1).** Everyone in Fort Payne knows somebody who has worked the same spot for years: a corner, an exit ramp, a bench. Kyle supplies one real place.
3. **Kyle's own story (fits the close).** A time when he was close to the things of God but sitting beside the road. Only if it's his.

## 9. Application

1. **Knowing His name is not the same as following His road.** Peter had the right answer in 8:29 and argued against the cross in 8:32.
2. **Cry louder when you're told to hush.** The crowd rebuked Bartimaeus with the same word the disciples used on parents bringing their children (10:13). Some of the people telling you to be quiet are religious people.
3. **Jesus asks what you want.** The question shows what's in the heart. James and John wanted position. Bartimaeus wanted sight.
4. **Throw off the cloak.** Name what has been your security, the thing you'd have to drop to get up. Don't say he lost it forever; say he didn't let it slow him down.
5. **Free to go anywhere, he follows.** Jesus said "Go." He went after Jesus, toward Jerusalem, toward a cross.
6. **Pentecostal angle.** The early church called itself "the Way" (Acts 9:2), and the same Spirit who opened eyes empowers people to walk the road (Acts 1:8). Keep it to a sentence or two; Mark's text should carry the sermon.
