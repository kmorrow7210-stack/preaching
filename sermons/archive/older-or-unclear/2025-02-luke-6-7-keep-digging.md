III. The Parable of the Wise and Foolish Builders (Luke 6:46–49)
A Story Jesus Knew Well
Now, when Jesus talked about building, He wasn't just makin' up a story. He knew construction.
In Mark 6:3, He's called a tekton – that's a Greek word that means more than just
"carpenter." It means "builder," a real craftsman. In first-century Galilee, that meant working
with wood and stone – the very foundation of everything they built.
Imagine Him working alongside Joseph, shaping beams, cutting stone, and – most important –
laying those foundations. He knew the difference between a quick, flimsy job and a structure
that would last.

The Reality of First-Century Construction in Palestine: Digging Deeper Than You
Think
Building a house back then? Not as simple as just plopping down some rocks. If you wanted it to last
through a storm, you had to dig deep. I mean, think about the land—valleys, hills, plateaus, and
the soil! It could go from soft and rich to rock-solid in a heartbeat

That top layer of soil might look solid in the summer sun – almost like concrete. But don't be
fooled! When those heavy winter rains came (and they always came), that seemingly solid
ground turned into a slippery, unstable mud pit.
Now, some builders – we might call them "shortcut builders" – they were always looking for
the easy way out. They'd see a spot that looked good, maybe even a little higher up, and they'd
start building right there.
No backbreaking work digging down to bedrock for them! Their houses went up fast, and they
might have looked impressive at first. Straight walls. Sturdy roofs. But underneath, a disaster
was brewing.
They were building on "sand" – not necessarily beach sand, but unreliable soil that turned to
sludge in the rain. In some places, that bedrock could be 20-30 feet deep down – and those
shortcut builders? They weren't about that life.
But the wise builder? He was different. He understood the land. He knew that real strength
wasn't about what you could see on the surface; it was about what was underneath. He put in
the hard work – digging, digging, and digging some more.
He didn't quit when his back ached or when his neighbors wondered why it was taking so long.
He kept going, sometimes through meters of dirt and loose rock, until he heard that satisfying
thunk when his tools hit solid rock – bedrock!
That's where he anchored his house. In a place like Palestine, with flash floods and shifting
ground, bedrock wasn't a luxury; it was essential. Just like the Temple in Jerusalem, built on a
massive bedrock foundation, these wise builders wanted that same unshakable base for their
homes.
They knew, without a doubt, that when – not if – the storms came down from the hills, when the
rivers overflowed and the ground turned to muck, the difference between a house standing
strong and one collapsing into ruin wouldn't be about fancy walls or a nice roof. It would be
entirely, completely, and utterly about the foundation.
And Jesus, who knew building as well as anyone, declared: "That's what faith is like." Are we
building our lives on what looks good on the surface, or have we dug deep to anchor ourselves
to the unshakable Rock?

III. Transition: A Living Example of a Rock-Solid Faith (Luke 7:1–10)
Now that we've seen how crucial it is to “keep digging” until we reach that unshakable
foundation, Luke's Gospel ain't gonna leave us hangin'! He gives us a real-life illustration of
exactly what that looks like. Jesus doesn’t just give us a concept; He puts flesh on it in the form
of one of the most unlikely figures in all of Israel.
We move from the Parable of the Wise and Foolish Builders straight into Capernaum, where a
Roman centurion steps onto the stage. Think about this:
● He’s not a disciple.
● He’s not a religious leader.
● He’s not even Jewish.
This man is a battle-hardened soldier—part of the army oppressing God’s people, no less!
With power, authority, and wealth at his disposal, he’d be the last person we’d expect to
teach us about rock-solid faith. Yet Luke wants us to see that this centurion exemplifies
everything Jesus just taught. He demonstrates a faith that stands in the storm—so
remarkable that even Jesus Himself is amazed!
So you gotta hear this: in the next section of Scripture, we’ll witness how this centurion’s
steadfast belief becomes a livin', breathin' illustration of what it means to build on the Rock.
Let’s turn our attention to Luke 7:1–10 and discover how a man with every earthly advantage
chooses humility and trust in Jesus—and ends up putting on display the kind of faith that just
might astonish the Lord Himself!

VI. Practical Application: How Do I Build My Life On The Rock?
Alright, you wanna know how to practically build on the Rock? You wanna know how to take this
digging thing and make it real in your life?
Number One: TRUST JESUS’ WORD FULLY! You got it right! You hit it hard! We can’t just
hear the Word, we can’t just read the Word, we gotta believe the Word! No doubt! No hesitation!

No excuses! When God speaks, we obey! Like that Centurion, just a word from Jesus is
enough! We gotta dig deep into that Word, bury our roots in it, plant our faith on it!
Because His Word is truth! His Word is power! His Word is bedrock!
For example, when God's Word tells us to forgive, even when it's hard, we choose to forgive.
That's trusting His Word fully. DIG DEEP INTO HIS WORD!
Number Two: LIVE OUT WHAT YOU PROFESS! Preach it! This ain’t a spectator sport! Faith
ain’t just head knowledge, it’s heart action!
Going to church, saying prayers, singing songs – that’s all good, but if we ain’t doing what He
says, it’s worthless! Like that house with no foundation – looks good on the surface, but it’s a
disaster waiting to happen!
We have to be doers of the Word, not just hearers only! Obedience isn’t optional, it’s essential!
It’s foundational! We can't just sing about loving our neighbors; we have to actually act like we
love them, even the difficult ones. That's living out what we profess. DIG DEEP INTO
OBEDIENCE!
Number Three: APPROACH GOD WITH HUMILITY! Yes! Humility before the Almighty! Not
pride, not arrogance, not thinking we deserve anything! That Centurion, he got it! He knew his
place before Jesus!
We have to come to God with empty hands, broken spirits, recognizing our need for Him! Not
demanding, but begging! Not entitled, but grateful! Humility opens the door to God’s grace!
Pride slams it shut!
The Pharisee in the temple focused on his own righteousness; the tax collector knew his need
for mercy. Which approach do we take? DIG DEEP INTO HUMILITY!
Number Four: RECOGNIZE JESUS’ ULTIMATE AUTHORITY! Jesus isn’t just a good teacher,
He’s not just a nice guy – He is LORD! He is KING!
He has ALL authority! And we gotta surrender to it! Not just in words, but in our whole lives!
Time, money, relationships, decisions, future – EVERYTHING under His Lordship! No holding
back! No half-measures! Total surrender!
That’s building on the Rock! Are there areas of your life where you're still holding back? Are you
truly surrendering everything to His Lordship? DIG DEEP INTO SURRENDER TO JESUS AS
LORD!
These four practical steps, they’re the shovels and picks we need to start digging! It ain't easy
work, it takes effort, it takes time, it takes determination. But the reward is worth it!

A life built on the Rock! A life that will stand strong when the storms come! So let’s get to work,
church! Let’s roll up our sleeves and KEEP DIGGING! KEEP DIGGING! KEEP DIGGING! Until
we hit that bedrock foundation of Jesus Christ! AMEN!
Conclusion Hook: The Leaning Tower of Pisa
As we draw to a close, let me leave you with one final image of why our foundation matters.
Think about the Leaning Tower of Pisa—begun in 1173 as a proud bell tower with grand
ambitions. Construction started with such excitement, but by the time workers reached the third
story, something alarming happened: the tower began to tilt.
Work stalled for nearly a hundred years, not because the bricks were bad or the design flawed,
but because the foundation was laid a mere three meters deep in soft, unstable soil.
Today, that world-famous tower—known for its permanent lean—stands like a silent sermon,
reminding us that outward beauty and lofty goals mean nothing if what’s underneath is
shallow. The lesson is clear: if the base isn’t solid, even the most impressive structures can tilt
off course.
So let me ask: Are we building our lives like the Tower of Pisa—on whatever’s convenient
at the surface? Or do we embrace the wisdom of digging deep, refusing to stop until we hit
bedrock?
Friends, the choice is ours. Let the Leaning Tower of Pisa be a vivid reminder—a visual call
that says: KEEP DIGGING! KEEP DIGGING! KEEP DIGGING! Don’t stop until you’re **firmly
established on the one Foundation that never fails—**Jesus Christ, our Rock.
Think about it! The Centurion built a synagogue—yes, bricks and mortar. But even more
importantly, he built his life on trust in Jesus! The wise builder dug deep—anchoring his very
soul to the Rock. The foolish builder took shortcuts, settled for the surface. And when the storm
hit, everything collapsed. The difference wasn’t in what they built—but where they built!
So let me look you in the eye and ask: Where are you building today? Are you digging deep into
Christ’s living Word, anchoring your life to something eternal? Or are you settling for the surface,
assuming what feels strong today will magically hold up tomorrow?

every cry of your heart. Come, and let us pray for you. Don’t carry that burden alone. Come to
the Rock.
(Pause again—maintain an atmosphere of prayer.)
(As individuals are coming forward, continue softly:)
“Come just as you are… He’s waiting with open arms… Don’t let this moment pass… He is the

4. Final Encouragement
What you have is already heartfelt and purposeful. These suggestions are simply ways to
fine-tune the flow and ensure listeners know exactly how to respond. Let the Holy Spirit guide
you in real-time—some of the most powerful altar calls happen when we lean on the Spirit’s
prompting rather than a script.
Trust God to meet people where they are, and don’t worry if not everyone responds visibly.
Sometimes, the deepest commitments happen silently in the pew. But for those who do step
forward, have prayer teams or church leaders ready to pray, listen, and guide them in next steps
(like baptism, discipleship, or counseling).

All in all, your altar call is compassionate, urgent, and clear: that’s exactly what people need to
hear when deciding how to build their lives on the Rock. God bless you as you deliver it!
