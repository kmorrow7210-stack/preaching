The Return of the Cool of the Day
Genesis 3:8–15
Opening Hook: Paradise Lost & the ache for presence
You don’t have to be into old poetry to recognize the title of John Milton’s most famous work:
Paradise Lost. Most people have never read ten thousand lines of 17th-century verse. I’m not
assigning it for homework. But you don’t need to read it to know the title is true.
You feel 'paradise lost' when a friendship that used to be easy to talk to becomes guarded and
cold. You feel it when a marriage that used to be safe feels like walking on eggshells. You feel it
when a church that be full has more empty seats than occupied.
Paradise Lost is not just a poem. It’s a diagnosis. Deep down, underneath all those smaller
losses, there is a bigger ache. We feel like we have lost Someone. We have a built-in
homesickness for a Father we can’t quite reach.

Connection to Christmas
That’s why Christmas hits weird for some of us. We’re handed candles and soft music and told
this is a season of 'joy' and 'peace,' but inside, we feel the disconnect. The Bible says that
feeling isn't random. It goes all the way back to the first 'paradise lost' moment in history.
So I want to take you to the original scene. Genesis 3. A garden. A man and a woman. And the
sound of God walking in the cool of the day.

Upsetting the Equilibrium: The Priest and the Perimeter
To understand what went wrong, you have to understand what we had. Before everything broke,
Eden wasn’t just a pretty garden with good weather. Scholars tell us that the way Genesis
describes the garden; with its gold, its entrance in the East, and its river, mirrors the
architecture of the Temple.
Eden was the first Sanctuary. It was the original Holy of Holies. It was the inner room of
creation, where God’s presence was thick and normal. And Adam? He wasn't just a farmer in
overalls. The Hebrew words used for his job, to work (avad) and to keep (shamar), are the exact
same words used later in the Bible for the High Priest guarding the Tabernacle .
Adam is the Priest-King. He is standing watch at the intersection of Heaven and Earth. He is
likely clothed in glory—'garments of light'—reflecting the God he serves . His job description is
simple: Expand the garden until the whole world is a temple . And, crucially, to guard the
perimeter . To make sure nothing unclean enters the Sanctuary.

The Disruption (The Breach)
But then... the equilibrium breaks. Not with a scream, but with a hiss. Genesis 3:1: 'Now the
serpent was more crafty than any other beast of the field.'
This wasn't just a snake in the grass. The Hebrew word is Nachash—which can mean 'The
Shining One'. This was a spiritual power, a rebel from God's council, slipping inside the wire. An
unclean intruder entered the Holy of Holies.
And the tragedy isn't just that Eve ate a fruit. The tragedy is that the Guard failed. The Priest
didn't draw his sword. He let the enemy in. And in that moment, the 'Cool of the Day'—the safety
of God’s presence—was compromised. The Invasion had begun.

Analyzing the Discrepancy: The Exile of Presence
And immediately, the atmosphere of the Garden changes. Genesis 3:8 says, 'And they heard
the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day.'
Now, we usually picture this as a gentle evening breeze. A nice time to chat. But scholars
suggest the Hebrew here—ruach hayom—can actually mean the 'Spirit of the Day' or the 'Wind
of the Storm.' This isn't a casual stroll. This is the sound of the King returning to camp. It’s the
sound of Inspection. And for the first time in human history, the sound of God doesn't bring joy. It
brings terror. Adam and Eve don't run to the Father; they run from Him. They dive into the
bushes.

The Loss of Glory (The Nakedness)
Why? Adam says, 'I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was
naked.' Wait. Weren't they naked five minutes ago? Yes. But they weren't ashamed.
Theologians believe that before the Fall, Adam and Eve were likely clothed in 'garments of
light'—the Shekinah glory of God. They shone like Moses did on the mountain. Like Jesus at the
Transfiguration. Like Stephen while stones were being hurled at his head. But when they sided
with the Serpent, the lights went out. The glory vanished. And suddenly, they looked down and
saw only their frailty. They tried to sew fig leaves together—the first attempt at religion, trying
to cover our own shame—but it wasn't enough.

We still do this.
We still hide from what we used to run toward. It just looks different now.
1. Some folks hide behind work-busyness.
They pick up every extra shift, answer emails at midnight, say yes to every project. It
sounds responsible, but if you peel it back, staying late at the office is easier than sitting
quiet with God long enough for Him to bring something up.
2. Some folks hide behind kids’ schedules.
It is travel ball, club ball, tournaments, lessons, practices, one more thing on the

calendar. Weekends are swallowed whole. There is always “just this season” that never
ends. It looks like good parenting. Sometimes it is really just a way to stay in motion so
they never have to sit in a room where God might speak.
3. Some folks hide behind screens.
Scroll TikTok until the eyes burn. Binge Netflix. Live in group chats. Always a podcast in
the ear, always noise, never silence. Earbuds in, soul shut down. If the room ever gets
quiet, the ache might surface, so they make sure it never gets quiet.
4. Some folks hide behind church work.
They will usher, run slides, sing, cook, stack chairs, run the Christmas program, you
name it. They will do anything for God as long as they do not have to sit alone with God.
Serving becomes a way to stay close to the things of God while staying guarded from
the presence of God.
5. Some folks hide behind theology and arguments.
They will go ten rounds online about Calvinism, end times charts, or Bible translations,
but never actually confess a sin, forgive an enemy, or obey a simple command. It is
easier to debate truth than to be searched by truth.
6. Some folks hide behind humor.
Always the funny one, always deflecting, always a joke ready when the conversation
gets close to the heart. If they can keep you laughing, you will never notice they are
dying inside.
7. We have fig leaves with Wi-Fi and logos on them now. Work. Sports. Church activity.
Opinions. Entertainment. All the while, the same pattern is playing out.
Adam and Eve hid behind trees. People today hide behind calendars, phones, ministries, and
noise.

The Ultimate Consequence (The Eviction)
And here is the hardest part of the story. God cannot let rebellion stay in the Sanctuary. If He
lets sin stay in His holy presence, it would be consumed. So, to save their lives, He has to exile
their persons.
The Locked Gate And to make sure they can't come back, God places the Cherubim at the
gate with a flaming sword.
This is the tragedy we are living in. The Cherubim—those angelic beings who were supposed to
be Adam’s choir—are now his armed guards. The gate to the Holy of Holies is shut. The 'Cool of
the Day' walk is over.

The Felt Need (The Homesickness)
This is why we have that ache I talked about earlier. We are literally 'East of Eden.' We are
trying to build little paradises out here in the wilderness—nice houses, nice careers, nice
Christmases. But we can't shake the feeling that we are wearing 'skins'—we are mortal, we are

broken, and we are hiding. We want to go home, but we can’t get past the sword. We are stuck
on the wrong side of the river.

Disclosing the Clue (The Promise & The Covering)
So there they stand.
Naked. Ashamed.
Fig leaves already starting to wilt.
They cannot fix this.
They cannot go back.
They cannot get past the sword that is about to bar the way.
If this were our story, this is where we would fade to black.
But God does something nobody in that garden saw coming.
He does not start by unloading on Adam and Eve.
He starts by turning to the snake.
That alone is mercy. The humans are braced for the lightning bolt, and God aims His first
sentence at their enemy.
“The LORD God said to the serpent…”
No questions.
No invitation.
No chance for the serpent to speak in his own defense.
He questions the man.
He questions the woman.
He does not question the serpent. There is no cross examination, just a verdict. Judgment with
no offer of repentance.
Which means every shred of mercy in this scene is being reserved for the guilty pair standing
there shaking in the bushes.

1. God flips the alliance
Listen to the first part of what He says:
“I will put enmity between you and the woman,
and between your offspring and her offspring…”
In other words:

“This partnership ends here.
You do not get to keep them as your allies.
I Myself am going to break this bond and set a line between you and her,
between your line and hers.”
Up until now, Adam and Eve have sided with the serpent. They believed his words over God’s.
By rights, God could have said, “You chose him. You can share his fate.”
Instead, God steps in and tears that alliance apart.
He personally inserts hostility into the relationship between the serpent and the woman.
That is mercy hiding inside a curse.

2. God narrows history to a Child
Then the language tightens:
“…he shall bruise your head,
and you shall bruise his heel.”
Not “they shall.”
Not “her offspring” as a faceless crowd.
“He.”
One particular descendant. One man. One champion.
This is the first hint that the rescue will not come through a new law, or a new prophet, or a fresh
start. It will come through a person.
And notice the strange phrase:
“Her seed.”
Throughout Scripture, family lines normally trace through the father. The seed is connected to
the man. Here, God points to the woman.
He is saying:
“From her line, despite your failure, a Seed is coming.
A human champion will rise from this broken family. He will be the serpent’s
end and your only hope.”
God hints that the deliverer will be truly one of us, born of a woman, yet marked off in a unique
way.

This is not vague optimism.
This is a pregnancy announcement at the crime scene.

3. God promises a mutual wounding, but not a mutual outcome
“He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel.”
Same verb. Different target.
A heel wound is painful. It can even be deadly.
A head wound ends the fight.
God is not promising a clean, painless victory. He is promising a costly one. The champion will
be wounded in the process of winning. There will be blood. There will be a strike that looks fatal.
But the outcome is never in doubt.
The serpent gets the heel.
The Seed gets the head.

Connection to Christmas:
At Christmas, that promised Seed steps onto the stage of history.
At the cross, the serpent sinks his fangs into His heel.
On the third day, the heel stomps down and the snake’s head is crushed.

4. God announces the serpent’s humiliation and final defeat
Around this promise, God also says to the serpent:
“On your belly you shall go,
and dust you shall eat all the days of your life.”
That is more than a comment about reptiles. In Scripture, “eating dust” is the language of
humiliation and defeat. It is being thrown down to the lowest place, associated with death and
the grave.
So in the same breath where God curses the serpent to the dust, He names the One who will
keep grinding him into it.
Genesis 3:15 is God planting a flag in the middle of the wreckage and saying to the
serpent,

“You started this. You will not finish it.
A child is coming, and He will crush you under His feet.”

Theologians call this verse the protoevangelium. It is a big word that simply means “the first
gospel.” The first announcement of good news in the Bible is not in a choir of angels. It is in a
sentence of judgment shouted over the enemy’s head while two guilty sinners listen in.
Adam and Eve are about to be sent out of the garden. The walk in the cool of the day is over.
The gate will soon be locked, guarded by flaming sword and cherubim.
But they do not leave empty handed.
They leave with a word ringing in their ears.
There will be a Seed.
There will be a Champion.
The serpent will not have the last word over this garden or over us.

5. God hints at a covering
And God does one more thing.
The fig leaves are not cutting it. Their shame is real, and their solution is pathetic. So God
Himself provides a covering.
“And the LORD God made for Adam and for his wife garments of skins and
clothed them.”
For those skins to exist, something had to die. Blood had to be shed. A life was given so that
their shame could be covered.
It is a small picture on the first pages of Scripture of a much greater covering that will come later.
One day, the promised Seed will not only crush the serpent’s head, He will also be the Lamb
whose blood does not just hide our shame, but removes it.
Right here, at the scene of the first sin, God gives His first promise and His first covering.
He speaks to the serpent to promise a Champion.
He clothes the sinners to show that He still intends to walk with people again.
This is the fulcrum of the whole story.
This is the first hint that the cool of the day is not gone forever.

Experiencing the Gospel: Emmanuel, The Walk Returns
By the time you get to the end of Genesis 3, it feels like the story is closed.
The storm-wind of the Day has blown through.
The verdict has been read.
The gate is locked.
The flaming sword turns every way.
The Spirit who moved in the garden as the wind of inspection and judgment now stands
between man and God. The problem is not that God stopped wanting to walk with us. The
problem is that we are no longer fit for that walk. We are defiled, and we cannot make ourselves
clean.
From that point on, the whole Old Testament is God carefully rebuilding what was lost in Eden.
He walks in a tent among Israel. Same verb, same idea.
“I will walk among you and will be your God, and you shall be my people” (Leviticus
26:12).
His presence settles on the tabernacle and later the temple like the Eden glory-cloud. There are
steps, sacrifices, priests, curtains, warnings. You can visit, but you cannot live there.
It is real grace, but it is not yet the cool of the day.
Then you turn the page into the New Testament and read this sentence:
“The Word became flesh and dwelt among us.”
Literally, “tabernacled among us.”
John is waving a flag. The God who walked in the garden, who moved about in the camp of
Israel, has pitched His tent again. This time not in canvas or stone, but in human skin.
Christmas is not just “a baby is born.”
Christmas is “God has stepped inside the tunics of skin.”
He has entered the exile.

God with us in the storm
Remember that ruach of Genesis 3, the “wind of the Day,” the terrifying approach of the Holy
One that sent Adam into the bushes.
Now hear what the angel says to Joseph:
“They shall call his name Immanuel,”
which means, “God with us” (Matthew 1:23).

The same Holy presence that once meant “you must hide or be destroyed” now arrives in a way
that invites you to draw near without being burned.
How?
By doing what we could never do.
By becoming what we were meant to be and failed to be.
Jesus comes as the Seed of the woman, the second Adam.
● Adam met the serpent in a garden full of food and failed.
● Jesus meets the tempter in a wilderness with an empty stomach and holds the line.
● Adam grasped at being like God and brought death to his line.
● Jesus, who is God, humbles Himself, takes flesh, and brings life to Adam’s line.

Where Adam opened the door of the sanctuary to the serpent, Jesus opens His own body on
the cross to break the serpent’s claim. Where Adam’s choice pulled us out of the presence,
Jesus’ obedience pulls us back in.

The woman and the fruit
You can even see it in the women at the two turning points.
In Eden, a woman listens to a lying spirit, reaches for forbidden fruit, and hands death to her
husband.
In Nazareth, a woman listens to the Holy Spirit, receives the living Word, and carries the One
who will give life to the world.
Eve extends her hand and offers a fruit that brings the curse.
Mary offers the fruit of her womb, the blessed One, who will hang on a tree and turn that curse
into blessing.
We do not worship Mary, but we can honor what God did through her. Where the first woman
helped usher sin into the world, this young Jewish girl, by simple faith, becomes the doorway
through which the Serpent Crusher steps into history.
Eve said, in effect, “My will be done.”
Mary says, “Let it be to me according to your word.”
Through that yes, the Seed of the woman finally arrives.

The Holy One steps into our uncleanness
Here is the heart of it.
We cannot scrub off our guilt.
We cannot argue our way back through the cherubim.
We cannot make ourselves fit for the presence of a holy God.
So He does something that no other god in any story ever does.
He comes all the way into our condition.
The Holy One is born into a family tree full of sinners.
He breathes our air, walks our ground, stands in our baptism waters, touches our lepers, eats
at our tables. The One whose presence once meant certain judgment now moves through the
world like a mobile Eden, healing, cleansing, forgiving, driving out the powers that chained us.
Everywhere He goes, it sounds a little like the cool of the day again.
“Follow Me.”
“Take heart, your sins are forgiven.”
“Come to Me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”
On the cross, the heel is struck.
At the empty tomb, the head is crushed.
At Pentecost, the ruach that once announced judgment in Eden fills human hearts as a gift, not
a threat. The Spirit who drove us out now comes to live within.
Christmas is the moment that makes all that possible.
The sound of God’s footsteps returns, not in a storm at the edge of a garden, but in the soft
cries of a child laid in a manger.
The One we were hiding from has come looking for us again.
Not to say, “Where are you?” in judgment, but to say, “Here I am, for you,” in grace.
That is what we are celebrating when we say, “Immanuel.”
The walk in the cool of the day is not gone forever.
In Christ, it has already begun again.

Anticipating the Consequences: The Wind Returns, Eden Completed
We started in a garden hearing the sound of God in the ruach hayom
…the wind of the day…
…and watching two people dive into the trees.
From that moment on, the question underneath the whole Bible has been:

“Will we ever walk with Him again without fear?”
At the Cross, the heel is struck.
At the Resurrection, the head is crushed.
But God is not done. He is not just interested in getting us forgiven. He is interested in getting
Eden back, upgraded.
So what happens next?

1. The ruach comes back as a gift, not a threat
Back in Genesis 3, that ruach word tagged the approach of God in judgment, the “wind of the
Day” that made guilty humans run for cover.
Fast forward.
On the night of His resurrection, Jesus stands with His disciples, and John says:
“He breathed on them and said to them, ‘Receive the Holy Spirit’” (John 20:22).
That should ring bells.
● In Genesis 2, God breathes into Adam and he becomes a living soul.
● In John 20, the risen Christ breathes on His followers and they become a new-creation
people.

Then, in Acts 2, it goes public.
“Suddenly there came from heaven a sound like a mighty rushing wind, and it filled
the entire house…”
Different language (Hebrew vs Greek), but the same word-field: breath, wind, Spirit.
● In Eden, the wind of the Day exposed sin and pushed humanity out.
● In Jerusalem, the rushing wind brings the Spirit in and pulls people back to God.

What used to announce, “The Judge is here, you’d better hide,” now announces, “The Helper is
here, you can come out of hiding.”
Pentecost is God saying,

“The cool of the day has started again.
I am not just with you in one garden or one temple.
I am in you, wherever you go.”
The church becomes a walking Eden, a living temple, a community where God “walks among”
His people again, not in a cloud in one room, but by His Spirit in many hearts.

2. The Spirit as promissory note of a better Eden
The Spirit is not just comfort; He is a down payment.
Paul calls Him the “guarantee” of our inheritance. In other words, Pentecost is the first
installment of the world that is coming.
● In Eden, one spot on the map was sanctuary and outside was wild.
● In Israel, one tent or one building was sanctuary and outside was common.
● In the church age, every believer is a little sanctuary, a place where heaven and earth
touch.
● In the new creation, there will be no “outside” at all.

The Spirit in you is God’s way of saying,
“What you feel in flashes now, you will live in forever then.
This presence is not a phase. It is your future.”

3. New Heaven and New Earth: the garden finally takes over
When you flip to the last pages of the Bible, you do not see clouds and harps. You see Eden
language, turned up to eleven.
● The New Jerusalem is a perfect cube, same shape as the Holy of Holies. That means
the whole city is now the inner room. There is no outer court anymore.
● A river of the water of life flows from the throne of God and the Lamb, just like the river
that flowed from Eden and split into four.

● The tree of life is back, not guarded now, but lining both sides of the river, “for the
healing of the nations.” Access is open. The curse is gone.
● The voice from the throne says, “Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man… They
will see His face.” No more hiding in the trees. No more distance. Face to face.
The story does not end with us going back to a primitive garden. It ends with a garden-city
where:
● Every square inch is sanctuary.
● The serpent is gone for good.
● The flaming sword is taken down.
● The glory that used to flash in a cloud fills everything.
Eden was the starter home.
New Heaven and New Earth are the finished house.
And the link between them is the Seed of the woman.
He crushed the serpent’s head at the Cross.
He poured out the ruach at Pentecost.
He will walk us into that garden-city when He returns.

4. So where does that leave us right now?
Right now, you live between Acts 2 and Revelation 22.
● The Spirit already lives in you, if you belong to Christ.
● The presence you lost in Adam is already restored in principle.
● The full, visible, unbroken walk is still ahead.

So the call is simple:
Stop living like Eden is only behind you.
Start living like Eden is ahead of you and the Spirit is with you.
When you pray in your car, that is a little taste of the cool of the day.
When you gather with God’s people and sense His nearness, that is a preview of the city where

His presence fills everything.
When you open the Word and the Spirit convicts and comforts you, that is the wind of the Day
blowing again, not to drive you out, but to draw you in.
Christmas, Pentecost, and New Creation are all one line:
● God with us.
● God in us.
● God around us, forever.
That is where the Seed of the woman is taking the story.

Closing: Stepping Out of the Trees
We’ve covered a lot of ground this morning.
● We started in Eden, where walking with God in the cool of the day was normal.
● We watched that fellowship shatter with one bite, and we felt the ache of living east of
Eden, hiding behind our modern fig leaves.
● We heard God speak a promise to the serpent: a Seed is coming. A Champion.
● We watched that promise show up in a manger as Immanuel, God with us.
● We watched Him crush the serpent’s head at the cross, walk out of the grave, and send
His Spirit like a fresh wind so the presence of God could move back into human hearts.
● And we looked ahead to a day when Eden isn’t just a memory behind us but a world in
front of us: a new heaven and new earth where God walks with His people forever.
So here’s the real question:
Where are you in that story?
God asked Adam that in the garden:
“Where are you?”
Not because God was confused about his GPS location.
Because Adam needed to admit where he was hiding.
That question is still hanging in the air this morning.

Two groups in the room
I think there are at least two kinds of people listening right now.

1. Those who have never stepped out of hiding and trusted Christ
You know the ache.
You know the homesickness.
You’ve tried to fix it with relationships, work, pleasure, religion, whatever. But if you’re honest,
you are still on the wrong side of the sword, still trying to sew fig leaves together and pretend
you’re fine.
Hear this as clearly as I can say it:
● You cannot clean yourself up enough to walk with a holy God.
● You cannot sneak back into Eden.
● But the Seed of the woman has already opened the way.

Jesus Christ, the Son of God, took your sin on Himself, died in your place, rose again, and now
stands as the only door back into the presence of God.
If you’ve never repented and trusted Him, today is not a Bible study for you. Today is an
invitation.
God is not just saying, “Where are you?”
In Christ He is saying, “Come home.”

2. Those who belong to Jesus but have been living in the bushes
You are saved, but you’ve been hiding.
You know the fig leaves we talked about.
Work. Kids’ schedules. Screens. Church busy-ness. Theology debates. Humor. Noise.
You still believe in Jesus, but you’ve quietly backed away from the cool of the day. You don’t
pray like you used to. You don’t open the Word like you used to. You don’t let yourself be fully
known like you used to.
You’re in the house, but you’re living like an exile.
The Spirit of God lives in you, and you’ve been treating Him like a landlord you occasionally pay
rent to, not a Father who wants to walk with you.

Today is an invitation for you too:
● To step out from behind the schedule and the screen.
● To confess the thing you’ve been hiding.
● To ask the Spirit to stir fresh hunger for His presence again.
