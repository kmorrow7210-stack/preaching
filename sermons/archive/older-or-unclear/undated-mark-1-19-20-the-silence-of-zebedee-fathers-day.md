Empty Boats and Abandoned Nets
Mark 1:19-20
Mark 1:19-20 says, "And when he had gone a little further thence, he saw James the son
of Zebedee, and John his brother, who also were in the ship mending their nets. And
straightway he called them: and they left their father Zebedee in the ship with the hired
servants, and went after him."
The Loudest Sermon Never Preached
Zebedee has no words recorded in Scripture. His boys walk off with a rabbi they're calling the
Messiah, and we don't get a "Get back in this boat!" We don't get a "Boy, have you lost your
mind?" Nothing. Just silence.
And somehow, that silence might be the loudest Father's Day sermon ever preached.
See, we read this story and picture it like a corporate raid—some charismatic leader shows up
and poaches the top talent in a single afternoon. But dig deeper into the Gospels, and you'll find
this was more like a long, strategic acquisition. The initial talks had been happening for weeks.
John—yeah, the son standing in that boat—he'd already spent a day with Jesus (John 1). Came
home with his face glowing, trying to explain what he'd seen. "Dad, John the Baptist pointed him
out. Called him the Lamb of God. We followed him, asked where he was staying. Dad, we talked
all day. This is Him. This is the One."
Zebedee's silence wasn't shock. It was the silence of a man watching his investment
mature.
The Family Business at a Crossroads
Now let me paint this picture for you. Zebedee wasn't some broke fisherman barely making it.
The Bible says he had hired men. In first-century Galilee, if you had employees, you're not
surviving—you're thriving. This wasn't not a one-boat operation. Each boat worth 3-5 years of
wages - would be a modern day $200,000 investment. This was Zebedee & Sons Enterprises.
The Galilean dream.
But here's what changes everything: This wasn't a hostile takeover. This looks more like a family
decision.
Picture the dinner table conversations over the past few weeks. John comes home from that
first encounter, barely able to eat. "Mom, Dad, Andrew was with me. You know, Simon's
brother? We found the Messiah."

Salome—that's Mama Zebedee—she doesn't say, "Found who?" She says, "Tell me
everything." Because this family had been waiting. just like we are today. Devout followers of
God were awaiting the arrival of the Promise
They'd built this business expecting to pass it down. But what if God had something bigger in
mind?
The Preparation Years
The boys didn't hesitate when Jesus called because they'd been prepared their whole lives for
this moment.
Think about it. What kind of father raises sons who are following John the Baptist in their spare
time? While other boys their age are focused on profit margins and market share, James and
John are out in the wilderness listening to a prophet in camel hair talking about the Kingdom of
God.
That's not rebellion. That's recognition. They recognized spiritual authority because
someone taught them what it looked like.
I believe we often see empty boats and think of tragedy. We see abandoned nets and write it up
as failure. But what was really happening? James and John were mending nets with their minds
on the Kingdom. Going through the motions of everyday work while their hearts were already
following a different call. This wasn't abandonment; it was graduation.
The Four-Stage Launch
Let me break down what really happened here. This wasn't a drive-by calling. This was a
four-stage process, and Zebedee was there for all of them:
A Four-Stage Calling
Stage One: The Endorsement
Every great calling has an inside man, a credible witness. But for the sons of Zebedee, the
recruitment process was even more direct than we remember. It didn't begin with a second-hand
report from their partner, Andrew. John—the future apostle—was standing right there with
Andrew when John the Baptist pointed to Jesus and said, "Behold, the Lamb of God." He was
the other disciple who spent that first day with the Messiah. The call wasn't based on someone
else's testimony; it was confirmed by his own.
Imagine the scene. Andrew and another disciple—most likely John, the son of Zebedee—are
following the nation's most trusted spiritual authority. One day, John the Baptist points to his
cousin from Nazareth and his voice cuts through the desert air: “Behold, the Lamb of God!”

This is a seismic event. The wild man endorses his replacement. For John, this is the beginning
of what John Wesley would champion as the Via Salutis—the first step onto the path of
salvation, prompted by a trusted voice. He and Andrew follow Jesus and spend the day with
him. The encounter changes everything! And then, the meeting ends. No “call to ministry” is
issued. It is simply an introduction that he must now process.

Stage Two: The Wrestling
After the spiritual high of meeting the one John the Baptist called the Lamb of God, something
strange happens: John goes back to work. The Spirit sends Jesus into the wilderness, and John
returns to mending nets with his brother James, his father Zebedee, and their business
partners, Simon and Andrew.
This is where "reality hits." Their experience must now survive contact with the mundane. It’s a
crucial marination period where John's testimony and the initial excitement must settle into a
deep and abiding question.
Can you imagine the conversations in the boat? This is the wrestling match for the whole family
enterprise. It's the season of fighting off the weeds of doubt and the rocks of old habits that
threaten to choke out the fragile new seed of faith.
Stage Three: The Evidence
While the Zebedee family business is wrestling and working, Jesus isn't waiting in the wings. He
comes out of the wilderness and launches his public ministry right in their backyard of Galilee.
This period is the vital context that makes the later calling logical. The man John met is now the
talk of the region. Jesus begins teaching with an unheard-of authority.
New Testament scholar and Asbury professor, Ben Witherington III, notes that the term for
'authority' (exousia) in the Gospels means more than just being a good speaker; it implies
having the actual power and right to act for God.
He is no longer a mysterious figure. He is the local rabbi with a messianic reputation and a
supernatural track record. Far from asking for a blind leap, Jesus is building a case for His
authority. He's providing a mountain of evidence, proving his credentials right before their eyes
before He ever asks for their lives.
James and John are still at work.
Stage Four: The Surrender of an Empire
This brings us to the climax on the lake. Before I get into this narrative, I want it to be known that
I put this together from an exhaustive study of the four gospels and the timeline. People write in
different styles and have various philosophies on chronology, so they are not always perfectly
correlated in the timeline. But this is what I’ve put together.

It's a corporate event for the whole fishing company. After a night of fruitless labor, Jesus directs
Simon to launch out and lower the nets. Simon, deferring to the Teacher's reputation, obeys.
The result is a catch so massive the nets begin to tear. This isn't just Simon's problem; it's a
crisis for the whole business. He signals for his partners—James and John—to bring their boat
to help.
The sons of Zebedee row over and witness the miracle firsthand. They see a catch that defies
all logic and experience. They help haul in a haul so massive it threatens to sink both boats.
This isn't just a sign; it's an irrefutable proof of concept delivered directly to their professional
doorstep. The power of this man from Nazareth is now a business reality, overwhelming their
combined expertise.
When they get to shore, stunned and surrounded by a mountain of fish, Jesus looks past the
haul and calls the brothers. And in that moment, James and John do the unthinkable. They
leave the boats. They leave the greatest single catch of their careers. And they leave their
father, Zebedee.
This is the ultimate surrender. For Zebedee, it’s the moment his world is dismantled. He watches
his sons—his partners, his heirs, the future of his empire—walk away to follow this rabbi. There
is no recorded protest. No shouting. Just the profound silence of a man watching his life’s work
become bait for catching something eternal. His silence is not resignation, but recognition.
He has prepared his sons to hear God's voice their whole lives, and now that the voice has
finally called, his hands open, and he lets them go.
Zebedee didn't lose his sons in an afternoon. He launched them over weeks of family prayer
and preparation.
When Success Looks Like Strategy
You know what this reminds me of? A master craftsman training apprentices. He's not just
teaching them to work wood or stone—he's teaching them to see, to think, to create. Every
lesson about grain patterns is really about paying attention. Every correction about
measurements is really about excellence.
That's Zebedee. He thought he was building a fishing business. God was using him to build
apostles. He just used nets and boats to do it.
The Empire We're Building vs. The Kingdom He's Calling
Here's what we gotta understand, church. Zebedee was building an empire. A good empire. A
godly empire, even. Boats and nets and security and legacy. Nothing wrong with that... until the
King of an entirely different Kingdom shows up at your dock.
And suddenly, Zebedee has to choose: The Empire of Zebedee or the Kingdom of God.

But see, because he'd been preparing all along, it wasn't really a choice. It was a graduation.
We all got our empires. I know I do. Maybe it's that full-ride scholarship you're dreaming about
for your daughter. Maybe it's watching your son become a doctor, making more money than you
ever dreamed of.
Maybe you got five acres carved out behind your property where they'll build. Close enough for
Sunday dinner, far enough for privacy. Maybe it's the family business with their name already
under yours on the sign.
Beautiful dreams. Nothing wrong with success. But you've been modeling Scripture in front of
them their whole lives. "What does it profit a man to gain the whole world and lose his
soul?"
There are plenty of depressed millionaires. That doesn't mean money makes you depressed,
but it sure doesn't cure anything either.
So what if Jesus is calling them to Myanmar instead of medicine? What if He's calling them to
ministry instead of millions? What if His plan looks nothing like your Pinterest board?
The question is: Have you been preparing them to recognize His voice when He calls?
Illustration - The Elliots
Clara Elliot is in the kitchen, and her six-year-old son, Jim, walks in with the stunning theological
clarity that only a child can possess. "Mama, Jesus can come whenever He wants because I'm
saved now."
That's not just a cute kid moment. That is the sound of discipleship taking root. That is a father
and mother realizing they aren't just raising a good boy; they are building a man of God who, at
age six, already understands grace.
Fast forward through his youth—a time marked by intense passion, discipline, and a journal full
of entries like, "He is no fool who gives what he cannot keep to gain what he cannot lose." This
wasn't a rebellious kid who suddenly found faith.
Then comes the ultimate test. Jim feels called to the Aucas of Ecuador, a tribal people so
isolated and hostile they were known for killing every single outsider who attempted contact.
From a modern parenting perspective, this is insanity. This is the moment you step in, you
forbid, you reason, you talk about safer mission fields. You bubble-wrap your brilliant son.
But Fred and Clara did the opposite. They had spent two decades teaching their son to hear
and obey the voice of God. How could they, at the final hour, tell him God was wrong? They let
him go. They released the arrow.

Their silence, their blessing, their support—it wasn't passive resignation. It was the final, most
costly act of their own discipleship. It was the raw, rugged faith of a parent who believes God's
claim on their child is greater than their own.
That is Zebedee parenting. It's building a business, an inheritance, a legacy, only to watch with
a surrendered heart as your children leave it all behind to follow the call of the Messiah.
The Divine Acquisition
Before we close, let’s look at what happened next in this story. Salome—Mama Zebedee—she
becomes one of Jesus's most devoted followers. She's there at the cross. She's there at the
tomb. This wasn't a family torn apart by faith; this was a family united in purpose.
And those hired servants? They kept the business running. God didn't destroy what Zebedee
built; He repurposed it. The business that used to support a family now supported a movement.
Every fish those hired men caught, every denarius they earned—it was funding the Kingdom.
It's like when Google acquires a promising startup. They don't burn it down. They integrate it into
something bigger. That's what Jesus did with Zebedee & Sons. He didn't destroy it; He acquired
it for the Kingdom.
Altar Call - The Invitation
Let's go back to that boat for a minute. Zebedee's sitting there with the hired men. The nets are
still in his lap. But his eyes? They're on the shore.
He's watching his boys follow that carpenter. And somewhere deep in his chest, past the ache
of goodbye, there's something else. Recognition. The same voice calling his boys is the voice
he's been teaching them to hear all along.
Let me tell you about some modern Zebedees. They're sitting right here in this room.
For years, Tessa's parents have been living the Zebedee story. When God called their daughter
to marry a preacher, they knew what that meant. Four hours away. Then three hours. We almost
moved 19 hours away—that's a two-day drive just to see your daughter.
But they didn't just accept it. They SUPPORTED it. Every move. Every transition. Every
goodbye. They helped us pack boxes knowing those boxes were taking their daughter further
away. They celebrated job opportunities that meant fewer Sunday dinners. They prayed for
churches that weren't theirs to thrive because their daughter was there.
You know what that is? That's Zebedee faith. Raising a daughter to hear God's voice, then
trusting that voice even when it calls her away from your table

Church, I'm not just talking to fathers this morning. I'm talking to every mother who's wondering
if you're messing up your kids. Every grandparent trying to pour into the next generation. Every
teacher, every coach, every mentor who's investing in somebody else's future.
The invitation this morning is simple: Will you prepare them to recognize His voice? Not your
voice. Not your dreams. His voice.
Some of you think God's gonna hijack your kids. That's not how He works. He's gonna give you
a thousand small moments to prepare their hearts. Teaching them to tie fishing knots? That's
preparation. Helping with math homework? That's preparation. Every conversation about right
and wrong, every bedtime prayer, every 'why' question you answer—you're not just raising kids.
You're preparing disciples.
The question isn't whether God will call them. The question is whether you'll prepare them to
recognize His voice when He does.
Others of you—you're the ones being called. You've been feeling it for weeks, maybe months.
Like James and John, you've had your first encounter with Jesus. You've been processing it,
praying about it, trying to explain it to your family. And now He's standing at your boat saying,
"It's time."
Don't wait for a miraculous catch of fish. The miracle is that He's calling you at all.
The altar's open. If you need wisdom to prepare someone for their calling, come get it. If you're
hearing His voice calling you to something that makes no earthly sense but perfect Kingdom
sense, come get the courage to follow. If you just need somebody to hold your arms up while
you release what you've been building, we're here for that too.
This is for everybody. Because we've all got something we're holding onto tighter than we're
holding onto Him.
Who's ready to let go? Who's ready to launch? Who's ready to trade their empire for the
Kingdom?
Come on, church. The boats are waiting. But the Kingdom is calling.
