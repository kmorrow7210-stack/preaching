A Tale of Two Sabbaths
Mark 6:1-6; Mark 1:21-28

Introduction – Seeing, but Not Seeing
Let me tell y’all what happened to me the other morning. Actually—who am I kidding? This
happens all the time.
I’m in my office, studying, reading, researching, getting deep in the Word… and after a while, it
gets a little warm. So, I take off my hoodie. No big deal, right? But then—my glasses are gone.
Vanished. Disappeared. Now, you already know—I can’t see a thing without ‘em.
So, I start TEARING UP MY OFFICE. Desk drawers flying open, books all over the place—I’m
flipping through pages like my Bible’s about to give me a direct answer to, “Where did I put my
glasses?!”
I go back home, full-on Roy Orbison mode, squinting my way through the house, searching
the bathroom, thinking maybe, just maybe, I left ‘em in there. But nothing.
Frustration building. Heart pounding. The whole time I’m muttering, “Where are those glasses?!”
And then… in a moment of sheer desperation… I reach up.
And there they are.
PLANTED RIGHT ON TOP OF MY OWN HEAD.
Y’all ever been there? (Pause for laughter and reaction.)
The thing I was searching for—the thing I needed—was right there all along.
And that, church, makes you stop and think.
How many times do we do the same thing with God?
● How often do we miss what’s right in front of us?
● How often do we search and search for something God has already placed within
reach?
● How often do we let our own expectations blind us to what Jesus is doing right now?

The Invisible Gorilla & The Blindness of Nazareth
You know, this isn’t just something that happens with glasses. It’s how our minds work.

There’s this famous experiment called the Invisible Gorilla Test. Maybe you’ve heard of it.
● Researchers had people watch a video and count how many times a basketball was
passed between players.
● Simple enough, right? But here’s the twist:
● Right in the middle of the video, a person in a full gorilla suit walks through the
scene.
And guess what?
Most people are so focused on counting the passes that they never even see the gorilla!
A gorilla. Right there.
And they miss it.
Sounds crazy, doesn’t it? But listen—this is exactly what happened in our Scripture today
First let’s look at what happened in Capernaum

Capernaum – The Authority of Jesus
It’s the Sabbath in Capernaum. The streets are alive with people hustling to the
synagogue—families, neighbors, friends—everyone pressing in, hearts aflame with
expectation.
And right there in their midst is Jesus. He’s fresh off calling His disciples, and there’s a fire in
His eyes—like He’s on a divine mission, laser-focused on what’s about to happen.
He steps into the synagogue—no introductions, no warm-up—and He begins to teach. And
let me tell you, He doesn’t sound anything like the scribes with their dusty interpretations and
scripted speeches. His words are alive—they’re electric, bursting with authority that seems to
shake the very walls.
● You can practically feel the air crackle!
● The congregation leans forward, wide-eyed, drawn in by every word.
● People whisper, “What is this new teaching?” and “Where does He get this authority?”
And in the middle of this holy hush, right as people are caught up in the wonder of God’s
Word, something shatters the atmosphere:
A shriek—high and piercing!
A man in the crowd, face contorted in torment, cries out at the top of his lungs:
“WHAT DO YOU WANT WITH US, JESUS OF NAZARETH?! HAVE YOU COME
TO DESTROY US?! I KNOW WHO YOU ARE—THE HOLY ONE OF GOD!”
A demon has just manifested, right there in church! Darkness has come face-to-face with

holiness, and it’s terrified. It sees light shining in a place it has no right to be.
Jesus doesn’t flinch. With a commanding voice that seems to roll back the gates of hell itself,
He rebukes the spirit:
“BE SILENT, AND COME OUT OF HIM!”
The entire synagogue is dead silent—it’s like the world is holding its breath. The man
convulses, he writhes, a terrible shriek escapes his lips… and then—
Silence.
The demon is gone.
Banished.
Cast out by the power of Jesus Christ.
Can you imagine the looks on people’s faces? The jaw-dropping wonder? They’re grabbing
each other, gasping, whispering, “What just happened?!”
Then it’s like a dam breaks—everyone starts talking at once:
“What is this new teaching?”
“Even evil spirits obey Him!”
“We’ve never seen power like this!”
Word spreads like wildfire across Capernaum, through the neighboring villages, into the
countryside:
Jesus is here.
He doesn’t just speak truth—He wields authority over the unseen realms.
This is no ordinary teacher.

Jesus in His Hometown – The Tragedy of Nazareth
But now, shift scenes with me. We leave the bustling energy of Capernaum and move to
Nazareth—Jesus’ own hometown. Where He grew up. Where everybody knows His name.
The news travels fast: “Jesus is back! The one we’ve heard about, the one who’s been stirring
up miracles everywhere—He’s coming to our synagogue!”
People buzz around, hyped for the Sabbath. They’re anticipating something big.
Sure enough, the synagogue is packed to the brim. Folks are squeezed into every seat,
standing in the doorways, peering in through windows, all eyes on Jesus as He steps up to
read and teach. And for a moment, it’s like we’re back in Capernaum—everyone is amazed,
struck by the weight of His words, the wisdom in His voice, the confidence that radiates from

Him.
But then—like a sour note in a beautiful song—skepticism creeps in. The whispers turn from
wonder to criticism:
“Wait a second… isn’t this the carpenter? Mary’s boy?”
“We know His brothers and sisters. We watched this kid learn to walk!”
“He’s nobody special—just a hometown boy who got a bit too big for his britches!”
Familiarity breeds contempt, and the mood in that synagogue chills. The same Jesus who
cast out demons in Capernaum is now questioned in Nazareth. The same Messiah who
astonished crowds just a few towns over is dismissed by the folks who watched Him grow up.
And here’s the heartbreak: their skepticism slams the door on miracles.
Scripture tells us:
“He could do no mighty work there, except that He laid His hands on a few sick
people and healed them.”
“He marveled because of their unbelief.”
Jesus—God in the flesh— is astonished. Not by great faith, but by great doubt.
It’s not that His power is suddenly limited; it’s that their unbelief literally locks out what God
wants to do. They’re so stuck in their small box of who they think Jesus is, they can’t
recognize who He truly is.
So He leaves Nazareth behind—a town robbed of its own blessing. He moves on to other
villages where people are hungry to receive.
That’s the tragedy of Nazareth: they had the Messiah right in front of them—and they let
familiarity blind them to the miracle.

From Miracles to Missed Moments
Capernaum welcomed Jesus with wide-eyed expectation—and saw His power on full
display.
Nazareth approached Him with arms folded and eyes rolling—and missed their chance to
witness the miraculous.
Same Jesus.
Same authority.
Vastly different outcomes.
Friends, the question is: which one are we?

Practical Application – Do We Have a
Nazareth Mindset?
Church, it’s easy to read about Nazareth and shake our heads.
It’s easy to say, “How could they miss it? How could they not see Jesus for who He was?”
But before we judge them too harshly, let me ask you something:
● How many times have we gotten so comfortable with church that we stop expecting God
to move?
● How many times have we put God in a box, assuming we already know how He works?
● How many times have we overlooked the presence of Jesus in our own lives because
He came in an unexpected way?
The people of Nazareth missed their miracle moment because they were too familiar with
Jesus.
And church, if we’re not careful, we’ll do the same thing.
We say we want revival… but are we open to it looking different than we expect?
We say we want Jesus to move… but are we actually making room for Him to do it?
We say we want miracles… but have we already decided how God has to do them?
Nazareth was waiting for the Messiah, but they were expecting Him to fit their mold.
So when He didn’t match their expectations, they rejected Him.
And if we’re honest… sometimes, so do we.
● We want a Superman Jesus—a God who swoops in with spectacular displays of power.
● But we dismiss a Clark Kent Jesus—a God who speaks in whispers, who works in the
ordinary, who shows up in places we least expect.
And that brings me to this:

Superman, Clark Kent, and the Hidden Glory of Jesus
Let’s talk about Superman for a second.
Everybody recognizes Superman—the hero, the symbol of strength, the savior of the city.
But Clark Kent? He blends into the background. He’s just an ordinary reporter, fumbling with his
glasses, easy to overlook.
But here’s the kicker:

Clark Kent and Superman are the same person.
The only difference?
How people see him.
The folks at The Daily Planet don’t realize that beneath Clark Kent’s mild-mannered disguise is
the world’s greatest hero. They’re looking right at him but don’t recognize the power standing in
front of them.
And church—this is exactly what happened in Nazareth.
They had Jesus. Right there. The Son of God in the flesh. The one who spoke the universe
into existence.
And they said, “Isn’t this just the carpenter?”
They couldn’t see past what was familiar to recognize what was divine.

Conclusion – Are We Looking for Superman While
Missing Clark Kent?
And here’s what I need you to understand:
Jesus still walks among us today.
But if you’re only looking for thunder from heaven, you might miss the whisper of the Holy
Spirit in your heart.
If you’re waiting for a dramatic miracle, you might miss the small ways God is showing His
faithfulness.
If you’re expecting God to move only in ways that make sense to you, you might miss the
very thing He’s doing right now.
Because the truth is:
● God shows up in the ordinary moments of life.
● He speaks through the people we overlook.
● He moves in ways we don’t expect.
And if we’re not careful, we’ll be like Nazareth—so stuck in our assumptions that we miss
Jesus standing right in front of us.
So here’s my challenge to you:
● Are we going to be a Capernaum church—hungry, expectant, ready for Jesus?
● Or are we going to be a Nazareth church—skeptical, closed off, blind to what God

is doing?
Because listen—Jesus is here.
Right now.
And the only question is: Do we recognize Him?

Altar Call – Seeing Jesus, Receiving
Jesus, Following Jesus
I. The Call to Salvation – Recognizing the Savior in Front of You
Nazareth missed their moment with Jesus.
But you don’t have to miss yours.
Some of you in this room—Jesus is standing right in front of you.
He’s calling your name.
He’s inviting you into new life, forgiveness, salvation.
And the question is: Do you see Him?
Some of you have been searching—for meaning, for peace, for something real.
And I’m here to tell you—what you’ve been looking for isn’t found in success,
relationships, or achievements.
It’s found in Jesus.
He is not just a good teacher.
He is not just a historical figure.
He is the Son of God, the Savior of the world.
And today, He’s calling you.
If you need Jesus—if you need forgiveness, if you need a fresh start—don’t wait.
Right now, lift your hand and say, “That’s me. I need Jesus.”
If you feel that tug in your heart, don’t ignore it.
That’s not emotion. That’s the Holy Spirit drawing you.
Come to this altar. Don’t hesitate. Don’t miss your moment.
Nazareth missed theirs. Don’t let that be you.

II. The Call to Faith – Moving from Nazareth to Capernaum
Now, church, some of you—you already know Jesus, but you’ve grown too familiar.
Somewhere along the way, your hunger faded.
Somewhere along the way, your expectation died.
And today, God is saying, “Shake off the Nazareth mindset!”
● If you’ve been putting God in a box—come forward.
● If you’ve stopped believing for miracles—come forward.
● If you’ve been showing up to church but haven’t truly been expecting God to
move—come forward.
It’s time to stop looking for a Superman while ignoring the Clark Kent Jesus who is moving in
our midst.
It’s time to believe again.
It’s time to expect again.
It’s time to step into faith again.
If you need a fresh move of God in your life—come to the altar now.

III. The Call for Every Need – He Sees You
Finally, this altar is open for anyone carrying a burden today.
Jesus is not just the Savior of your soul—He is the healer of your heart, the restorer of
your home, the deliverer of your mind.
Maybe you’re here, and you’re carrying something heavy.

Maybe it’s a sickness—in your body or in someone you love.
Maybe it’s a broken relationship—a marriage, a family situation that feels impossible.
Maybe it’s a mental battle—anxiety, depression, fear.
Maybe it’s a financial struggle—you’re trusting God, but the bills keep piling up.

Jesus sees you.
He hasn’t forgotten you.
And today—He is here to meet your need.
So if you need healing, breakthrough, strength—come.
If you need to feel His presence again—come.
This is your moment.

Don’t hold back. Don’t hesitate.
Come, and let’s believe together.
Jesus is here.
And today—we choose to see Him.
