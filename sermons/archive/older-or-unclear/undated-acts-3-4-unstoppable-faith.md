Unstoppable: When Your Faith Gets You in Trouble

(Sermon Draft)
(The Hook: J.W. Buckalew)
There are names in the history of the Church of God that carry the weight of legend. Names
like J.W. Buckalew. They called him "Rough and Ready" because of his brusque manner and
stormy temperament1. Before his conversion, he was a gambler, a drunkard, and a fugitive
from the law2. But when God got ahold of him, he served the Lord as vigorously and daringly
as he had once lived for Satan3.
In 1910, he was conducting a tent revival in Alabama City that was shaking the whole town4.
People were falling under the power of God all over the tent, some getting saved right in the
public schools5555. Gambling houses and church ice cream suppers alike were shutting down
because everyone was at the revival6. Finally, the authorities had enough. They threatened to
burn his tent, but he refused to leave7. So one night, three drunk policemen arrested him and
threw him in jail8. Through the window of his cell, he watched them burn his tent to the
ground9.
But does he sulk or cower? No, sir. The record says he and his friends spent the whole night
praising God, feeling they were "counted worthy to suffer shame for His name"10. Come
morning, they release him, no charges filed11. And what does J.W. Buckalew do? He walks right
back to the ruins, stands on the ashes of his burned-out tent, and preaches the gospel
anyway12. And right there, on burned ground, revival broke out and a Church of God was
planted that remains to this day13.
That’s how it works when the gospel really moves—it upsets the equilibrium. It rattles the
world’s neat little plans. It happened in Alabama, and it happened long before in Jerusalem.

1. Upsetting the Equilibrium: A Miracle and an Arrest (Acts 3 & 4)

In Jerusalem, the equilibrium was a man who had been lame for over forty years, sitting at the
gate of the temple called Beautiful14. Everybody knew him. He was part of the landscape. Until
one day, two of Jesus’ followers, Peter and John, walk by. They don’t have silver or gold, but

they have a Name. And in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, that man gets up walking,
and leaping, and praising God.
The whole city is in an uproar. And this public, undeniable miracle deeply, profoundly, "greatly
annoyed" the religious establishment15. That’s not a mild irritation, church; the Greek word
there means they were viscerally vexed16. Why? Because these uncredentialed fishermen
were "proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection from the dead"17. This message was a direct
assault on the Sadducees' theology that denied any resurrection, and it was a political threat
to their power18181818. The gospel always upsets a comfortable, man-made equilibrium. So what
do they do? They arrest Peter and John. Not for a crime, but for a healing. For daring to act in
the name of Jesus.
And yet, while the messengers are being locked up, the message breaks free. Luke tells us
that "many of those who had heard the word believed; and the number of the men came to be
about five thousand"19. The first attempt to stop the gospel only caused it to explode.

2. Analyzing the Discrepancy: Fishermen vs. The Religious Elite (Acts 4:5-13)

The next day, the scene is set for maximum intimidation. Peter and John are brought before
the Sanhedrin, the Jewish Supreme Court20. And look who’s there: Annas and Caiaphas, the
high-priestly dynasty that had orchestrated the death of Jesus just weeks before21. The most
powerful, educated, and connected men in the nation are all there.
And who do they place in the middle? Peter and John. Fishermen. Small business owners. The
council saw them as "uneducated, common men"22. They had no degrees, no titles, no
religious credentials.
And the council asks the central question: "By what
power or by what name did you do this?"23. They're not asking
how it happened; the evidence was right there in front of them. They were asking about
authority. They were asking, "Who gave you the right?"

3. Disclosing the Clue to the Resolution: "Better to Obey God" (Acts 4:8-22)

And here, in the face of all that earthly power, we see the source of the Church's unstoppable
inertia. The clue to the resolution is in verse eight: "Then Peter,
filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them..."24.
Brother, sister, this is our Pentecostal truth! This isn't just Peter mustering up his own courage.
This is a fresh filling for the specific task25. This is the Holy Ghost anointing him for the
assignment. And out of that anointing comes
parrhesia—a Spirit-given boldness, a freedom to speak the truth frankly and fearlessly26.
Peter doesn't cower. He doesn't apologize. He goes on the offense, preaching the name of the
very one they crucified and God raised from the dead27. He tells them that the Cornerstone
they, the builders, rejected has become the only name under heaven by which we must be
saved28.
The council is stunned. They see the boldness. They see the healed man. They see the
undeniable evidence. And they come to a profound conclusion: "they recognized that they
had been with Jesus"29. Proximity to Jesus, empowered by the Spirit, is an authority that
trumps any earthly credential.
Trapped, they resort to the only tool a weak authority has: threats. They issue a gag order:
"speak no more to anyone in this name"30.
And that’s when Peter and John give the answer that must define our lives: "
Which is right in the sight of God: to listen to you, or to him? You be the judges! As for
us, we cannot stop speaking about what we have seen and heard"31. Our ultimate
allegiance is to God.

4. Experiencing the Gospel: A Shaking, A Sharing, A Spreading (Acts 4:23-37)

So what happens when the Church lives out that allegiance? The rest of the chapter is our
playbook.
First, when they are threatened, they don't scatter; they gather. They go straight "to their own
people"32. Community is our first refuge.
Second, they pray. With one accord, they lift their voices to God33. And what a prayer! They
don't start with their problem; they start with God's power: "Sovereign Lord, you made the
heavens and the earth..."34. They see their crisis through the lens of Scripture35. And then
comes the most radical part: they don't pray for the threats to go away. They don't pray for
safety. They pray for
more boldness to keep doing the very thing that got them in trouble!36.
And God answers. The place where they were meeting was
shaken37. They were
all filled with the Holy Spirit—not just the apostles, but the whole church got a fresh
outpouring38. And they went out and "spoke the word of God with boldness"39.
The result? The church was of "one heart and one soul," sharing everything they had, so that
there wasn't a needy person among them40404040. The experience of facing opposition and
being filled by the Spirit made them not only a bold church, but a beautiful, generous, loving
family.

Conclusion: Anticipating the Consequences

Church, let's bring this home. We look at the world around us and we can get discouraged.
Just recently in Scotland, their parliament passed a bill creating "safe access zones" around
abortion clinics. Within that space, it's now a criminal offense to do anything that might

influence a person's decision—and that includes praying where you might be seen or heard.
We hear that and we think, "Well, that's over there." But I'm telling you, church, we are always
just one election, one court decision, one cultural shift away from facing our own version of
that right here.
When and if that day comes, what will our response be? When our faith is deemed a public
nuisance, when our prayers are called harassment, will we have the apostolic grit of Peter and
John? When we gather in this sanctuary, what will be our prayer? Will we pray for escape?
"Lord, get us out of this! Make them stop!" Or will we have the faith to pray like the early
church prayed? "Lord, look upon their threats, and grant to Your servants to speak Your Word
with even more boldness!"
But that’s a "what if." Let's talk about the "right now."
What about the person you work with who mocks your faith every time you mention church?
What about the family member at the cookout who tells you to keep your religion to yourself?
What about the pressure at your job to cut a corner, to fudge a number, to participate in
unethical practices and just keep quiet about it?
That is your Sanhedrin. That is your moment of opposition.
Is the Holy Spirit just for a good church service? Is His power only for feeling a chill during a
worship song? Or is the same Spirit who gave parrhesia to Peter in the courtroom available for
you in the breakroom? Is the anointing that shook that prayer meeting in Jerusalem available
to shake up your fear on a Tuesday afternoon? The Bible says YES! The Holy Spirit is not a
weekend luxury; He is our daily, empowering presence for the mission!
(Altar Call)
With every head bowed and every eye closed, I want to ask you, where do you stand today?
Maybe you're here and you've never personally met the One they were willing to risk it all for.
You’ve been trying to face the pressures of this world in your own power, on sinking sand. I
want to tell you, the Rock they stood on—the Rock that was struck for you, Jesus Christ—is
calling you today. He wants to be your foundation, the source of a life that can never be truly
shaken. If that’s you, I want you to come to this altar. Don't leave here without Him.
Maybe you're a Christian, but if you're honest, you've been silent. Fear has put a gag order on
your faith. Intimidation from the world has quieted your witness. You've been praying for an
escape route when God is waiting for you to ask for boldness. Today is your day to come and
ask for a fresh filling of the Holy Spirit for your assignment. To pray like they prayed: "Lord, I'm
scared, but see their threats, and grant Your servant boldness!" If you need that Holy Ghost
fire, that unstoppable inertia in your soul, you come.

And maybe you're in the fight right now. You’re facing the opposition, you’re standing firm, but
you're weary. You feel isolated. Remember the first thing Peter and John did? They went to
"their own people." The answer for your weariness isn't retreat; it's community. This altar is a
place to find your people, to draw strength from your brothers and sisters, to be prayed over,
and to be reminded that you are not in this fight alone.
Whatever your need, the Spirit of God is moving in this place. The world and its threats could
not stop the early church, and nothing should stop you from coming to meet with the God
who makes us unstoppable. Come now. This altar is open. Come and drink freely from the
Rock. Let's pray together.
