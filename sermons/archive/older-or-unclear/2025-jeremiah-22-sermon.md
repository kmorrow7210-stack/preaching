Jeremiah 22:24-40

You’ve seen that clip of Jamal Adams walking up to the Jets facility, grabbing the handle like he
belongs there… and the door won’t open. He pulls again. Nothing. He looks around like, ‘Wait…
did I just get cut?’”
“Now listen, it’s funny when it’s an NFL player and it’s probably a busted badge or a locked door.
But the reason it went viral is because we all know that feeling: I thought I had access. I thought
I was still in. I thought I still belonged.”
“And that is exactly the fear Jeremiah drops on Judah.”
“God made David a promise: ‘Your throne will be established forever.’ We build our whole hope
on that. Then Josiah dies, the nation gets shoved around by empires, and a puppet king sits in
David’s chair.”
“And then Jeremiah looks at the royal line and basically says: the badge isn’t working anymore.
God says to Jeconiah, ‘Even if you were a signet ring on my right hand, I would pull you off.’
That’s access revoked. That’s authorization removed. That’s the moment the promise starts to
feel like it just got locked behind glass.”
“Here’s the question: if the King lost the ring, and the door won’t open… what happens to the
promise?”

I.

Upsetting the Equilibrium

We all know the promise. We know the hope that anchors our faith. God told David, 'Your house
and your kingdom will endure forever before me; your throne will be established forever.' That is
the bedrock of our theology. It implies sovereignty. It implies safety. It implies that no matter how
dark the world gets, God has a man on the throne and a plan in place.
But have you ever lived through a season where the Promise felt like a lie?
Let me take you to the streets of Jerusalem around 600 BC. The Golden Age is gone. Good
King Josiah—our last best hope—is dead, killed not by glory, but by a stray arrow. The political
situation is in freefall.
We have a king, Jehoiakim, but he’s not a sovereign; he’s a puppet. He was installed by Egypt.
He answers to Pharaoh, not Yahweh. And to pay his dues to his foreign master, he taxes you.
But he doesn't stop there. While the economy crashes, he decides it's the perfect time to
renovate the palace. He builds spacious upper rooms, cuts out windows, and panels them with
cedar. And who builds it? You do. For free. Forced labor while the nation starves.

You look at the palace and ask, 'Where is the Promise of David now?'
And then, the prophet Jeremiah shows up. And what he says doesn't fix the problem—it seems
to destroy our last shred of hope. He looks at the royal line, specifically the son, Jeconiah, and
God speaks:
'As surely as I live,' declares the Lord, 'even if you, Jehoiachin son of Jehoiakim king of
Judah, were a signet ring on my right hand, I would still pull you off.'
Do you hear that? The Signet Ring is the symbol of authority. It is the signature of the King. And
God says, 'I’m taking it off. I’m throwing it away.'
If the ring is removed... then the line is broken. If the line is broken... then the Promise is void.
And if the Promise is void... where does that leave us?

II.

Analyzing the Discrepancy: The Loss of Agency

We need to pause and look at that ring. We can't just gloss over the metaphor.
In the ancient world, the Signet Ring wasn't jewelry. It wasn't a wedding band. It was a tool of
administration. It was the King’s signature.
● If a law needed to be passed, the King pressed his ring into the wax.
● If a treasury needed to be opened, the ring broke the seal.
● If a general needed orders to march, the ring authenticated the command.
Whoever held the ring held the Power of Attorney for the Kingdom.
So when God says to the King of Judah, 'I am pulling you off my hand,' He isn't just firing an
employee. He is revoking the power of attorney. He is canceling the credit card. He is saying,
'You no longer have the authority to act on My behalf.'
And here is the discrepancy that crushes us: We thought the 'Throne of David' was a
permanent fixture—like a mountain that couldn't be moved. But Jeremiah is showing us that the
Throne wasn't just a chair; it was a function. It was the vehicle of God's rule on earth.
When that ring comes off, two devastating things happen at once:
1. We lose our Sovereignty. To lose the ring means we are no longer our own people. If our
King doesn't have God's authority, then he is just a man in a fancy hat waiting to be conquered
by a stronger man. And that is exactly what happened. Nebuchadnezzar didn't just defeat an
army; he filled the vacuum where God's authority used to be. We went from being the 'People of
God' to being the 'Subjects of Babylon.'
2. The Throne becomes a Museum Piece. This is the hardest part to swallow. God didn't say,
'I'm giving the ring to your uncle.' He didn't say, 'I'm giving it to a better candidate.' He took it off.

He put it in His pocket. For the next 600 years, there is no King in Jerusalem. There are
governors. There are puppets. There are tetrarchs. But the Throne? The Throne is empty.
So here we sit. The promise of 'Forever' has collided with the reality of 'Never Again.' The ring is
gone. The throne is empty. And we are left wondering... if God took the ring back, how can He
ever keep His promise?

III.

Disclosing the Clue to Resolution: The Shoot from the Stump

So, the words of Jeremiah didn't just hang in the air; they crashed into the earth.
Babylon didn't just knock; they kicked the door down. The prophecy about Jehoiakim? Fulfilled.
He didn't get a royal burial; he was dragged off and tossed out like refuse, just as God said. And
Jeconiah? The man who wore the signet ring? He was stripped of his crown, shackled in
bronze, and marched 900 miles to Babylon.
The throne is empty. And for 70 years, the silence is deafening. The people settle into the
despair of exile. They assume the story is over.
But history is God's canvas, and He isn't finished painting. Just as the people accept their fate,
the geopolitical map changes overnight. Babylon—the unshakeable empire—falls to Persia. And
King Cyrus does something unprecedented. He doesn't want captives; he wants loyal subjects
in their own lands. He issues a decree: 'Go home.'
Suddenly, the road to Jerusalem is open. And look who is leading the way.
It’s not just a random administrator. Alongside Nehemiah, who would come to rebuild the walls,
stands a man named Zerubbabel. He is appointed as Governor. He is the grandson of
Jeconiah.
The grandson of the man who lost the ring is now standing in the rubble of the Temple, holding
the blueprints for God’s house.
He isn't a King—he’s a governor under Persian rule. He doesn't sit on a throne—he stands on a
construction site. But he is there. The tree was cut down, yes. But there is a shoot coming out of
the stump. God has brought the seed of David back to the City of David.
And the question hanging in the air is: Has the favor returned with him? Or is he just a governor
of ruins?

IV.

Experiencing the Gospel (The Resolution)

Imagine being Zerubbabel. You are the grandson of the king who lost everything. You grew up
hearing the story: 'We used to be the Signet Ring. We used to have authority. But God took it
away.' Now, you are standing in Jerusalem, but you aren't a king. You are a mid-level manager

for the Persian Empire. You look at the rubble of the Temple, and you feel the weight of that
generational curse. You feel like an impostor.
But then, an old man named Haggai clears his throat. He stands up in the middle of the
construction site—dust on his robes, fire in his eyes—and he says, 'Zerubbabel, look at me.
God has a message for you.'
And the words he speaks echo back through the corridors of history, all the way to that terrible
day in Jeremiah 22. But this time, the message is different.
Listen to the Word of the Lord from Haggai 2:23: 'On that day, declares the LORD Almighty, I
will take you, my servant Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel,' declares the LORD, 'and I will make you
like my signet ring, for I have chosen you,' declares the LORD Almighty.'
Do you hear the thunder in that verse? God is looking at the grandson of the rejected king and
saying: 'The Ring is back.' 'The Authority is returned.' 'The Line is not broken.'
In one sentence, God reverses the curse. He takes the Power of Attorney that was stripped
from the grandfather and places it firmly on the finger of the grandson. He says, 'I haven't
abandoned the House of David. I am still writing this story.'
But here is the mystery... Zerubbabel never became a king. He died a governor. The Persians
remained in charge. So, was God lying? No. Because Zerubbabel wasn't the end of the story;
he was the bridge. He was the vessel God used to carry the Promise across the gap of the
Exile. He held the ring so he could pass it down. To his son... and his son's son... generation
after generation...

V.

Anticipating the Consequences

But I want you to look closely at the text. Don't miss the specific grammar of grace. God says: 'I
will make you LIKE a signet ring.'
He doesn't say, 'Zerubbabel, you are the Messiah.' He doesn't say, 'The Kingdom is fully here.'
He says, 'You are like the ring.'
Why does that matter? Because if you look around Zerubbabel’s Jerusalem, it doesn't look like
the Kingdom of God. There are still enemies. There is still poverty. They are still under the
thumb of Persia. It feels a lot like our world—we have the promise of God, but we still deal with
cancer, corruption, and death.
This is the 'Already/Not Yet.' Zerubbabel was the Down Payment. He was the proof that God
was back in the business of David’s line. It’s like the Garden of Eden. In Genesis 3, we were
kicked out. In Haggai, the people are allowed back into the 'Garden' (the Land), but the Serpent
is still crawling around and the Tree of Life isn't blooming yet. The curse is reversed legally, but
it hasn't been removed totally.

God is saying to Zerubbabel: 'You are the assurance that the True King is coming. You are the
shadow; He will be the substance.'"

Application: Living in the "Not Yet"
And this is where the rubber meets the road for us in 2025.
We are living in the same tension as Zerubbabel. We look back and see that the True Signet
Ring has come. Jesus Christ lived, died, and rose again. He proved He has the authority. He
showed us what the Kingdom looks like—blind eyes opening, sinners forgiven, the dead raised.
But... we look around, and we still see the rubble. We still feel the brokenness of the world. We
are 'Like' people of the Kingdom, but we aren't fully home yet.
So what do we do while we wait? We do exactly what Zerubbabel did. He didn't sit down and
cry because the Kingdom wasn't fully finished. He built the Temple. He laid the foundation. He
did the work God gave him to do in the middle of the imperfection.
Because we know the King has the Ring, we can build with confidence today.
● 1. We can build our families. "And I know, some of you are looking at your families and
it feels like you are staring at a construction site that has been shut down. You see
broken communication, you see wayward children, you see a culture that is trying to tear
your home apart. But because the King has the Ring, you don't have to parent out of
fear. You parent out of authority. You are the watchman on the wall. When you pray over
your children at night, you aren't just wishing on a star; you are stamping the King's seal
over their lives. You are saying, 'This house belongs to the Lord.' The world may be
chaotic, but this home is under the jurisdiction of the Signet Ring."
● 2. We can serve our neighbors. "In a world that is obsessed with 'paneled
houses'—with building our own little kingdoms and keeping people out—we have the
confidence to open the doors. Why? Because we aren't just neighbors; we are
Ambassadors. When you serve the person across the street, when you love the
unlovable, you are acting as an authorized agent of Jesus Christ. You are bringing a
taste of the Kingdom into the 'Not Yet.' You are showing them what the King looks like
before they even meet Him."
● 3. We can endure hardship. "This is the hardest one. Zerubbabel had to build amidst
the rubble. You might have to build amidst a diagnosis. You might have to build through
grief. You might have to build when the bank account is empty. But we endure... not
because we are stubborn, but because we are secure. If the Ring is on Jesus' hand,
then your hardship is not the end of the story. It’s just a chapter. You can stand in the
middle of the storm and say, 'My King holds the seal. He has signed my future. And what
He has signed, no storm can wash away.'

Not because we can fix everything, but because we know the One who is the Signet Ring is
coming back to finish what He started. He came once to wear the Ring of Suffering. He is
coming back to wear the Ring of Glory.
And until that day, we hold on to the promise: The Equilibrium has been reset. The King is on
the throne. And the best is yet to come.

The Altar Call: Standing in the Rubble
We’ve done a lot of thinking today. We’ve looked at kings, and curses, and genealogies. But I
want to stop thinking for a moment and start feeling the reality of this.
I am speaking to everyone in this room who feels like they are living in the 'Not Yet.'
Maybe you connected with Zerubbabel today. You are a believer. You love God. But you are
standing in the middle of rubble.
● Maybe it’s the rubble of a marriage that isn't getting better.
● Maybe it’s the rubble of a career that feels stalled.
● Maybe it’s a spiritual dryness where you remember the 'glory days' of your faith, but right
now, you just feel tired.
And the enemy has been whispering a lie to you. He’s been whispering the curse of Jeconiah.
He says, 'God has pulled the ring off your life. You’ve messed up too much. You’ve been
disqualified. God is done using you.'
If you are hearing that voice today, I need you to hear the voice of Haggai even louder.
The King is in the room.
Jesus Christ, the True Signet Ring, the Authority of God, is here. And He is looking at your
rubble, He is looking at your imperfection, and He is saying what He said to Zerubbabel: 'I have
chosen you.'
I want to open these altars for three specific groups of people:
1. The Disqualified. You feel like your past has canceled your future. You feel like the ring was
taken off a long time ago because of a mistake you made. You need to come down here and let
Jesus reverse that curse. You need to hear Him say, 'My grace is greater than your history.'
2. The Tired Builders. You are doing the work. You are serving, you are giving, you are trying
to build a godly family in an ungodly world—but you are exhausted. You are living in the 'Not Yet'
and you are ready to quit. You need to come receive a fresh sealing of the Holy Spirit. You need
the strength that doesn't come from your grit, but from His authority.

3. The Unmarked. Maybe you realized today that you don't have the Ring. You’ve never
surrendered to the King. You don't have the seal of the Holy Spirit on your life. Today is the day
you stop being an orphan and become a son or daughter.
Here is the call: I’m going to ask you to move. If you are tired of looking at the rubble and you
want to look at the Ring... If you need to trade your anxiety for His authority... I want you to step
out of your seat right now.
Don't wait for the situation to get perfect. Zerubbabel worshiped while the temple was still
unfinished. Come worship in your rubble. Come claim the authority of the Signet Ring over your
life.
These altars are open. You come.
