## MOVE 1 - Opening (2-3 min)

  
- Good to see y'all. Week two in our walk through Samuel.
    
- Last week we knelt at Shiloh with a woman named Hannah. She came in wrecked. Prayed so hard the old priest figured she'd been into the wine. And God heard the prayer nobody else in the building heard.
    
- [self-deprecating beat] I'll be honest, I relate more to Eli than I'd like to admit some mornings. I can walk past holy and not even feel my pulse change.
    
- This week we're back at the same address. Same Shiloh. Same altar. Same old priest. Different world inside that tent.
    

  

Invocational prayer [hand it over]

  

- Lord, I thank You for Your living Word. I ask You this morning, anoint me, God. Let me get out of the way. Season my words and let them be Your words. Give us the fear of the Lord again. In Jesus' name, and the church said Amen.

## MOVE 2 - The Hook: numbness, not scandal (3-5 min)

[the felt crisis: holy things going common in the hands of the people who handle them most]

  

- Let me tell on myself. I have held these communion trays so many times I could do it in my sleep. And there was a Sunday, I won't tell you which one, where I stood right here holding the body and the blood of the Lord Jesus, and I was thinking about where we were going to eat after church. [pause] The holiest thing in the room was in my hands, and my heart had it filed under routine.
    
- That is the danger this whole passage is about. It is not mainly scandal. It is numbness. When you handle holy things long enough, they can start to feel common.
    
- [the bridge from last week, say it slow] Last week the prayer nobody heard was heard by God. This week the sin everybody tolerated was also seen by God. Same God. Same building. He sees both.
    
- Here is where we are going this morning. When holy things become common, when they become tools for selfish men, God sees, God judges, and God raises up faithful servants in hidden places.
    

  

[Alternate hook if the personal one feels too raw on a given Sunday: the museum guard who walks past a priceless painting forty times a day until it is just a thing on a wall. Familiarity is the thief. You can stand closest to the holy and feel it least.]

## MOVE 3 - Re-narrate the text (10-15 min)

[Read 1 Samuel 2:12 aloud to open. Then re-tell as human drama. Voice the characters. Keep the camera cutting between the corruption and the boy, the way the text does.]

### I. The sons who served themselves (vv. 12-17, 22-25)

- Verse 12 lands like a slap. "Now the sons of Eli were worthless men. They did not know the Lord." The old word there is "sons of Belial." It means worthlessness, ruin, men who are good for nothing.
    
- And catch the brutal part. They did not know the Lord, and they are priests. They know the system. They know the rituals, the vocabulary, the motions. They can run the whole service blindfolded. They just don't know Him.
    
- [re-narrate, present tense] Picture a farmer climbing the hill to Shiloh with his best lamb over his shoulders. He has come to make peace with God. He knows the rule. The fat belongs to the Lord. It gets burned on the altar first, the richest part, given to God before a single person eats. That is Leviticus 3 and 7. God gets the best, and He gets it first.
    
- Here comes the priest's servant with a three-pronged fork. While the meat is still boiling he jabs that fork down in the pot, and whatever it brings up, the priest keeps. [build] Then it gets worse. The servant says, "Don't boil it. Give it to me raw, now, before the fat is even burned." The worshiper says, "At least let them burn the fat for the Lord first." And the servant says, "No. You give it now, or I will take it by force."
    
- They are not waiting on God to get His portion. God gets the leftovers, if there are any. The verdict, verse 17: "the men treated the offering of the Lord with contempt."
    
- [Hebrew color, deliver it conversationally] Later in the oracle God uses a rare word, verse 29, "kick at my sacrifice." Same word shows up one other place, Deuteronomy 32, "Jeshurun grew fat and kicked." They got fat on the very thing that belonged to God.
    
- And verse 22, it is not only the offering. They lay with the women serving at the entrance to the tent of meeting. They turned the house of God into a place where the vulnerable get used. The light is going out, and it is the men holding the lamp who are putting it out.
    

### II. The father who corrected but would not restrain (vv. 22-25, 27-34)

- Now meet Eli. He is very old, and he hears all of it. He even says the right words. "Why do you do such things? It is no good report I am hearing."
    
- He gives them good theology, verse 25. If you sin against a man, somebody can step in and mediate. But if you sin against the Lord, who is going to intercede for you?
    
- But that is all he does. He talks. He never acts. "They would not listen to the voice of their father, for it was the will of the Lord to put them to death."
    
- [slow, direct, pastoral warning] Eli saw enough to speak, but not enough to act. Church, that is a heavy word for anybody who carries authority. A parent. A boss. A coach. A pastor. Naming the evil out loud is not the same thing as restraining it. You can be right and still be guilty if all you ever do is talk.
    
- Then the man of God shows up, verses 27 through 34, and reads the indictment. God starts with grace. I chose your family back in Egypt. I gave you the offerings. Then the charge: "You honor your sons above me, fattening yourselves on the choicest parts." Then the line the whole sermon turns on, verse 30: "Those who honor me I will honor, and those who despise me shall be lightly esteemed."
    
- The sign: both sons die on the same day. That happens, exactly, in chapter 4. And the poetic justice is brutal. The men who grabbed the choice cuts off the altar end up with their family begging for a piece of bread.
    

### III. The child God was raising in the background (vv. 18-21, 26, 35)

- Now watch how the story is built. Every time the camera shows you the corruption, it cuts back to a boy. [callback to the camera image]
    
- Verse 18: "Samuel was ministering before the Lord, a boy clothed with a linen ephod." A child in priest's clothes, while grown men in priest's clothes despise the office.
    
- Verse 19: Hannah makes him a little robe every year and carries it up to him. A mama who gave her son away to God is still stitching faithfulness into him one robe at a time.
    
- Verses 21 and 26: "the boy Samuel grew in the presence of the Lord," and "grew in stature and in favor with the Lord and also with man." [note for yourself: that is the exact line Luke 2:52 uses about Jesus]
    
- So here is the contrast in one sentence. Hophni and Phinehas are grown men with titles and no reverence. Samuel is a child with no power who is growing before the Lord.
    
- And then the promise, verse 35: "I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who shall do according to what is in my heart and in my mind. And I will build him a sure house, and he shall go in and out before my anointed forever."

## MOVE 4 - The reveal: the faithful Priest, and the pattern (5-8 min)

[the payoff. Name the typology out loud.]

  

- That promise of a faithful priest runs on three tracks at once.
    
- Right now: it is Samuel. The boy in the linen ephod becomes the prophet who anoints kings.
    
- Down the road: it is Zadok. Generations later Solomon removes Abiathar, from Eli's line, and puts Zadok in his place. First Kings 2 says plainly it was "to fulfill the word of the Lord that he had spoken concerning the house of Eli." God's word came true on schedule.
    
- All the way down: it is Jesus. [build] Hebrews calls Him our great High Priest. He is the one Priest who never treated the Father's things with contempt. He never grabbed the offering. He never took it by force. He became the offering. He honored the Father perfectly, and the Father honored Him perfectly. He built the sure house, His church, and the light in that house never goes out.
    
- [broaden with the pattern, let it build] And once you see this, you see it everywhere. When one thing is dying under judgment, God is already growing the future somewhere small, hidden, weak, or overlooked.
    

- The old world is drowning, and new creation is floating in an ark.
    
- The nations scatter at Babel, and a whole new people starts in a barren tent with Abraham and Sarah.
    
- The family is falling apart through betrayal, and God is planting Joseph in Egypt to keep them alive.
    
- Pharaoh is killing the baby boys, and the deliverer is floating down the river in a basket.
    
- Naomi's house looks dead, and David's line is being born through Ruth.
    
- Saul's crown is rotting, and a shepherd boy is out in a field in Bethlehem.
    
- The temple is burning, and a remnant is carrying a promise into exile.
    
- Herod's temple is still standing, and the real temple is walking around Galilee in flesh and blood.
    
- The cross looks like the end of everything, and God is growing new creation right out of a tomb.
    

- Same pattern at Shiloh. The lamp is going out in one house. God is already tending it in another. The renewal is wearing a little linen ephod, and nobody important even notices.

## MOVE 5 - Altar call (3-5 min)

[direct, personal, repetitive. Bring the numbness home before you bring the hope.]

  

- Hear me. This is not just a sermon about bad leaders far away. The deeper word this morning is the numbness, and it can get on any of us.
    
- So let me ask you. Where have holy things become common to you? Where did worship turn into routine? Where did the Bible become a textbook you skim? Where did prayer become a habit you do with your eyes half open? Where did the people God put in your care become a job, a chore, a problem to manage?
    
- [build the rhythm] Come put the ephod back on this morning. Come back into the presence. Come until the holy feels holy again. Come until the fear of the Lord comes back. Come until the lamp burns bright in your house again.
    
- And if you have been Eli, if you have seen it and only talked about it, come. It is not enough to name it. Ask God for the backbone to act.
    
- Here is the hope. You are not the corrupt house unless you choose to be. And even if the old thing in your life is already dying, God is already growing the new thing in a hidden place. There is a faithful Priest. His name is Jesus. He never grabbed, He gave. And in Him the light never, ever goes out.
    
- [open the altar] This altar is open. Come. Come and let the holy be holy again. Come and meet the Priest who will not fail you.
    

  

Closing prayer [pray it over them, return to the lamp image]

  

- Lord, light the lamp again in this house, in these hearts. Burn the numbness out of us. Give us the linen ephod, the quiet, faithful, submitted heart that grows in Your presence. We honor You this morning, so honor us, Lord. In Jesus' name, and the church said Amen.
    
- [dismiss] Shake some hands, hug some necks.
