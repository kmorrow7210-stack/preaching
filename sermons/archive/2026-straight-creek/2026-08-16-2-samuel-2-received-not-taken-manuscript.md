## **The first man is David**

And let's be honest about where David is when this chapter opens, because it matters.

David is in Ziklag. Ziklag is a Philistine town. It was handed to him by Achish, the king of Gath. First Samuel 27 tells us David lived in Philistine country a year and four months.

That's where the man is standing when chapter 2 begins.

And verse 1 says David inquired of the Lord.

That word is *sha'al*. It just means to ask. And it's the same word from chapter 28, when Saul inquired of the Lord and the Lord answered him not, neither by dreams nor by Urim nor by prophets.

Saul asked, and heaven was quiet.

David asks, and God gives him two words.

Go up.

David says, where?

God says, Hebron.

Now Hebron is not a random dot on a map. Hebron is Abraham's town. Abraham is buried there. So is Sarah. So is Isaac and Rebekah and Leah and Jacob. It sits up in the Judean hill country, about nineteen miles south of Jerusalem, three thousand feet above the sea. When God says go up, he means go up.

And David doesn't take a raiding party. Verse 2, he takes his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail. Verse 3, he takes the six hundred men that are with him and every one of their families, and they settle in Hebron and in the towns around Hebron.

That's not a camp. That is a government moving in.

And verse 4\. The men of Judah came to Hebron, and there they anointed David king over the house of Judah.

Put your finger on that word came. They traveled. They showed up. Nobody marched them there.

## **The second man is Abner**

Abner son of Ner. Commander of Saul's army, and Saul's own cousin. When everything else in that government died on Gilboa, Abner walked off that mountain alive.

Verse 8\. Abner took Ish-bosheth the son of Saul, and brought him over to Mahanaim, and made him king.

Look at the three verbs. Took him. Brought him. Made him.

Ish-bosheth is forty years old. He is a grown man. And in this entire chapter he does not do one thing and he does not say one word. Abner is the subject of every verb and Ish-bosheth is the object of every verb.

Abner took a man and put him in a chair.

And where did he put him? Mahanaim. And Mahanaim is not Gibeah. Gibeah was Saul's capital. Mahanaim is on the far side of the Jordan River. Abner crossed the water. He set up a government on the other side of the river and called it the kingdom of Israel.

And verse 9, he claims all of it. Gilead. The Ashurites. Jezreel. Ephraim. Benjamin. And all Israel.

He claims the whole country while sitting across a river from the whole country.

\[slow, still at the pulpit\]

Now let me give you two Hebrew words and I'll make them plain.

Judah anointed David. That word is *mashach*. It means somebody poured oil on a man's head.

Abner made Ish-bosheth king. That's the word *malak*, in a form that means he caused him to reign. It means somebody put a man in a chair.

And here is what I want you to see, and I want to get this exactly right.

Ish-bosheth is never anointed. Not in this chapter. Not in the next one. Not anywhere in this book. Nobody ever poured oil on that man's head.

Judah came to Hebron.

Nobody ever came to Mahanaim.

\[land it\]

So count what Israel has now.

One people. Same covenant. Same God. Same twelve tribes. Same blood. They are still one people.

Two thrones. One in Hebron, down in the south. One at Mahanaim, across the river to the east. And notice, neither one of them is sitting in the middle of the country. The middle is where the Philistines are.

And no common allegiance. There is not a man in that nation who can tell you who the king of Israel is. It depends entirely on which side of the map he woke up on.

Joshua chapter 20\. God tells Joshua to set apart certain cities in Israel, and He names them, and Hebron is one of six in the entire nation.

## **Reference check for Movement Two**

* Zeruiah is David's sister, 1 Chr 2:16. Joab, Abishai, Asahel, 2 Sam 2:18.  
* Joab swears "as God lives" at 2:27, and he says a pious line before the Ammonites at 10:12. He never once inquires of the Lord. That's why I wrote it as "he'll swear by him but he never asks him," not "he never mentions God."  
* *sachaq* at 2:14. Same root as Isaac. Also the word for Michal's contempt at 6:21 and Samson's "entertainment" in Judges 16:25, if you want it.  
* Joab's reply at 2:14 is literally one Hebrew word, *yaqumu*. That's safe to say.  
* *rea'* at 2:16. "Neighbor," "companion." Solid.  
* Helkath-hazzurim, 2:16. Best rendering is field of daggers or field of sharp edges. Etymology is contested, so don't build past "they named the ground."  
* Asahel: gazelle 2:18, the two warnings 2:20–22, the spear butt 2:23, the army stopping 2:23.  
* I dropped the mileage from Gibeon to Hebron on purpose. It's farther than the nineteen miles you'll say for Jerusalem to Hebron and it's easy to fumble live.  
* Hebron a city of refuge, Josh 20:7 and 21:11–13. Six cities total, Josh 20:7–8. The manslayer stands "at the entrance of the gate," Josh 20:4. That verse is the whole movement.  
* Abner's twenty men and the feast, 2 Sam 3:20. "In peace" three times, 3:21, 22, 23\. "David did not know it," 3:26. The gate murder, 3:27.  
* "Avenging in time of peace blood that had been shed in war," 1 Kings 2:5. This is the strongest legal card you have against Joab and it comes out of David's own mouth.  
* Saul's spear at David, 1 Sam 18:10–11 and 19:9–10. Nob, 1 Sam 22:18. Eighty-five is the Hebrew number; Greek says 305\. Don't get pinned to it.  
* Ish-bosheth's hands go limp, 2 Sam 4:1. All Israel at Hebron, 5:1–3.

## **Two things I held back on purpose**

1. **David makes Joab a pallbearer.** 2 Sam 3:31, David tells Joab and all the people to tear their clothes, put on sackcloth, and mourn before Abner. The king orders the murderer to walk in the funeral. It is a devastating detail and it belongs in Movement Three, where the innocence question lands. Don't spend it here.  
2. **Chronicles cleans Joab up.** Written centuries later, Chronicles never tells you Joab killed Abner or Amasa, and it credits him with rebuilding Jerusalem instead. It's a real "did you know" and it's true. But it will raise a question about Scripture in somebody's mind that you then have to stop and answer, and this movement can't afford the detour. Save it for Substack or the YouTube cut.
