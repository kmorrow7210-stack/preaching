# **Nevertheless**

## **2 Samuel 5:4-10** 

### **Straight Creek Church of God**

Read 2 Samuel 5:4-10,KJV

**5** In Hebron he reigned over Judah seven years and six months: and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty and three years over all Israel and Judah.

**8** And David said on that day, Whosoever getteth up to the gutter, and smiteth the Jebusites, and the lame and the blind that are hated of David's soul, he shall be chief and captain. Wherefore they said, The blind and the lame shall not come into the house.

**9** So David dwelt in the fort, and called it the city of David. And David built round about from Millo and inward.

**10** And David went on, and grew great, and the Lord God of hosts was with him.

## **HOOK**

1790. The capital’s in New York, and has been there since 1785\. Used to be in Philadelphia and they are pushing hard to get it back. Largest city. Most developed. More affluent. Better everything. The South didn’t want that. They didn’t want the North having control of the capital.

*Alexander Hamilton*, Secretary of the Treasury, wants the country to have credit. So he wants the federal government to take the war debts from the states. If the nation carries the debt, the country has credit with the world, and the people with money tie themselves to the government, not just to their own state.

Stalemate. Two fights. They can’t decide where to put the capital. Hamilton can’t get the votes for his treasury plan.

*Jefferson has an idea*. He invites Madison and Hamilton to dinner, closes the door, and they come up with a plan. The capital won’t stay in New York. It’s not going back to Philadelphia. They move it to the **Potomac**, closer to Virginia, in a territory no single state can claim. *Hamilton, in exchange, gets the debts*.

Now you have a city on a river that belongs to no tribe.

We find a similar situation in **2 Samuel 5**.

## **TRANSITION**

There’s a city called *Jerusalem*. The people living there are Jebusites. Judah’s border. Benjamin’s land. Nobody kept it.

They've had run-ins with these people before:

* In **Joshua 10**, you have *Adoni-zedek*. He is the king of Jerusalem. He and four other kings are hiding in a cave. They are found and executed. The king is dead, but Judah still didn't take the city. Jerusalem's still standing.

* Later on, we were told in **Joshua 15** that when the land was allotted, Jerusalem is still unconquered. Scripture again reminds us that Judah can't drive the Jebusites out of Jerusalem, saying they live with Judah to this day.

* In **Judges 1**, you got this guy named *Adoni-bezek*. He's a ruler of Bezek, not king of Jerusalem. That was Adoni-zedek. They capture him, cut off his thumbs and big toes, which was the same punishment that he had put on 70 kings earlier. They bring him to Jerusalem, where he dies. Again, Judah attacks Jerusalem, takes it, puts it to the sword, and burns it. A few verses later, we are told Benjamin still cannot drive out the Jebusites. They continue living together in Jerusalem..

*They burned it. And they still cannot stay.* 

**Every time an Israelite looked up at the ridge of Jerusalem (***called Jebus in 2 Samuel 5*,**) they were reminded of what they couldn't do**. 

*For four centuries, Israel had tried everything*. 

* Joshua killed their king, but the walls stood.   
* Judah burned their streets, but the fortress rebuilt.   
* Benjamin settled on their borders. 

Generation after generation, Israel threw its best efforts against this mountain, only to retreat in defeat. Their feelings are summed up in ***Joshua 15: 'But we cannot drive them out.”***

## **THE BOAST & THE SHIELD**

Then **2 Samuel 5**. *David goes up against them*. 

What was the response? **You’re not getting in here**. The blind and the lame will keep you out. They don’t think he can take this hill.

This is pure boast …. with theatrics. They would shut the gates and put the blind and the disabled on the wall.

*Why would they do that?*

In the *ancient Near East*, you didn't just fight with swords. You fought with and for your gods. Soldiers would take an oath by parading a blind woman and a deaf man, declaring that anyone who attacked the city would be cursed by the gods to become blind and deaf.

The Jebusites are using their pagan practices. They are thrusting their most vulnerable citizens onto the front lines as a living hex. They are telling David: *If you breach these walls, the curse will fall on you. You will be struck blind. You will be crippled.*

They wanted to paralyze David. If he attacks the wall, he slaughters the marginalized. If he retreats, the stronghold wins.

This is why verse 8 says these defenders were "hated of David’s soul." David doesn’t hate the disabled—just a few chapters from now, he will seat Jonathan’s crippled son at his royal table forever. What David hates is the witchcraft. He hates the idolatry. He hates the grotesque reality of a regime that uses broken people as shields.

## **THE PIVOT: "NEVERTHELESS"**

For all they knew, they had good reason to say that. 

* The walls were 23 feet wide at their foundations.   
* Built of massive limestone boulders nearly 10 feet wide.   
* Each stone weighed two to three tons.

In the physical world, they had every right to say, *"You will never get in here."* This was a fortress built to survive the ages. Designed to keep out any threat. 

**Verse 7** ignores all that: **“Nevertheless, David took the stronghold of Zion...”**

We don’t get a multi-chapter description of a bloody siege. The narrator treats the "impregnable" stronghold like a paper curtain. One moment the door is cursed and locked. 

The next thing we know: **David took it**.

Jerusalem was a fortress, but every fortress has a weakness. 

Jerusalem's guy gets his water from a single water source, the Gihon Spring. It's a siphon-type spring. That means it doesn't flow steadily. It gushes periodically. 

The ancient Jebusite engineers created this system of underground downs and gates. Those walls also acted as downs, and they would artificially raise the water level and allow the excess to go into this trench they called the Canaanite channel. They used that to irrigate the valley.

This was a brilliant piece of engineering, but it was also the thermal exhaust port on the Death Star. So David understands these people are scared to death. He probably remembered how the men felt when Goliath was taunting. 

David said, 'Whoever takes this city, I'll make Commander-in-Chief.' Joab said, 'I'll do it.' Joab gets his squad together, and they slip down into the Kidron Valley. They drop to their knees and crawl through this aqueduct underneath the city's defense line. Now, they didn't pop out of a sewer grate swinging swords. They went to the heart of the system. The dams. 

The word is tsinnor. A channel. A pipe. The water.

Jerusalem was a fortress. But every fortress has a weakness.

That whole city drank from one source. The Gihon Spring. And the Gihon is a siphon spring, which means it doesn't flow steady. It gushes, and it stops, and it gushes again.

So the Jebusite engineers built a system for it. Underground dams and gates. Those dams raised the water level artificially and let the overflow run out into a trench they called the Canaanite channel, and they used that to irrigate the valley.

It was a brilliant piece of engineering. It was also the thermal exhaust port on the Death Star.

That city's life was never on top of that hill. It was underneath it. In the dark, where nobody performs.

And David understands that these people are scared to death. He's watched fear do this to men before. He was the boy in the valley when a giant stood out there taunting and grown soldiers stood still.

So he makes an offer. Whoever takes this city, I'll make him commander-in-chief.

Joab says I'll do it.

Here's how that probably went. Joab gets his squad together and they slip down into the Kidron Valley. They drop to their knees and crawl up that aqueduct, under the city's defense line, in the dark.

They didn't pop out of a sewer grate swinging swords. They went for the heart of the system. They blocked the dams and diverted the water out and away from the city.

Not one stone of that twenty-three foot wall was thrown down. And the impregnable stronghold is dry.

They had no walls left to fight for, because they had nothing left to drink.

They surrendered. They opened the gates. And David marched into Zion.

Nobody fought the wall. Nobody argued with the priest. Nobody answered the curse. They went under it.

**2 Samuel 5:7**. *Six words that took four hundred years*.

***But David took the stronghold of Zion.***

## **THE MOUNTAIN OF WORSHIP: ZION**

David gets in. The stronghold of Zion becomes the **City of David**.

No longer an isolated, foreign wedge. It belonged to no single tribe. Not Judah’s Hebron. Not Benjamin’s Gibeah. It becomes the neutral capital of a unified nation.

His royal house is built here. 

The Ark is brought here. 

The threshing floor purchased. 

The Temple was erected. 

For the next millennium, Jerusalem is **The Place**.

You want to draw near to God? Offer sacrifices? Pack up your life. 

Go **up** to the mountain.

A localized, fortified sanctuary. 

## **THE PORTABLE HUB: ANTIOCH**

1,000 years later… Israel has been through a LOT. The system is broken. And David’s greater Son arrives. Jesus.

He upends the entire system. 

On the cross, the temple curtain tears. 

By His Spirit, He turns **His people** into the portable, Temple of the living God.

A massive tectonic shift in the spiritual plane. 

*The early church starts in Jerusalem*. 

But the Spirit moves the hub.

By **Acts 11**, the mission flows primarily out of a place called **Antioch**.

Sprawling. Chaotic. Multi-ethnic. No temple. No holy mountain. 

It is a **shared table**. Jews and Gentiles. Slave and free. Breaking bread as family.

Here they are first called *“Christians” Acts 11*. 

The temple in Jerusalem is destroyed, and the Holy Spirit creates living, moving Temples. 

## **THE PAGAN IMPERIAL CITADEL: ROME**

The  reach doesn’t stop at Antioch. A Pharisee gets converted and God takes him on some journeys and he ends up in **Rome**.

Absolute center of the civilized world. Secure behind iron legions. Governed by Caesar. Huge pagan pantheon. Rome looks down on the Christians. And laughs.

For four hundred years, the Empire issues its own Jebusite decree: **“Your way will never get in here.”**

They enforce this with persecution. 

Martyrs in arenas. 

Enforced loyalty oaths. 

Rome’s message wast: *“We will break you. We will defile you. We will extinguish your name.”*

## **THE ULTIMATE "NEVERTHELESS"**

Then, the second great **“Nevertheless”** of history.

How did the early Christisans respond?  

No political coups. They didn't climb the imperial walls. 

No

They went through the catacombs.

They died in arenas.

They loved the poor. 

They loved each other. 

Cared for the sick during devastating plagues.

They chose to bleed rather than demanding that others bleed for them.

By **400 AD**

The city that placed believers in the center of the Colosseum and sent wild animals out to attack and devour is now the global center of the Christian faith. 

Temples of Jupiter have been repurposed and rededicated to a crucified Jewish carpenter whe defeated death itself through His resurrection. 

The church entered the unbreachable city anyway.

And from there, changed civilization. 

## **The Appeal**

**1\. Do not fear the strongholds of our culture.**

* In 2025, reports of child sex trafficking jumped 320 % – **But I still believe there’s a *nevertheless* ahead.**   
* In 2025, violent attacks and plots linked to extremist movements rose twenty-seven percent **– But I still believe there’s a *nevertheless* ahead.**   
* Over 1 million abortions in 2025, youth mental health crisis and the breakdown of the family unit continue to climb **– But I still believe there’s a *nevertheless* ahead.**   
* Even right now, A surrogate mother is being sued for $100,000 by the bios because she refused to abort a baby diagnosed with a treatable heart condition. **– But I still believe there’s a *nevertheless* ahead.** 

Secularism. Institutional greed. Political idolatry. They boast of permanent, unbreachable invincibility. 

But history full of impossible situations followed by God’s *“Nevertheless.”*

**2\. Refuse to build our own fortresses.**

We are never more like the Jebusites than when we try to secure our survival by weaponizing power. Shutting the doors of grace to the "unclean."

**3\. Embrace the Antioch way.**

We don't conquer modern Romes by throwing ourselves against Caesar’s walls. We do it through the quiet, radical, subterranean life of the gospel.

Because a thousand years after David, the true Son of David arrived at this exact same piece of real estate in Jerusalem.

He didn't ask the vulnerable to form a human chain around His throne. He didn't put the blind and the lame on the front lines to defend His reputation. He healed them in the temple courts.

And rather than staying safely inside the citadel while the people died for Him, Jesus allowed Himself to be dragged *outside* the city gates to absorb the artillery of the empire Himself.

Our King won the victory not by sitting on Caesar's throne, but by dying on Caesar's cross.
