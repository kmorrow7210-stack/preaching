# Shipwrecked and Snakebitten

## Acts 27:21-25

---

## Introduction

17th-century England.

There's this guy. Fought in the British Civil War. Gets out, finds a job repairing pots and pans. A tinker.

Then he gets saved. Joins a small church in Bedford. And as he grows in the Lord, he starts to preach.

People notice something. This guy can preach.

They come from miles around. Crowds grow. Lives change.

But while this is happening, King Charles II is in exile, bouncing between France and the Netherlands. Then in 1660, the king comes back. And he brings new laws.

No preaching outside the Church of England. No unauthorized gatherings. You need a license from the state.

Friends warn him: Stop, or they'll come for you.

He can't. He won't.

November 1660. He gets tipped off about a raid. But he shows up anyway to preach at a farmhouse in a little hamlet called Lower Samsell.

Just as he's about to start, authorities burst in. They arrest him on the spot. Bind him. Drag him before the local justices.

The charge? "Devilishly abstaining from church and holding unlawful meetings."

He stands firm. Looks them in the eye and says: "If you release me today, I will preach tomorrow."

The justices sentence him to three months—or longer, if he won't promise silence.

He won't.

So the door clangs shut on Bedford County Jail.

Family left behind. Including his blind daughter.

Ministry over. Voice silenced.

But in that damp, crowded cell... something begins to stir.

That's the kind of strange journey God sometimes uses.

And it's exactly where we pick up with Paul today.

## Part 1: The Setup (The Courtroom)

Last week, we left Paul standing right there in front of King Agrippa.

Now, you need to understand... this wasn't really a trial. It was more of a show.

Festus is barely two weeks into a job that he didn't really ask for. And on the other side, you have the chief priests and the religious leaders, and they are pushing hard.

They want Paul handed over. Not because they want justice. They want to kill him.

So Festus has a problem.

He has the prisoner. He has the politics.

But he doesn't have a charge.

At least... not one he can put in writing.

So now he's running around holding this public hearing. It looks official. It sounds official.

But it solves nothing.

Paul is in chains. And he's watching these men shuffle his life around.

Then... he does something that, on the surface, looks completely unspiritual.

He doesn't ask for an angel.

He doesn't pray for an earthquake.

He doesn't beg.

He uses his legal right.

He looks the governor right there in the eye, and he says one sentence:

"I appeal to Caesar."

And just like that... the room changes.

That sentence shuts the door on his freedom.

Agrippa said later, "We could have let him go."

But Paul didn't want freedom. He wanted Rome.

He kicked open the door that Jesus had already promised: "You must testify also in Rome."

Festus talks with his council. He comes back and gives the verdict:

"To Caesar you appealed? To Caesar you're gonna go."

[PAUSE]

Think about that for a moment.

The chains Paul is wearing? He wasn't wearing them because he loved suffering. He wasn't a glutton for punishment.

No. He's just over it.

He's spent two years in a Caesarean dungeon.

He's ready to go to Italy.

He is done being managed by people with agendas.

He decides right then and there: If I'm going to stand trial, it's going to be in the highest court in the world.

And let me tell you, that one decision?

It launches the strangest missionary journey in the New Testament.

It's going to involve a grain ship out of Alexandria.

A Nor'easter off the coast of Crete.

Fourteen days of total darkness.

And a snake... that thinks it has nothing better to do than bite an Apostle.

---

## Part 2: The Voyage (The Spreadsheet vs. The Storm)

October, AD 59. Yom Kippur just passed. Safe Season over.

→ Paul warns them. They ignore him.

Owner looking at spreadsheet, not clouds. Phoenix is 40 miles. Gentle south wind.

Fatal bet. Euroclydon — typhonic northeaster.

It grabs the ship. Instantly, the experts lose control.

They start throwing cargo overboard. Buying time.

→ Then the lights go out.

Acts 27:20 — "Neither sun nor stars appeared."

No stars = no GPS. Blind. Starving. Drifting. Fourteen days.

They are going blind. They are starving. And they are drifting in a hurricane in total darkness.

---

## Part 3: The Crash

It's the fourteenth night. Midnight.

The sailors hear something.

Surf breaking on rocks.

So they drop a weight to check the depth.

120 feet.

A few minutes later... 90 feet.

The bottom is coming up to meet them.

They drop four anchors from the back and pray for daylight.

And right here is where the chain of command breaks down.

The sailors decide to they’re going to jump ship…literally. They pretend to lower anchors from the front, but they're actually lowering the lifeboat. They are going to row away and leave 200 people to die on a sinking ship.

Paul sees it. He shouts to Julius: "Unless these men stay on the ship, we are all going to die!"

And the soldiers don't hesitate. They cut the ropes.

And the lifeboats fall into the sea... empty.

Now nobody is leaving. They are all in this together. Sink or swim.

So here they are, stuck in the dark, pitching back and forth. Paul stands up.

He says, "You've been starving for 14 days. You're weak. You need to eat."

And then Paul, who isn’t the kind of man to say “I told you so,” except for the fact that Paul is absolutely the kind of man to say "I told you so", says:

"I told you we shouldn't have left Crete. You should have listened to me then... but listen to me now."

"Last night, an angel of the God I belong to stood beside me. And he said, 'You are going to stand before Caesar.' And because of that... I'm going to live."

"And God is so good, that He has granted me the lives of every single one of you. The ship is gone. The cargo is gone. But not one hair of your head is going to be lost."

Paul takes bread. He gives thanks. And he eats.

And for the first time in two weeks... the panic stops.

Morning comes. They see a bay.

They cut the anchors. They raise the sail. They aim for the beach.

But the wind is still violent. The ship is moving too fast.

They hit a sandbar.

BOOM.

The bow hits the sand and sticks fast.

The stern is left exposed to the crashing waves.

One wave. Two waves. The wood snaps.

The ship begins to tear apart.

The soldiers realize they have a problem. Under Roman law, if a prisoner escapes, the guard pays with his life.

So they draw their swords. They are going to execute Paul and the prisoners right there on the deck.

It's not personal. It's protocol.

But Julius intervenes. "STOP!"

Why? Does he just like Paul?

Or maybe he realizes... Paul is the only man on this boat who has been right every single time.

He orders them: "Those who can swim, jump first. The rest of you... grab a plank."

So they jump.

It is chaos. Men fighting the surf. Holding onto pieces of the hull.

They wash up on the beach, one by one.

Then they count heads.

10...

50...

100...

200…

276…

Paul was right. The experts were wrong.

The ship is kindling. The cargo is at the bottom of the sea.

But the promise held.

---

## Part 4: The Viper (The Shake)

They wash up on the beach. They are freezing. It's raining.

The locals are incredibly kind, and they start building a fire right there on the sand.

Now look at Paul.

He's the Apostle. He's a Roman Citizen. He just saved the ship.

He could sit by the fire like a dignitary.

But what is he doing? He's gathering sticks.

He grabs a bundle of brushwood and piles it on the fire.

And the heat wakes something up.

A viper.

It comes out of the wood and strikes Paul's hand.

And it doesn't just bite and release. The text says it fastened on.

It locked its jaws.

The locals see it. And they know exactly what this means.

They say, "This man must be a murderer."

"He escaped the sea... but Justice has not allowed him to live."

In their worldview, the universe has a police force.

The ocean missed him, so the snake got him.

So they pull up a chair.

They wait for him to swell up.

They wait for him to drop dead.

But Paul doesn't scream. He doesn't ask for a medic.

He just looks at the snake... and flicks his wrist.

The snake falls into the fire.

And Paul goes back to work.

The locals are watching.

Five minutes pass. Nothing.

Thirty minutes. No swelling. No fever. No death.

And suddenly, the verdict changes.

Thirty minutes ago, they said, "He's a murderer."

Now they say, "He must be a god."

Paul isn't a god. But he serves One.

And the God he serves just made a statement on that rain-soaked beach:

The sea couldn't take him. The snake couldn't take him.

You can't kill a man that God has an appointment with.

---

## Part 5: The Healing (The Delay)

So the "First Man" of the island—a guy named Publius—takes them in.

He owns the land near the shipwreck. He's wealthy.

But he has a problem in the back room.

His father is dying.

Luke gives us the diagnosis: Fever and dysentery.

The old man is burning up and wasting away.

Paul walks into the room.

He doesn't check his vitals. He doesn't call for Luke.

He prays.

He lays his hands on him.

And the fever breaks.

The old man stands up. He walks out of the room.

And word spreads fast.

Suddenly, everybody on that island who has a sickness... they start coming to the house.

And they keep coming.

One after another.

For three months, Paul is stuck on Malta.

He has no ship. He has no schedule. He has no plan.

But God never wastes a delay.

God wrecked a ship to bring healing to an island.

---

## Part 6: The Departure (The Provision)

Three months later, a new ship arrives. The "Castor and Pollux."

It's time to go.

But watch this contrast.

Paul arrived on Malta with nothing. He was a wet, shivering prisoner with a snake hanging off his hand.

But when he leaves?

Verse 10 says the islanders "honored them in many ways."

They loaded them up with supplies. They gave them everything they needed.

He arrived empty. He leaves full.

He arrived as a castaway. He leaves as a blessing.

God didn't just save Paul from the storm.

He used the storm to save the mission.

---

## Disclosing the Clue to the Resolution

That's the story. But there's a question we haven't answered yet.

How was Paul so sure?

Think about it.

Experienced sailors. A ship owner with money on the line. A Roman centurion responsible for getting prisoners to Rome alive.

Why would any of them listen to a Jewish preacher in chains?

And later—when the soldiers draw their swords to kill the prisoners—why does Julius stop them?

The text says he wanted to save Paul.

Why?

I think they saw something.

Not a title. Not credentials.

They saw a man who wasn't panicking when everyone else was.

They saw a man who broke bread and gave thanks while the ship was falling apart.

They saw steadiness. And they didn't know where it came from.

But we do.

Remember Acts 16? Paul and Silas are in a Philippian jail. Beaten. Chained. Midnight. They're praying and singing hymns. And God sends an earthquake. The chains fall off. The doors fly open. That's the story we love to preach.

But that's not the only story.

In Acts 23, Paul's in Jerusalem. The religious leaders want him dead. And that night—alone in the barracks—the Lord stands beside him and says: 'Take courage. You testified in Jerusalem. Now you're going to testify in Rome.' No earthquake. Just Presence. And a promise.

Now here we are in Acts 27. The storm is raging. Fourteen days of darkness. The ship is coming apart. Paul prays. He gives thanks. He breaks bread. And there's no earthquake. The chains stay on. The storm keeps raging. But an angel shows up and says: 'Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar. And God has granted you the lives of everyone on this ship.'

Same God. Same Presence. Different method.

- While the sailors are sneaking off in lifeboats...
    
- While the soldiers are drawing swords to kill the prisoners...
    
- Paul is standing there with quiet confidence.
    
- Not because he knew an earthquake was coming.
    
- But because he knew who was standing with him.
    
- Sometimes God sends the earthquake.
    
- Sometimes you get shipwrecked and snake-bitten.
    
- But you always get Presence.
    

And Presence is enough to keep you steady when the world is swinging from one extreme to the other.

---

## The Turn

Now let me ask you something.

What's your storm right now?

Because I know what mine is. And I think I know what some of yours are too.

You turn on the news and it feels like the world is coming apart. Laws that should mean something don't get enforced. People who should be trustworthy aren't. Last week a church in Minnesota got interrupted by protesters—in the middle of a service—and nothing happened. No consequences. No accountability.

And you sit there thinking... Does anyone have this under control?

You've got voices coming at you from everywhere. Everybody's got an opinion. Everybody's got a prediction. And half of them are trying to scare you into clicking something.

It feels like we're on a ship with no stars. No GPS. Just... drifting.

Here's what I want you to see.

Paul was on a ship full of experts who got it wrong. He was surrounded by agendas. He was chained to a system that didn't care about him.

And the storm still couldn't stop what God had promised.

The snake couldn't stop it.

The soldiers with drawn swords couldn't stop it.

You can't kill a man that God has an appointment with.

And you can't cancel a mission that heaven has already scheduled.

So here's the question:

What if the chaos you're living in isn't evidence that God lost?

What if it's the route?

## Experiencing the Gospel

"That's great for Paul. He's an Apostle. He gets angels. He gets the Lord standing beside him in the barracks. What about me?"

Fair question.

Let me show you something.

Do you know what the name "Immanuel" means?

It means "God with us."

That's not a Christmas decoration. That's a job description.

When Jesus showed up, He wasn't visiting. He was moving in.

And here's what most people miss:

Jesus went through His own storm.

He was handed over by religious leaders who wanted Him dead.

He was shuffled between politicians who didn't want the responsibility.

He was beaten. Mocked. Chained to a system that didn't care about justice.

And then He died.

But death satisfies.

It locked its jaws on Him... and He shook it off.

Three days later, He walked out of that grave the same way Paul walked off that beach.

And before He left, He made a promise.

"I am with you always. Even to the end of the age."

That's not a slogan. That's a covenant.

And He didn't just promise to be with you. He sent His Spirit to be in you.

You're not waiting for an angel to show up.

The Presence already lives in your chest.

Paul could stand steady on a sinking ship because he knew God was with him.

You can stand steady in a world that feels like it's coming apart... for the same reason.

Not because you know how it ends.

But because you know who's standing with you while it unfolds.

---

## Anticipating the Consequences

So what does this actually change?

Let me tell you what it doesn't change.

It doesn't mean the storm stops.

Paul still got shipwrecked. Paul still got bitten. Paul still spent three months stuck on an island he didn't plan to visit.

The Presence doesn't mean you get a pain-free life.

But here's what it does mean.

It means you don't have to panic when everyone else is.

It means you can break bread and give thanks while the ship is coming apart.

It means when the experts are wrong and the systems fail and the voices won't stop... you've got something they don't.

You've got Someone standing with you.

And here's the thing about Paul on that ship.

He didn't just survive. He became the steadiest man on board.

The sailors looked to him.

The centurion protected him.

276 people ate because he told them to.

When you know the Presence is with you, you stop being a victim of the chaos.

You start becoming an anchor in it.

Your kids are watching you watch the news.

Your coworkers are watching you respond to uncertainty.

Your neighbors are watching how you handle the same fear they're drowning in.

And when they see someone who isn't panicking...

When they see someone who can name the storm and still have peace...

They're going to ask the same question those sailors asked:

Where does that come from?

And you'll have an answer.

Not a formula. Not a political opinion. Not a prediction about how it all ends.

Just a Presence.

And a promise that hasn't failed yet.

Remember that preacher I told you about at the beginning?

The tinker. The one they arrested for preaching without a license. The one who said, "If you release me today, I will preach tomorrow."

His name was John Bunyan.

He sat in that cell for twelve years.

Twelve years.

He could have walked out any time. Just sign a paper. Promise to stop preaching. Go home to his blind daughter.

He refused.

And somewhere in that cell, he picks up a pen. And he starts writing.

What comes out is a little book called The Pilgrim's Progress.

Did you know that for 200 years, it was the most-read book in the English language after the Bible?

It's been translated into over 200 languages.

It shaped how we talk about faith.

C.S. Lewis said it was one of the books that shaped him most. So did Spurgeon. So did countless missionaries who carried it into unreached places.

It's been adapted into films, plays, even video games.

How did he last twelve years in that cell?

Same way Paul lasted on that ship.

Not willpower. Not stubbornness.

Presence.

Listen to what Bunyan himself wrote from that prison:

"Jesus Christ was never more real and apparent than now; here I have seen and felt Him indeed."

He called that cell a place where God visited him.

And after one hearing where they refused to release him, he wrote this:

"At my return to prison, I did meet my God sweetly in the prison again, comforting me and satisfying me that it was His will and mind that I should be there."

→ Did you hear that?

He didn't meet bitterness in that cell. He met God. Sweetly.

No earthquake. No open door. Just Someone standing with him in the dark.

And that was enough.

→ The cell became his writing desk.

What looked like an ending was actually a beginning.

→ That's what Presence does. It doesn't remove the delay. It redeems it.

## Invitation

Bunyan met God sweetly in a cell. Paul met Him steady on a sinking ship. Let me ask you a question: where do you need to meet Him?

Where are you panicking right now?

Is it the money math you keep doing in your head, and it never works out? Rent, groceries, car repair, and now your 401K grows less and less secure?

Is it your kids? School stuff. Behavior stuff. Screen stuff. The fear that you cannot protect them from what's out there, and you're one parent trying to hold back an ocean.

Is it 2 a.m.? That old familiar time when your mind turns into a courtroom and you're the defendant and the prosecutor. You can't shut it off. You're tired, but you can't rest.

Is it singleness, where you did everything "right" and you're still alone, and you're mad at God and ashamed that you're mad?

Is it the way politics is poisoning everything, even church, and you're walking on eggshells around people you used to trust?

Or is it deeper than all of that?

You're not even sure God is near anymore. You've prayed. You've cried. You've tried. And you're sitting here thinking, "I believe, but I'm empty."

Maybe you're not even sure why you came today. You just knew you couldn't sit at home again.

Listen to me.

If that's you, you don't need to fake strength. You don't need to impress anybody.

You need Presence.

So right now, just open your hands.

Take a breath.

And pray like a human being:

"Jesus, I'm scared. I'm worn out. I don't know what to do. But You promised You would be with me. So I'm asking You to be near, and to steady me."

And if you need help, hear this too:

Getting help is not betraying faith.

Talking to someone is not weakness.

Medication is not a moral failure.

You are not "less Christian" because your nervous system is on fire.

So here's the invitation.

If you need that steadiness, if you need prayer, if you need someone to stand with you, don't sit there alone in your row.

Come. Or raise your hand. Or grab me after.

Not because you've got it all figured out.

But because you're done trying to survive the storm by yourself.

Paul arrived on that island empty, shivering, with a snake on his hand. He left loaded down with everything he needed.

You came in here carrying something. You don't have to leave the same way.

Come.
