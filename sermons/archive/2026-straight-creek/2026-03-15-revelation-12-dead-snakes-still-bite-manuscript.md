# "Dead Snake Still Biting"

### Revelation 12:1–17 | The War of the Lamb

  

---

  

## THE COLD OPEN

  

Let me tell you a story.

  

It happened on May 27, 2018. Memorial Day weekend. Jeremy and Jennifer Sutcliffe are outside their home near Lake Corpus Christi, Texas. Getting ready for a cookout. Doing yard work. Pulling weeds. Just a regular afternoon — so it seemed.

  

Jennifer reaches for a patch of flowers and she stops.

  

Because hiding right there in the dirt was a western diamondback rattlesnake. Four feet long.

  

She screamed. She bribed the nail because I would have ran. Her husband does run — he comes running with a shovel, swings it down, and takes that snake's head clean off.

  

Threat eliminated. Or so it seems.

  

About ten minutes pass. Now that detail matters. Ten minutes. That's enough time to believe the danger's gone. Jeremy bends down to clear the remains. He reaches for the severed head.

  

And it struck.

  

Jaws open. Fangs locked through Jeremy's right hand. The severed head held on for thirty-five seconds — just pumping venom into his bloodstream. He had to physically tear it off his hand. And the snake didn't deliver a warning strike. He unloaded the clip.

  

Jennifer's a nurse. She knows they don't have time to wait. She puts him in the truck, they take off to the hospital. On the highway the venom starts taking over. He's having seizures. Fading in and out of consciousness. Goes blind for a while. He's bleeding out and he looks at his wife and says — *if I die, I love you.*

  

They meet an ambulance. Paramedics see the severity and call a helicopter. He's airlifted to the hospital already in septic shock. Blood pressure collapsed. Organs shutting down. Doctors put him in a medically induced coma and hook him to a ventilator.

  

A normal snakebite takes two to four vials of anti-venom.

  

It took twenty-six for Jeremy. Twenty-six doses and he still almost didn't make it. Acute kidney failure. Dialysis. Almost lost his hand.

  

All from a dead snake.

  

According to snake experts, a severed rattlesnake head can stay lethal and strike for up to ninety minutes. They're cold-blooded. Their nervous system doesn't shut down like ours does. The fangs still work. The venom's still lethal.

  

It's dead. But the head didn't get the memo.

  

Hold that image. We're going to need it before this morning is over.

## CONTEXT

  

We are in week ___ of our series through Revelation — The War of the Lamb.

  

And I need to tell you something about where we've landed before we step into this vision.

  

Everything that came before chapter 12 — the seven seals, the seven trumpets — it all moves forward. It's a timeline. Events unfolding. Judgments rolling out. The momentum builds and builds until the seventh trumpet sounds in chapter 11 and a voice announces — *the kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of his Christ.*

  

And then John stops.

  

He stops the clock. He pulls back the curtain. And chapters 12 through 14 become a massive parenthesis — a pause in the action to answer the most pressing question the whole book has been building toward.

  

*Why is the church being hunted?*

  

Not just in John's day. In every day. Why has there been organized, sustained, ferocious opposition to the people of God since the beginning of time? Why does it feel personal? Why does it never fully stop?

  

Before you can understand what God is doing to the empire, you have to understand why the empire is doing what it's doing to you.

  

That's what chapter 12 is for.

  

G.K. Beale calls it the key to the whole book. The structural center. The moment John pulls back the veil and shows you the spiritual reality underneath every earthly conflict.

  

So let's step into the vision.

## THE SCENE

  

The sky splits open.

  

And the first thing you see is a woman.

  

She's standing on the moon. The sun is wrapped around her like a robe. On her head — twelve stars arranged like a crown. She is radiant. She is enormous. She is the most beautiful thing you have ever seen.

  

And she is in labor.

  

Screaming. Bent over. Every muscle in her body working against the weight of what she's carrying. Whatever she's about to bring into the world — it's almost here.

  

And then you see the second thing.

  

Behind her.

  

Red. Enormous. Seven heads, ten horns, and on every head a crown. His tail sweeps across the sky and a third of the stars come down like burning rocks. The ground shakes when they land.

  

He sees the woman.

  

And he doesn't move toward her.

  

He moves toward the ground in front of her.

  

He positions himself at the place where the child will be born.

  

He's not hunting the woman. Not yet. He is waiting for the baby.

  

The moment that child hits the ground — he intends to swallow it whole.

  

[Pause]

  

Before I tell you what happens next — let me tell you who you're looking at.

  

---

  

## WHO SHE IS

  

That woman.

  

She's not a literal woman. She's not Mary, though Mary is in her somewhere. She's bigger than that.

  

John is pulling from a dream you've read before. Genesis 37. Joseph is sleeping and in his dream the sun and the moon and eleven stars are bowing down to him. His father hears it and says — are your mother and I and your brothers going to bow down to you? The sun. The moon. The twelve tribes.

  

John takes that image and puts a crown on it.

  

This woman is the whole people of God. She is every generation that ever carried the promise. She is Eve, who first heard the word *seed*. She is Sarah, who laughed and then stopped laughing. She is Israel in Egypt, crying out under the weight of four hundred years. She is the prophets who looked down the tunnel of time and saw something coming they couldn't fully name. She is every mother, every exile, every faithful remnant that kept the fire burning when it looked like the fire was going out.

  

She's been pregnant with this promise for a long time.

  

And now — finally — it's time.

  

---

  

## WHO HE IS

  

Now look at the dragon.

  

Red. Seven heads. Ten horns. Crowns on every head. His tail alone is enough to pull a third of the stars out of the sky.

  

John names him four times in one sentence. Chapter 12, verse 9. *The great dragon. That ancient serpent. The devil. Satan.* Four names. One face.

  

And that phrase — *that ancient serpent* — is John making sure you don't miss something. He is reaching all the way back to a garden. To a conversation that started with *did God really say.* To a curse that ended with *I will put enmity between you and the woman, between your seed and her seed.*

  

Same enemy.

  

He's been at this since the beginning.

  

And here's what he knows — standing in front of that woman, waiting — he knows the promise. He was *there* when God said it. He knows that one day a child is going to be born who will end him. He's been hunting that child through every generation. He came after Moses in a basket. He came after the line of David when Athaliah tried to kill every royal heir. He came after the promise every time it looked like the promise might survive.

  

And now the woman is in labor.

  

And he thinks — *this is it. This is the one. And I am going to be right here when it happens.*

  

He doesn't know what you know.

  

He doesn't know what that child is going to do before he's done. He doesn't know about a cross and an empty tomb and a throne. He's calculating based on every enemy he's ever beaten, every promise he's ever delayed, every fire he's ever put out.

  

He has never met this child before.

  

So he waits.

  

---

  

## THE CHILD

  

And the child is born.

  

A son. A male child.

  

And God says this about him before the dragon can move: *he is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron.*

  

That's a quote. John's congregation knew it. It's Psalm 2. Written a thousand years before this moment. A king is enthroned in Zion and God looks at every empire, every power, everything that has ever set itself against his purposes — and he laughs. *You think you're a threat?* He installs his king anyway. And he says to that king — ask me. I will give you the nations as your inheritance. You will break them with a rod of iron. You will shatter them like pottery.

  

The dragon has been running from that psalm his entire existence.

  

But here's what the dragon gets wrong about the rod.

  

That word *rule* — in the Greek it's *poimainō.* It doesn't mean conquer. It doesn't mean dominate. It means *shepherd.*

  

He will *shepherd* the nations with a rod of iron.

  

The dragon understands Caesar's rod. He understands the iron fist, the crushing heel, the boot on the neck. That's his language. That's how power works in every system he has ever built or broken.

  

But this king doesn't come with Caesar's rod. He comes with a shepherd's.

  

And if you know your Psalm 23 — the shepherd has two tools. A rod and a staff. The staff pulls the sheep back from danger. The rod drives off the predator. Same shepherd. Two different directions. One for the wolf. One for the wandering lamb.

  

The dragon hears *rod of iron* and thinks he knows what's coming.

  

He doesn't understand that the rod coming for him is the same rod that's been protecting the sheep.

  

He has never been on the receiving end of a shepherd before.

  

[Beat]

  

Every tyrant who ever thought they held that rod found out the hard way.

  

Og of Bashan. Giant king east of the Jordan. His bed was made of iron — thirteen feet long. He thought the iron made him immovable. He's a footnote now.

  

Nebuchadnezzar. Built a gold statue ninety feet tall and told the world to bow or burn. Ended up eating grass in a field until he acknowledged who actually runs the world.

  

Herod the Great. Used a literal iron sword to kill every boy under two in Bethlehem trying to stop one child. Died in agony, eaten by worms, while the child he hunted grew up to sit on the throne of the universe.

  

Domitian. The very emperor who exiled John to Patmos. Demanded to be called Lord and God. Stabbed to death by his own household staff. The man he exiled was writing words that would outlast the Roman Empire by two thousand years.

  

Every one of them held a rod. Every one of them thought it was iron. Every one of them is gone.

  

There is only one rod that hasn't broken yet. And it's in the hand of a shepherd.

  

[Pause]

  

So the dragon opens his mouth to devour once and for all.

  

And the child is gone.

  

Snatched up to God and to his throne.

  

John skips everything — the manger, the miracles, the ministry — and jumps straight from birth to throne. But don't miss what happened between the birth and the throne.

  

Because the dragon did get his moment. He used the iron of Rome to nail the Shepherd to a tree. He used the silence of a tomb to try and seal the story shut. For three days he stood over a grave and thought — *I finally broke the rod.*

  

He was wrong.

  

On the third day the rod of iron didn't just break the seal of the tomb. It shattered the power of death itself. The resurrection was the moment the Shepherd turned the rod away from the sheep and toward the wolf. He didn't just survive death. He subverted it. He took the very thing the dragon uses to terrify the nations and turned it into the doorway to his throne.

  

The snatching up isn't a rescue mission for a victim. It's a victory march for a conqueror.

  

No fight. No contest. No close call. The dragon is still calculating and the child is already at the throne. Daniel saw that coronation in Daniel 7. The Ancient of Days. The Son of Man. The throne. The kingdom that will not end.

  

The dragon is standing in an empty field.

  

He missed.

## THE WILDERNESS

  

He's not coming after you because he's winning.

  

He's coming after you because he has nothing left.

  

There's a military doctrine called Dead Hand. The Soviets built it during the Cold War. North Korea has a version of it now. The idea is simple and terrifying: if the regime is ever decapitated — if the leadership is taken out — the system automatically launches. Every warhead. Every missile. No human hand required. If I go down, I'm taking everyone with me.

  

That's verse 17.

  

The child is on the throne. The woman is in the wilderness, fed and carried. The dragon has failed at every objective. So he triggers the failsafe. He turns toward the rest of her offspring — everyone who holds the testimony of Jesus — and launches everything he has left.

  

Not because he has a winning strategy.

  

Because he doesn't.

  

Look at what he's been doing ever since.

  

The Jewish people have carried the mark of that enmity for four thousand years. Pharaoh. Haman. Antiochus. Rome. The Crusades. The pogroms. The Holocaust. There is no people on earth who have been hunted with more consistency, more ferocity, more organized hatred. You can say what you want about almost any group in public and find applause somewhere. Say it about the Jews and you've crossed a line that even secular culture feels. That instinct — that almost involuntary recognition that something uniquely sinister is at work — is not just political. It's theological. The dragon has never stopped trying to erase the woman. He knows where the promise came from. He's been trying to cut the root.

  

And then the church.

  

Rome fed them to lions. Nero lit them on fire. Diocletian erected a monument that read: *the name of Christians has been extinguished.* China tried to erase them during the Cultural Revolution. North Korea puts them in labor camps today. Iran. Nigeria. Eritrea. India. The persecution of the global church is not a footnote. It is the consistent, unbroken reality of what it costs to carry the testimony of Jesus in a world where the dragon is still launching.

  

Three failures and a failsafe. That's his entire history.

  

---

  

But here's what you need to see.

  

The wilderness is not where God lost his people.

  

The wilderness is where God *met* them.

  

Verse 6 — the woman flees into the wilderness. And look at the language. She doesn't stumble into it. She doesn't get lost and end up there. She runs to a place *prepared by God.* That word — prepared — it's the same word Jesus uses in John 14. *I go to prepare a place for you.* God scouted the location before the woman ever needed it. The wilderness was furnished before the dragon ever turned around.

  

She's fed there. Twelve hundred and sixty days. Three and a half years. A broken seven — evil gets a measured season. Never a complete one.

  

And if you're a student of this Book — you've seen this before.

  

Exodus 19. Moses at the base of Sinai. God has just ripped his people out of the most powerful empire on earth — through water, through desert, through hunger and thirst and a pharaoh who wouldn't quit. And before the commandments, before the tablets, before any of it — God tells Moses what to say to the people. And this is what he says:

  

*You yourselves have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself.*

  

Not — I showed you the way out. Not — I gave you a map. *I carried you.*

  

Deuteronomy 32 fills in the picture. A mother eagle stirs the nest. Hovers over her young. Spreads her wings. And when the young one falls — she swoops underneath and catches it on her back.

  

God says: that's what I did. Egypt was the fall. The Red Sea was the fall. The desert was the fall. And every time you dropped — I was underneath you.

  

Now look at Revelation 12:14.

  

The woman is given two wings of the great eagle so she can fly to her place in the wilderness.

  

Same eagle. Same God. Same wings.

  

John is not being subtle. What God did for Israel at Sinai, he is doing for the church right now. The wilderness you're in — that season, that stretch, that place where it feels like God forgot your address — that's not where God lost you. That's where God carries you.

  

And then the serpent tries one more thing. Verse 15. He opens his mouth and pours out a flood after her. Trying to sweep her away. And if you're a first-century Jew hearing this read aloud, your spine just straightened. A body of water. God's people running. An enemy behind them. Pharaoh sent chariots. The serpent sends a flood. Same play. Different era.

  

And the earth opens its mouth and swallows the river.

  

Exodus 15 — the sea swallowed Pharaoh's army. Numbers 16 — the earth opened and swallowed Korah's rebellion. The ground itself fights for the woman. Creation takes her side. Because the God who made the dirt told the dirt what to do before the serpent ever opened his mouth.

  

Three failures. Count them. The dragon failed to devour the child. Failed to drown the woman. Failed to reach her in the wilderness. And verse 17 — he turns, furious, toward the rest of her offspring. That's you. That's everyone who holds the testimony of Jesus.

  

He's not coming after you because he's winning. He's coming after you because he has lost every other target. The child is on the throne. The woman is in the eagle's grip. And the dragon — the one with seven heads and ten horns and a tail that swept the stars — is reduced to chasing the offspring because the mother and the firstborn are both out of reach.

  

The wilderness is not punishment. It's not exile. It is the place God prepared.

  

And the Spirit led Jesus there too.

  

When the Father said at the Jordan — *this is my beloved Son* — where did the Spirit take him? Into the wilderness. Not away from it. Into it.

  

Luke says the Spirit led him there. Mark says the Spirit drove him there. The same Spirit that hovered over the waters in Genesis. The same wings that carried Israel out of Egypt. And the first thing the Son does before his public ministry — before one miracle, one sermon, one disciple — is forty days in the desert.

  

Being tested by the same dragon.

  

And he came out.

  

Not depleted. *Full of the Spirit.* Luke 4:14. He went in led by the Spirit. He came out in the power of the Spirit.

  

The wilderness doesn't drain what God put in you. Sometimes it's the only place deep enough to hold it.

  

---

  

## THE WEAPON

  

So John is writing to people living in the ninety minutes.

  

Not martyrs yet — most of them. They haven't been fed to lions. But they've been pushed out of the trade guilds. You couldn't work in Thyatira without belonging to a guild. No guild membership, no income. No income, no food. They've been socially cancelled, economically strangled, publicly shamed. The empire didn't always need to kill you. Sometimes it just needed to make you invisible.

  

And John says — *this is how you conquer.*

  

Not by taking up arms. Not by waiting for rescue. Not by going quiet and surviving until it passes.

  

*They conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony.*

  

Let me slow down on that first phrase.

  

That word *by* — in the Greek it's *dia* with the accusative case. It doesn't mean instrument. It means *ground.* It means *basis.* They conquered *on the basis of* the blood of the Lamb. Not on the basis of their courage. Not their strategy. Not how clean their record was or how long they'd been faithful.

  

The blood of the Lamb is not a spiritual technique. It's a legal verdict.

  

Here's why that matters. The dragon's entire power rested on one thing — your guilt. He's called the accuser for a reason. Revelation 12:10 — *the accuser of our brothers, who accuses them before our God day and night.* Day and night. Filing charges. And every charge was real. He didn't make them up. He didn't have to.

  

But the moment the Lamb was slain, the debt was cancelled. Colossians 2 says God nailed the certificate of debt to the cross. The accuser walks into the courtroom, opens his file, and the Judge says — *paid in full. Case dismissed. You have no standing here.*

  

The dragon has been disbarred.

  

He still files. He still accuses. He still shows up at the throne with your name on a folder. But the Judge keeps saying the same thing. *That debt was settled at Calvary. Get out of my courtroom.*

  

[Pause]

  

And then — *the word of their testimony.*

  

The Greek is *martyria.* It's where we get the word martyr. In John's world, to open your mouth and say *Jesus is Lord* in a city where Caesar demanded that title — that was not a bumper sticker. That was a declaration of war. That was Michael's battle, fought down here, with a human voice, under the Spirit's anointing.

  

Let me show you what that looks like in flesh and blood.

  

177 AD. Lyons, Gaul. The Romans drag a group of Christians into the amphitheater. The crowd wants blood. And in this group is a slave woman named Blandina.

  

She is the least significant person in that arena by every Roman measure. Small. Female. A slave. Nobody. The kind of person the empire doesn't even count.

  

The torturers work on her in shifts. All day. Every method they have. They exhaust themselves on her and walk away. The ancient letter says they admitted it themselves — *we have nothing left. We don't know what to do with her.*

  

Her confession never changed.

  

Through all of it — the same words. *I am a Christian. And nothing vile is done among us.*

  

That's it. That's the whole testimony. Eleven words. A slave woman. No sword. No army. No political power. Just eleven words she refused to stop saying.

  

The early church didn't call her a victim. They called her a conqueror. A noble athlete who defeated the ancient serpent.

  

That's what a testimony does.

  

Revelation 19:10 says the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy. When you open your mouth under the Spirit's anointing and declare that Jesus is Lord — you are not just encouraging somebody. You are Blandina in the arena. You are wielding the weapon that threw the accuser out of the courtroom.

  

Your testimony is not peripheral to the battle.

  

It *is* the battle.

  

This is for the person in this room who stopped talking about Jesus because somebody laughed at you. That was the dragon playing his hand. He doesn't need to kill you if he can silence you. Every time you go quiet, he wins the round without throwing a punch. Your testimony is a weapon. Don't lay it down.

  

[Pause]

  

And then the third thing. *They loved not their lives even unto death.*

  

The dragon's last card is always the same card. Fear of death. Fear of loss. Fear of what happens if you don't comply, don't compromise, don't go quiet. He played it in the garden. He played it in Egypt. He played it in Rome. He's still playing it.

  

But when you reach the place where Jesus is worth more to you than your own survival — when you hold your life loosely enough to keep your testimony — he has nothing left to play. You just took his last card.

  

That's not recklessness. That's the wilderness doing its work.

  

Forty days in the desert. Came out full.

  

The wilderness teaches you what you actually need to live on. And it turns out — it's not the empire's approval. It's not the guild membership. It's not the comfort of fitting in.

  

It's the bread that comes down from heaven.

  

Jesus went into the wilderness led by the Spirit. He came out in the power of the Spirit. He went to the cross knowing what the third day looked like. He held his life loosely because he knew who held it for him.

  

And what he modeled — the blood that covers, the testimony that conquers, the life surrendered and therefore unlosable — that's the pattern. That's what the overcomer looks like in every generation.

## THE CALLBACK

  

Remember Jeremy Sutcliffe.

  

Twenty-six vials. Septic shock. Organs shutting down. Blind on the highway telling his wife he loved her.

  

From a dead snake.

  

The head was already gone. The dragon was already defeated. But the venom was still real. The fangs still worked. The threat was not imaginary — Jeremy almost died. Don't let anybody tell you the enemy isn't dangerous just because he's defeated. The ninety minutes after the head comes off are still ninety minutes.

  

But here's what the experts said.

  

Every strike that severed head takes is the last of what it has. It cannot regenerate. It cannot regroup. It cannot call for reinforcements. It is spending itself. Every lunge is one fewer lunge left. The venom runs out. The nervous system winds down. And the countdown was set the moment the shovel fell.

  

The shovel fell at Calvary.

  

That's why the dragon is raging. That's why the attacks feel intense. That's why it seems like things are getting worse before they get better. He knows his time is short — Revelation 12:12 says it plain. He is not winning. He is panicking. Every strike he takes is the last of what he has. He is spending himself on you.

  

And the venom has an antidote.

  

Not two vials. Not twenty-six. It doesn't run out. It doesn't get rationed. It's the blood of the Lamb and it's infinite and it's already been applied to everyone in this room who has called on his name.

  

The countdown was set at Calvary.

  

The dragon knows it.

  

The question is whether you know it.

## ALTAR CALL

  

If you're in this room today and the venom has been working on you — not a literal snakebite, but the poison of shame, of accusation, of the voice that says *God is done with you, you've gone too far, the charges against you are too many* — that voice has a name. And he has been disbarred.

  

The blood of the Lamb answers every charge he files. Every one.

  

There are two groups I want to speak to right now.

  

If you've never brought your guilt to that blood — if you've been living under the weight of a case that was dismissed two thousand years ago and you've never walked into that courtroom and said *I'm claiming what the Lamb did for me* — I want to give you the chance to do that right now. That's why we're here. That's why the Lamb was slain. The blood is for you.

  

And if you know the blood but you've gone quiet — if the guild pressure got to you, if the social cost felt too high, if somewhere along the way you decided survival was worth more than testimony — Jesus is not standing over you with condemnation.

  

He's standing over you the way he stood over Peter on the beach.

  

*Do you love me?*

  

That's still the question. Not how badly you failed. Not how long you've been quiet. Not what you said or didn't say or walked away from. Just — do you love me?

  

Because if you do, the testimony isn't over. The war isn't over. And the blood still speaks.

  

The shovel has already fallen.

  

Come on up.
