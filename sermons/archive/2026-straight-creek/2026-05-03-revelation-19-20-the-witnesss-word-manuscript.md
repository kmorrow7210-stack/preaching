## MOVE 1 — Heaven Opens And The Rider Is Faithful

When I was a kid, there was a movie I'd watch over and over again: The Karate Kid. I love the moment when Daniel does the crane kick and Johnny gets what's coming to him. Daniel gets the trophy. Daniel gets the girl, and Johnny just got called a cream puff. Don't you just love it? And Back to the Future, when George McFly finally knocks Biff out? We've been built to want vindication. And when I read the book of Revelation, that's what I expect to get. I'm ready for that moment when Jesus finally cuts loose.

We're in Revelation 19, and I want to ask you a question, something I think a lot of us have asked. It's a question I believe the people in John's first audience were carrying when they received this letter. The question is this: when is Jesus finally going to show up and do something about this?

You ever asked that question? Have you ever had that moment, maybe watching the news, eating dinner with your family, just observing all that's going on, and ask Lord, "How long?" We see that question in Revelation 6, and it matters because we've all wanted Jesus to do something at some point. We wanted Him to clean it up, to right the wrongs. "Vengeance is mine," says the Lord. "Well, when is He going to enact it, because we've been faithful and the wickedness keeps getting louder, and sometimes we just want to know, Lord, how long?"

When I was a kid, there was a movie I'd watch over and over again: The Karate Kid. I love the moment when Daniel does the crane kick and Johnny gets what's coming to him. Daniel gets the trophy. Daniel gets the girl, and Johnny just got called a cream puff. Don't you just love it? And Back to the Future, when George McFly finally knocks Biff out? We've been built to want vindication. And when I read the book of Revelation, that's what I expect to get. I'm ready for that moment when Jesus finally cuts loose.

Because when you read Revelation 19, it sounds like that's exactly what's happening. John sees heaven opened up. He sees a white horse, yes, that white horse, and the rider on the horse. His eyes are like a flame of fire, and he has many crowns on his head, and he's wearing a robe, but not just any robe, a robe that's been dipped in blood. There are armies of heaven following him, and they have white horses, and a sword is coming out of his mouth, and it's going to strike the nations. He's going to tread the winepress of the wrath of God Almighty. I really don't know what it means, but it sounds awesome, because you read that and you think, "Finally, this is it. This is the moment Jesus is going to turn it around. The Lamb is going to roar."

But I want to slow it down just a moment, because Jesus does something on purpose right at the beginning of this scene.

- Before John tells us about the eyes
- Before John tells us about the crowns
- Before John tells us about that blood-dipped robe
- Before he tells us about the sword and the winepress and the armies

he tells us the rider's name. The name is the first thing we get.

> Then I saw heaven opened, and behold, a white horse. The one sitting on it is called Faithful and True.

Faithful and True. That's the first thing John tells us about the man on the horse: his name.

That's not a warrior's name. That's not a vengeance name. That's not a name you give a guy that's run up the stairs at the Philadelphia Museum of Art. Faithful and True is the name of a witness. Faithful and True is the name you'd put on a man who told the truth and never backed down from it.

And this isn't the first time John has used this name in the book. All the way back in chapter 1, John opens his letter, he says, "Grace and peace from Jesus Christ," and what does he call him? He calls him the Faithful Witness, same word, the Faithful Witness, the Firstborn from the Dead. That's chapter 1.

Then in chapter 3, when Jesus is writing the letter to the church at Laodicea, what does he call himself? He says, "The words of the Amen, the Faithful and True Witness." There it is again, Faithful and True, same exact phrase. We're going to come back to that word for witness in just a moment.

So when John gets to chapter 19 and he says the rider's name is Faithful and True, he's not introducing a new Jesus. He's not introducing the angry version. He's not introducing the version that finally cracked. He's introducing the same Jesus he's been showing you since chapter one: the Lamb, the Faithful Witness, the One who told the truth all the way to a cross and never backed down.

The man on that white horse, he's not a different Jesus. He is the same Lamb, but he's been vindicated. He's the same Witness, but he's been glorified. He's that same Faithful One now revealed in front of every nation, every tribe, and every tongue. The name he gets first is not Conqueror. The name he gets first is not Avenger. The name he gets first is not Judge. The name he gets first is Faithful.

Now let's think about that. In this Revelation, what is it that Jesus wants us to know? It's that the way he reigns is the same way he conquered. He didn't change methods. He didn't switch tactics. He didn't lay down the cross and pick up a different weapon.

The Rider on the white horse is Faithful before he is anything else, because the Kingdom of God runs on faithfulness. It was faithfulness on a cross that bought us. It's faithfulness on a horse that vindicates us. Same name, same Jesus, same victory.

So when we ask that question, Lord, when are you finally going to do something? The answer is this: Jesus is the Faithful One. Jesus is patient because Jesus is faithful. One of these days the heavens are going to crack open, and the Rider is going to come on that white horse, and the church and the world are going to see what the church has known the whole time: the Faithful One was telling the truth. The Faithful One was keeping his word. The Faithful One was reigning the whole time.

He has a weapon. So let's see what that weapon is.

## MOVE 2 — The Sword Is in His Mouth

And John says something strange. He says, "From his mouth comes a sharp sword."

Now slow down right there. Because John does not say the sword is in Jesus' hand. He does not say the sword is hanging on Jesus' side. He does not say Jesus is swinging the sword over His head. John says the sword comes from His mouth.

And that matters. Because a sword from the mouth is not about Jesus needing a metal weapon. It is about Jesus speaking. It is about the power of His Word. It is about truth. The sword is in His mouth because Jesus wins by speaking what is true.

Now think about that. In Revelation, the enemies of God use lies. The beast lies. The false prophet lies. Babylon tricks people. The dragon, who is Satan, accuses people and deceives people. He fools them. He tells them things that are not true. He makes evil look good. He makes sin look normal. He makes rebellion against God look powerful.

But Jesus does not fight lies with more lies. Jesus fights lies with truth.

The beast deceives. The Lamb speaks. The dragon tricks people. Jesus tells the truth. Babylon seduces people. Jesus exposes the lie.

That is why the sword is in His mouth. Jesus' weapon is His Word. And when Jesus speaks, lies start falling apart. When Jesus speaks, fake power gets exposed. When Jesus speaks, evil cannot hide anymore.

Rome thought power was a sword in Caesar's hand. Rome thought power was armies, threats, prisons, and death. But John says real power is not in Caesar's hand. Real power is in Jesus' mouth.

One word from Jesus is stronger than every empire on earth. One sentence from Jesus can defeat every lie Satan ever told. One command from Jesus can judge evil. One truth from Jesus can set people free.

That is what Revelation is showing us. Jesus does not become like the beast in order to beat the beast. Jesus does not use Satan's methods to defeat Satan. Jesus does not use deception to defeat deception. Jesus wins as the Lamb. He wins by truth. He wins by testimony. He wins by the Word of God.

And this is not a brand-new idea. The Bible has been teaching this for a long time. The prophet Isaiah said the Messiah would judge with His mouth. He said His words would be like a sword. Hebrews says the Word of God is sharper than any two-edged sword. That means God's Word can cut deeper than a regular weapon. It can reach the heart. It can show what is true. It can reveal what people are really thinking. It can judge what is hidden.

So when John sees a sword coming from Jesus' mouth, he is showing us something important. Jesus conquers by declaring the truth. His Word is the weapon. His truth is the sword. His mouth carries the authority of God.

And that connects straight to where we are headed in Revelation 20. Because in Revelation 20, John sees faithful followers of Jesus sitting on thrones. These are people who suffered because they would not deny Jesus. They were faithful witnesses. They kept telling the truth about Jesus, even when it cost them. They did not beat Rome by becoming Rome. They did not defeat the beast by acting like the beast. They did not win because they had bigger armies or sharper swords. They won because they stayed faithful to Jesus. They won because they kept their witness. They won because they refused to lie. They won because they held on to the Word of God.

So here is the pattern. The Rider's sword is in His mouth. The church's weapon is its witness. Jesus conquers by the Word. His people overcome by telling the truth about that Word.

Same kingdom. Same pattern. Same victory.

That means the way Jesus wins teaches us how the church is supposed to fight.

We do not fight lies with lies. We do not fight manipulation with manipulation. We do not fight evil by becoming evil. We do not fight Babylon by becoming Babylon. We do not fight the beast by becoming beastly.

We fight deception with truth. We fight darkness with light. We fight fear with faithfulness. We fight pressure with obedience. We fight lies by telling the truth about Jesus.

So when you tell the truth about Jesus, that matters. When you refuse to lie just to fit in, that matters. When you stay honest even when it costs you, that matters. When you keep following Jesus even when people make fun of you, that matters. When you confess Jesus publicly, that matters. When you live with integrity, that matters. When you keep your witness, that matters.

Because witness is a weapon. Faithful preaching is a weapon. Clear truth-telling is a weapon. Public confession is a weapon. Sacrificial obedience is a weapon. Refusing to deny Jesus is a weapon. Not because we are powerful on our own. But because Jesus' sword is in His mouth. His Word is powerful. His truth wins. His testimony cannot be stopped.

So church, hear this. The world may think power means control. The world may think power means force. The world may think power means being louder, stronger, richer, or more popular. But Revelation shows us something different.

The King of kings rides out with a sword in His mouth. That means truth is stronger than lies. The Word of God is stronger than the beast. The testimony of Jesus is stronger than Babylon. And the faithful witness of the church is not weak. It is how we fight. It is how we overcome. It is how we share in the victory of Jesus.

So do not put the sword where John did not put it. The sword is not in His hand. The sword is in His mouth.

The Rider's weapon is the Word. And our weapon is witness.

## MOVE 3 — The Dragon Is Bound by an Unnamed Angel

So we've watched the heavens open. We've seen the Rider. We've heard about the sword that comes out of His mouth. And if we stopped right there, you might think the next thing that happens has to be a duel. The Rider versus the dragon. Sword versus claws. King of kings versus the ancient serpent. That's what we'd expect. That's how the movies do it.

But watch what John actually shows us when chapter 20 opens.

> Then I saw an angel coming down from heaven, holding in his hand the key to the bottomless pit and a great chain.

Not Jesus. Not the Rider on the white horse. Not Michael the archangel. Not Gabriel with a trumpet. An angel. Unnamed. Unidentified. No title, no biography, no introduction. Heaven doesn't even stop long enough to tell us his name.

And church, that ought to tell us something about evil.

Because by the time we get to Revelation 20, the dragon has had all kinds of names. John has already called him "the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is the devil and Satan." He's got history. He's got reputation. He's got aliases. He's been deceiving since the garden. He's been accusing since Job. He's been raging through this whole book.

But when heaven decides to bind him, God does not send somebody famous. God sends an unnamed angel.

That means the dragon is real, but he is not ultimate. He is ancient, but he is not eternal. He is dangerous, but he is not sovereign. He is loud, but he is not Lord.

Y'all hear me?

See, sometimes evil looks so big from down here because we are standing too close to it. We are reading the news close. We are watching the diagnosis close. We are sitting at the kitchen table close, looking at the bills close, listening to the fight in the next room close. And from that close, the dragon looks like he is swallowing the sky.

But Revelation pulls the camera back. Revelation lets us see evil from heaven's side. And from heaven's side, the dragon does not require a duel with Jesus. He does not require the Rider to dismount. He does not require the Lamb to panic.

One unnamed angel comes down with a key and a chain. And look at the verbs, church.

He seized him. He bound him. He threw him. He shut it. He sealed it.

The dragon is not negotiating. The dragon is not winning. The dragon is not in control. The dragon is handled.

And don't miss what is in the angel's hand. The key.

The dragon does not have the key. Hell does not have the key. The deceiver does not have the key. The key is in the hand of heaven.

That means evil can rage, but it cannot rule. Evil can deceive, but it cannot decide. Evil can be released for a little while, but it cannot release itself. Even the devil's timeline is on God's calendar.

And John tells us why he is bound. He says, "so that he might not deceive the nations any longer."

There it is again. Deception.

That has been the dragon's weapon all along. In chapter 19, the false prophet deceived people by signs. In chapter 20, Satan is bound so that he cannot deceive the nations. And when he is released for that little while, what does he do? He goes right back out and tries to deceive the nations.

So Revelation is showing us something. Evil's deepest power is not creativity. It is deception. The dragon does not create truth. He twists it. He does not build a kingdom. He counterfeits one. He does not make life. He parasitizes what God has made. He lies. He accuses. He distorts.

But when heaven speaks, deception starts losing its territory.

So what does it say about evil that the dragon is bound by an unnamed angel? It says evil is not as strong as it advertises. It says hell has better marketing than authority. It says the dragon may be ancient, but he is still bindable. It says Satan may have many names, but heaven can handle him with a servant whose name we never even learn.

And that ought to encourage somebody this morning.

Because some of us have been giving the devil too much credit. We talk like he is everywhere. We talk like he is equal with God. We talk like every hard season means hell is winning. But Revelation says no.

The devil is not God's opposite. The dragon is not the Lamb's equal. The beast is not the church's final future. The deceiver is on a leash, and heaven is holding the chain.

So yes, evil is real. Yes, deception is dangerous. Yes, the dragon still hates the people of God. But don't confuse noise with sovereignty. Don't confuse rage with rule. Don't confuse a released enemy with an uncontrolled enemy.

Because when the time comes, heaven does not panic. Heaven does not improvise. Heaven sends an unnamed angel with a key and a chain. And the dragon gets bound.

Now if that is true, church, everything changes for the witnesses. Because if evil's weapon is deception, then the church's weapon is witness. And if the dragon can be bound by heaven's unnamed servant, then the saints who stayed faithful have not been wasting their lives.

Their testimony matters. Their witness matters. Their truth-telling matters.

Evil is loud. But the witness of Jesus is louder.

## MOVE 4 — The Witnesses Are On Thrones

I want you to come with me to a rock in the middle of the Aegean Sea. The year is somewhere around 95 AD. The rock is called Patmos. And there is one old man on this rock. He's the last one. His name is John.

Now stay with me for a minute. Because to understand Revelation 20, I think you have to understand the man writing it.

John was one of the twelve. He was one of the three Jesus pulled aside on the mountain. James, Peter, and John. The inner circle. The ones who saw him glow at the Transfiguration. The ones who fell asleep on him in the garden.

You remember the day James and John came up to Jesus and asked if they could sit at his right and his left in the kingdom? Mark says it was the brothers. Matthew says it was their mama. The accounts vary a little. But you know how mamas are. Whoever brought it up, the request was on the table.

And Jesus looked at them and he said, "Can you drink from the cup I'm going to drink from?"

And they said, "We can."

And Jesus said something almost matter of fact. He said, "You will."

Now I want you to imagine being James. James was the first. Acts 12 says Herod ran his sword through James, and James drank the cup. Beheaded. Not even the first chapter of church history is finished, and one of the inner three is gone.

Then it kept going. Tradition tells us Andrew on a cross. Bartholomew flayed alive. Peter, the one Jesus said "feed my sheep," crucified upside down because he doesn't feel worthy to die the way the Lord did. Thomas run through with a spear in India. Philip. Matthew. Thaddeus. James the son of Alphaeus.

One by one, the brothers go home.

And John? John gets old. John is the last man standing. Of the twelve who walked with Jesus, John is the one left holding the lamp.

And listen, we read that as honor. We hear the story of John on Patmos, the beloved disciple, the one who lived the longest, and we think, "What a blessing."

But I want you to put yourself in John's sandals for just a second.

He has watched every one of his brothers go home. He preached the gospel in Ephesus. He was discipling the next generation. And then Rome decides he's a problem too, and church tradition says they boil him in oil. And the oil doesn't take him. So Rome doesn't know what to do with him. They can't kill him. So they exile him. They put him on a rock. Patmos. A piece of stone in the middle of the sea, not quite ten miles long. No church. No congregation. No brothers. No bread, no cup, no fellowship. Just an old man and the seagulls and the sound of the wind off the water.

Paul wrote in 2 Corinthians, "To be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord." That's the verse we put on funeral programs. That's the hope. To go home is gain.

But what happens when everybody goes home and you don't? What happens when the cup you wanted to drink, the cup you said you could drink, the cup Jesus said you would drink, gets passed to your brother and not to you?

Could you imagine John on that island? An old man. Alone. Watching the sun rise over water. Knowing James is with the Lord. Knowing Peter is with the Lord. Knowing his brother and the rest are with the Lord. And John is still here. Still breathing. Still old. Still alone.

I'm telling you, church. Sometimes faithfulness can feel like punishment. Sometimes obedience can feel like being left behind. Sometimes you can be the one God kept and feel like the one God forgot.

And that's the man writing this book. That's the man Jesus shows up to. "I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last." That's the man who hears the trumpet and turns and sees the Son of Man with eyes like a flame of fire. That's the man we're reading.

Now turn with me back to chapter 6 for a minute. Stay with me. Revelation 6, when the fifth seal opens. John writes this. He says, "I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slain for the word of God and for the witness they had borne."

Under the altar. That's the location. The faithful witnesses are under the altar. And what do they say? "How long, O Lord, holy and true, until you judge and avenge our blood?"

How long. That's the cry of every faithful witness who ever paid the price. How long, Lord. James paid. Peter paid. Thaddeus paid. How long?

And they're given white robes and told to wait a little longer. Just a little longer.

That's chapter 6. Hold that picture. Faithful witnesses, under the altar, in white robes, asking how long.

Now jump fourteen chapters with me. Revelation 20. John says, "I saw thrones, and seated on them were those to whom the authority to judge was committed. I saw the souls of those who had been beheaded for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God."

Did you catch what just happened? Same group. Same witnesses. Same beheaded saints. Chapter 6 had them under the altar. Chapter 20 has them on the thrones. They moved. From under the altar to on the throne. From asking how long to sitting in judgment. From white robes to crowns.

Heaven did not forget one of them.

And I want you to understand what John is seeing. He's an old man on a rock who has watched every one of his friends die for Jesus. And now he sees them. He sees James. He sees Peter. He sees Thaddeus. He sees them standing up. Resurrected. Like the bones in Ezekiel's valley. Bone to bone. Sinew to sinew. Breath into them. An army of witnesses on their feet.

And I think something hits John on that rock that's worth us hearing this morning.

The Greek word for martyr. Y'all know what it is? Martus. And it doesn't mean dead. It means witness. The person who tells the truth. The person who refuses to lie about what they saw.

The early church started using martus for the ones who paid in blood, because most of them did. But the word itself just means witness.

And I think on Patmos, looking at the thrones, John finally got it.

You don't have to lose your head to be a witness. You don't have to be crucified upside down to be a faithful witness. You don't have to die in a Roman arena to wear the crown. You just have to remain faithful. Wherever Jesus put you. However long he keeps you. With whatever cup he hands you.

James drank a short cup, and James is on a throne. John drank a long cup, and John is on a throne. Both of them witnesses. Both of them faithful. Both of them vindicated.

Because here's the thing the dragon never figured out. Witness is the weapon. Witness is the throne. Witness is the cosmic authority Jesus has been handing his church the whole time.

That's why the Rider's sword comes out of his mouth. Because the gospel itself is the conquering weapon. That's why the dragon got bound by an unnamed angel. Because evil is loud, but the witness of Jesus is louder. That's why the saints under the altar are saints on the throne. Because faithfulness is what gets enthroned in the divine court of heaven.

Listen, church. If you're sitting here this morning and you feel forgotten. If you feel like the cup got passed. If you feel like you've been faithful and you've been faithful and you've been faithful and nothing has changed, I want you to hear what John heard on that rock.

Heaven has not forgotten one faithful witness.

Not one.

Not James. Not John. Not your grandmother who prayed for thirty years. Not your daddy who took you to church when nobody else would. Not the brother who got cancer and stayed kind. Not the sister who got divorced and stayed faithful. Not the prodigal's mama who's still standing at the door.

Heaven keeps a record under the altar. And one day heaven moves you from under the altar to on the throne.

Revelation 6 showed them under the altar. Revelation 20 shows them on the thrones.

Your witness is not wasted.
