**

## Controlling Question

Whose voice will form you for what comes next?

## Big Idea

God forms people for futures they cannot yet see by teaching them to listen and obey in hidden places.

## Opening (~4 min)

Graduation Sunday usually asks a good question.

What are you going to do next?

Where are you going to school?

What job are you taking?

What is the plan?

Those are real questions.

I am not minimizing them.

But 1 Samuel 3 puts a deeper question underneath all of them.

Whose voice is going to form you for what comes next?

Let me ask you another question.

You ever find yourself sitting in your recliner, maybe laying in bed, scrolling?

Laughing at a couple TikToks.

Crying at a couple of them.

Next thing you know it is one o'clock in the morning and you are wondering where all the time went.

And somewhere in that scroll, you are carrying a major decision.

Your family is saying you need something stable. Good benefits. Close to home.

Your friends are texting you to chase your passion, refuse to settle, build your brand, scream "you do you" on one hand—and threatening to cancel you if you do something they disagree with on the other.

All the while you are telling yourself, "If I do not make something of myself right now, I will never be enough."

What if I make the wrong choice?

What if the window closes?

What if I end up ordinary?

And the answer the world gives?

Hustle harder.

Be exceptional.

Or be nothing.

Every post.

Every notification.

It looks like somebody else is ahead.

You are alone in a crowded room.

A room where success gets measured in likes, square footage, and bottom lines.

That is the room a lot of graduates are walking into this morning.

But it is not only graduates.

Parents feel it.

Young adults feel it.

The whole church feels it.

We are all living in a loud room.

And the text we are in today is about a boy coming of age in a loud room of a different kind.

Open with me to 1 Samuel chapter 3.

## Series Bridge + Text Intro (~2 min)

If you have been with us in this Samuel series, you already know how we got here.

In chapter 1, Hannah carried a prayer nobody else understood.

She poured it out before the LORD.

And when God answered, she did not clutch the gift.

She surrendered Samuel back to God.

In chapter 2, Shiloh got darker.

Hophni and Phinehas treated holy things like they belonged to them.

Eli saw enough to speak, but not enough to restrain.

And while that old house was collapsing, Samuel was quietly growing before the LORD.

Chapter 2 ends with a question Israel cannot answer on its own.

If anyone sins against the LORD, who will intercede for them?

That is not a small question.

That is the whole problem of a compromised priesthood.

Who will stand between a sinful people and a holy God?

Chapter 3 is God's answer beginning to rise.

Not with a platform.

Not with a coronation.

With a boy lying down near the ark while the lamp is still burning.

## Movement 1 — The Lamp Had Not Gone Out (~10 min)

Verse 1 says, "Now the boy Samuel was ministering to the LORD in the presence of Eli."

Samuel's not a hero.

He's just a boy.

Growing up in a house under judgment.

And then the text gives us one of the scariest sentences in the Bible.

"The word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no frequent vision."

Think about that.

They still had the religion.

They still had the sanctuary.

They still had the priest.

They still had all the activity.

People were showing up.

People were making sacrifices.

But God's word was rare.

That's a scary spiritual condition.

Because it's the kind of thing that will sneak up on you if you're not careful.

It's not produced by empty religion.

It's produced by busy religion.

More going through the motions without having a relationship with God.

Shiloh stayed busy while the living word grew rare.

And it was loud there too.

Not the sensory overload we're used to.

But from the coast they had the Philistines about to attack.

And folks were scared.

Not gas-at-four-dollars-a-gallon scared.

More like our village is about to be burned and we're going to be taken as slaves scared.

And it wasn't a bunch of atheists there.

They were religious.

But their religion was noisy.

They hedged their bets.

To them, Yahweh was a loophole, not Lord.

He was there because if you were going to be a good Israelite, you had to look the part.

But they also had their backup gods.

Whatever helped the crops come in.

Whatever helped the kids stay healthy.

All of those different faiths get crowded.

And it gets really loud.

So when verse 1 says the word of the LORD was rare, it's not that people weren't hearing anything.

They heard the threat of enemies coming in.

They heard multiple voices of faith.

They would visit a sanctuary that stayed busy.

Why was there no word from God?

Let's look at the man of God.

Verse 2 says Eli's eyes had begun to grow dim so that he could not see.

Now we know that is physical.

But it is not just physical.

Because when his two sons were messing up, he just gave them a slap on the wrist.

He was committing the sin of intentional blindness.

The spiritual muscle memory of ignoring.

But let's go to verse 3.

"The lamp of God had not yet gone out, and Samuel was lying down in the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was."

The lamp was low.

But it wasn't out.

Anyone in here ever feel like you're low but not out?

You know why?

Because God can still speak in a dim room.

God can still find a faithful remnant in a blind, dead priesthood.

When it looks like the house is falling apart, God can still form the future.

Those of you that are graduating, you're not stepping out into a quiet world.

You're stepping out into a crowded world.

The issue is not whether voices are going to speak.

They're going to holler at you.

The issue is, are you going to let God's voice be the voice that forms you?

The lamp hasn't gone out.

God's not finished.

He's not through speaking.

## Movement 2 — The Lord Called the Boy (~9 min)

Verse 4 says the LORD called Samuel.

And Samuel said, "Here I am."

That sounds beautiful.

And it is beautiful.

But it is also incomplete.

Samuel is available.

Samuel is responsive.

Samuel is willing.

But Samuel does not yet know who is calling.

Verse 7 makes that plain.

"Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD, and the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him."

Hear that.

Samuel is in the house of God.

Samuel is ministering before the LORD.

Samuel is growing up in the sanctuary.

And he still does not know the LORD's voice.

That should humble every one of us who grew up around church.

Proximity is not the same as recognition.

Activity is not the same as intimacy.

Serving near holy things is not the same as hearing the living God.

So when the LORD calls, Samuel runs to Eli.

"Here I am, for you called me."

Eli says, "I did not call. Lie down again."

The LORD calls again.

Samuel runs to Eli again.

The LORD calls again.

Samuel runs to Eli again.

And there is something patient in that.

God does not despise the boy for not recognizing His voice yet.

He keeps calling.

He keeps speaking.

He keeps pursuing.

You may not get it right the first time.

Hear me: That's okay.

God is not looking for perfect discernment on the first ring.

He is looking for availability.

Just be available when He calls.

Samuel keeps saying, "Here I am."

Even when he is confused.

Even when he runs to the wrong voice.

Even when he does not have the future figured out.

Finally Eli realizes what is happening.

Verse 9—Eli tells Samuel what to say.

"Go, lie down, and if he calls you, you shall say, 'Speak, LORD, for your servant hears.'"

That is the posture.

Not, "Lord, make me impressive."

Not, "Lord, give me every detail."

Not, "Lord, make the future easy to manage."

Not, "Lord, tell me how to win."

Speak, LORD.

Your servant hears.

And thousands of years later, God hasn't changed.

That is still who God uses.

Not the one who got there by legacy.

Not the one who got there by taking shortcuts.

But the one who will continually lay before the Lord and say, "Speak, God, I am listening to you."

Before Samuel became a voice to Israel, he became an ear before God.

Think about Eli. He could barely see, lying there in the dark in a sanctuary where nobody was hearing from God. He watched his own sons turn the house of the Lord into a place of ill repute. But now this boy has come to him three times. And Eli realizes he is not having a dream. This is God.

After all these years, God is speaking again.

He is not speaking to me.

He is not speaking to my boys.

He is not speaking to the ordained priest.

He bypassed all of us, and He is speaking to this boy.

It reminds me of Simeon in the temple.

All that silence, and suddenly there is a fresh word from heaven.

That is what is going on with Eli.

Yes, he has lost his edge.

He has lost his sons.

He is about to lose his house.

But he has got enough of God in his spiritual memory to teach this boy how to listen.

He doesn't let jealousy stop him.

He says, "Go and say, 'Speak, LORD, I'm listening.'"

Don't just count out someone because of their failures.

Just because someone may have lost their vision doesn't mean they cannot impart wisdom into you.

And I want the adults in the room to hear me today.

The generations coming behind us are being formed by our faithfulness or our passivity. Even if we feel like we cannot see what is going on, we can at least pass on that posture of a listening servant.

## Movement 3 — The Listener Becomes a Witness (~8 min)

Look at what God actually says to this boy in verses 11 through 14. It is not a word of reassurance. It's judgement. God tells Samuel, "Behold, I am about to do a thing in Israel at which both ears of everyone who hears it will tingle". God declares that He is about to judge Eli's house forever. Why? Because Eli's sons brought a curse upon themselves, and Eli did not rebuke them.

I want you to feel the sheer danger of this moment.

We love the story of Nathan the prophet confronting King David.

That took incredible courage.

But Nathan was a grown, established prophet.

Samuel is a boy.

He has no platform.

He has no title.

He has no power.

Eli is not just an old man; he is the high priest.

He is the acting judge of Israel.

He holds absolute legal authority over the sanctuary, and he holds Samuel’s daily survival in his hands.

And it is not just a power dynamic; it is a relational crisis.

Eli is Samuel’s mentor.

Eli is his surrogate father.

Just moments prior, it was Eli who patiently taught Samuel how to recognize the voice of God.

It was Eli who gave him the words, "Speak, LORD".

And Samuel’s very first prophetic assignment is to turn around and tell this spiritual father that his legacy is in ruins.

That is a staggering burden for a boy.

And the text does not pretend it was easy.

Verse 15 tells us exactly how Samuel felt: "So Samuel lay down until morning... But Samuel was afraid to tell the vision to Eli".

He lies there in the dark, terrified, holding a word that is going to shatter his mentor's world.

He only speaks because Eli forces his hand.

Eli calls him and places a heavy oath on him: "May God do so to you, and more also, if you hide anything from me".

So Samuel tells him everything.

He hides nothing.

And to his credit, Eli humbly receives it, saying, "It is the LORD; let Him do what seems good to Him".

Graduates, hear this clearly.

When God forms you to hear His voice, He is not just giving you a life-hack for personal success.

He is not just handing you a clear five-year plan or a stepping stone to your brand.

He is forming the kind of resilience that can carry a hard truth.

The listener must become a witness.

The voice that forms you will also teach you what truth to carry.

It is easy to say, "Speak, LORD," when we want a blessing or an opportunity.

It is entirely different to say, "Speak, LORD," when His word requires you to confront the very compromise that everyone else is tolerating.

A tool can give you answers, but it cannot give you obedience.

God is forming people who can stand faithfully when obedience costs something.

So do not only ask God for a plan.

Ask Him for ears.

If you say, "Speak, LORD," you must be prepared to carry what He says—even when it makes you afraid, even when it costs you your comfort, and even when you feel like you have no power in the room.

## Application (~3 min)

So let me land this in three directions.

To graduates.

The world will ask what you are going to do next.

That matters.

School matters.

Work matters.

Plans matter.

But here is the deeper question.

Whose voice will form you while you live those plans out?

Parents. Peers. Fear. Ambition. Social media.

All of it will try to name you.

Learn to say, "Speak, LORD," before you say, "Here is my plan."

Do not let the loudest voice in the room become the voice that names you.

To parents and adults.

Hannah and Eli both stand behind this story.

One surrendered in prayer.

One failed in restraint.

The next generation is always being shaped by both kinds of examples.

Do not only prepare young people for achievement.

Help them recognize the voice of the LORD.

Give them ears before you give them pressure.

To the whole church.

The lamp has not gone out.

The LORD is still speaking.

The question is whether we will become a people who can say, "Speak, LORD, for your servant hears."

## Gospel Turn (~4 min)

In Samuel's day, the word of the LORD was rare.

But the gospel says the Word became flesh and dwelt among us.

Remember the question hanging over Shiloh.

If anyone sins against the LORD, who will intercede for them?

Samuel becomes a bridge.

He carries the word.

He stands between a compromised house and a holy God.

But Samuel is still only a boy. Still only a man. Still only a prophet in a line of failing priests.

The true answer to Eli's question is not Samuel.

It is Jesus.

Jesus is the faithful Prophet who speaks God's word without corruption.

Jesus is the faithful Priest who never treated the Father's things with contempt.

Jesus is the King who looks at the heart and makes a people for Himself.

He did not just sleep near the ark.

He became the mercy seat for us.

He broke the silence.

He called us by name.

He went all the way into death so that the lamp of God would never go out for the people He came to save.

We do not listen our way into being loved by God.

We listen because the Word has already come to us.

He has spoken first.

He has opened the way home.

And now He is still calling.

Not always with a map.

Often with a name.

Samuel.

Church.

Graduate.

Parent.

Speak, LORD.

Your servant hears.

Give them ears before You give them platforms.

Give them obedience before opportunity.

Give them courage to carry truth when truth is costly.

Form them in hidden places.

Teach them to say, "Speak, LORD, for your servant hears."

And for the rest of us—parents, mentors, pastors, friends—make us the kind of people who help the next generation hear You.

Not only with advice.

With prayer.

With surrender.

With faithful lives.

Keep the lamp burning in this church.

Keep speaking.

Keep calling.

Keep forming servants who will carry Your word into the future we cannot yet see.
