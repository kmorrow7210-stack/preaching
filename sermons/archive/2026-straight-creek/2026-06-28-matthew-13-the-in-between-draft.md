## Opening **\[NEW — keep or cut\]**

I want to be honest with you this morning. I am not a farmer. I grew up on this mountain around people who could grow anything, and somehow that gene skipped me. Tessa will hand me a plant and say, "Just water it," and I will look that plant dead in the eyes and somehow both drown it and starve it in the same week. We have a graveyard on our back porch. I have killed cactus. You are not supposed to be able to kill a cactus.

So when Jesus starts talking about seeds and soil and weeds, part of me wants to tap out. But stay with me. Because this morning Jesus is not really giving us a gardening lesson. He is telling us the truth about the season we are living in right now. The season between the planting and the harvest. The in between.

Let us pray.

\[invocation\] Lord, I thank You for Your living Word. Your Word is sharper than any two-edged sword, and it is true. I ask You this morning to anoint me, God, to season my words and let them be Your words. Speak to the people who are tired of waiting. Speak to the people who are doing right and still fighting weeds. Give us fresh revelation, God, not to add to what You are saying, but to confirm it. In Jesus' name. Amen.

## Opening Text — Matthew 13:24–30 (ESV)

Hear the Word of the Lord.

"He put another parable before them, saying, 'The kingdom of heaven may be compared to a man who sowed good seed in his field, but while his men were sleeping, his enemy came and sowed weeds among the wheat and went away. So when the plants came up and bore grain, then the weeds appeared also. And the servants of the master of the house came and said to him, "Master, did you not sow good seed in your field? How then does it have weeds?" He said to them, "An enemy has done this." So the servants said to him, "Then do you want us to go and gather them?" But he said, "No, lest in gathering the weeds you root up the wheat along with them. Let both grow together until the harvest, and at harvest time I will tell the reapers, Gather the weeds first and bind them in bundles to be burned, but gather the wheat into my barn."'"

This is the Word of the Lord.

## OOPS — How We Got Here

Matthew gives us an account in chapters 11 and 12\.

We see Jesus. He has been out healing the sick, casting out demons, raising the dead, and He looks at the people following Him and gives them an invitation: "Come to me. My yoke is easy, my burden is light."

That sounds like a pretty good deal, doesn't it. But look at what they do. They say, "No thank you." The cities that He blessed in Galilee, they did not accept Him. They refused to repent. All of Capernaum basically refused. Even His own cousin, John the Baptist, the one who leaped in the womb when Mary walked in the room, he is sitting in a damp prison cell sending messengers to Jesus saying, "Are You the one, or do we need to wait for somebody else?"

It seems like everyone is wavering. And then it gets more intense.

They bring a man to Jesus. He is demon-possessed, blind, and mute. Can't see, can't speak, a prisoner in his own body. And Jesus sets him free just like that. The man can see. The man can talk. The crowd goes wild and they begin asking, "Could this be the Son of David?" The Pharisees, the religious leaders who are supposed to know better than anyone, look at what happened and they say, "He casts out demons by Beelzebub, the prince of demons."

Jesus had just done something only God can do. He walked up to a man the enemy had completely bound, blind, mute, tormented, and He set him free. The religious folks' response was to say He was doing it by the power of the devil. They did not even say, "We don't understand this." They did not walk away confused. They looked straight at the power of God and they called it the power of Satan. That is not just doubt. That is an attack.

And Jesus responds. A kingdom divided against itself cannot stand. If Satan is casting out Satan, he is fighting himself. How does that work? Jesus knows exactly what is happening. He is not confused. He is not rattled. But He is human.

Matthew tells us what He does next, and he is being precise. It says that on that same day, the day of the Beelzebub accusation, the day Jesus looked at a generation that called the Holy Spirit demonic, that same day Jesus went out of the house and sat by the sea.

He just walked out. I guess He needed some air. I have been there. He needed to hear the sound of water, some space between Himself and the suffocating pressure of that house, of that crowd, of that accusation. I do not think we talk enough about the humanity of Jesus, the Son of God, fully human, needing to step outside. Sometimes we all just need a moment.

But He did not get it. Because the crowd followed Him out there too. Of course they did. Crowds do not respect exhaustion. Crowds cannot read the room. They press in, they surge forward, they want more. Matthew says they gathered in such a large number that Jesus had to get into a boat. He literally had to push off from the shore, floating on the Sea of Galilee, just so He could speak to the people and be heard.

So now we have Jesus in a fishing boat, rocking back and forth under Him. In front of Him there is a hillside absolutely packed with people. Some are genuinely hungry for the truth. Some are just there to see a show, they want another miracle. Some of them are there because they have been planted there, to criticize, to find something they can use against Him.

Jesus knows about all of them. And then He makes the decision. Jesus switches strategies right there in that boat. Up to this point He has been pretty plain: "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand." Direct, clear, no code. He has done miracles out in the open and then explained exactly what they meant. He has given Himself without any ambiguity, and look what they did. They used it against Him. Now when He opens His mouth, He does not give straight exposition. He tells them a story about a farmer and seeds and dirt.

He is not trying to hide the truth. He is revealing it to those who have ears to hear it. The people sitting on that hillside think they are hearing a good story about agriculture. But they are receiving the most important message they have ever heard in their lives.

## UGH — The Agony of the In Between

You remember those old cartoons where somebody is working on a project, and the minute they go to sleep, the rival sneaks in on tiptoes? The music gets quiet. The villain looks left, looks right, creeps across the screen, and starts cutting wires, switching signs, loosening bolts, planting something he should not plant.

That is the feel of this parable.

Jesus says the man sowed good seed in his field, but "while men slept, his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat, and went his way." The enemy does not walk in during daylight and announce himself. He comes when nobody is watching. He does his damage in secret. Then he leaves the servants to wake up and wonder, "What happened to the field?"

And Jesus uses a very specific word here. He does not just say weeds. He says *zizania*. Darnel ryegrass. Under Roman law, planting darnel in a rival's field was a criminal act. It was agricultural terrorism. Why? Because when darnel first starts growing, it looks exactly like wheat. You cannot tell them apart. But underneath the dirt, the roots of the darnel twist and braid and choke the roots of the good wheat. They steal the water. They steal the nutrients.

We know exactly what that feels like.

You decide you are going to be faithful. You decide this church is going to pursue God. You prepare the soil. You pray for revival. You do everything right. And then comes the sabotage. It feels like a thousand paper cuts. It is the critic who questions every move you make. It is the temptation to compromise the standard just to keep people happy. It is the public moral failure of a leader that shatters the witness of the church and leaves you doing damage control.

The roots of the enemy's work are twisting right around the roots of your obedience.

And here is what makes the weeds so dangerous. The enemy still knows how to work a field. He does not always walk into a church with horns and a pitchfork. Sometimes he walks in with a whisper. He plants suspicion and calls it discernment. He plants gossip and calls it concern. He plants comparison and calls it vision. He plants compromise and calls it wisdom. He plants busyness and calls it faithfulness. He plants discouragement and calls it realism.

And most of the time it does not look dangerous at first. That is what makes it dangerous. If it looked like poison, we would spit it out. If it sounded like the devil, we would rebuke it. But the enemy is more subtle than that. He plants things close enough to the truth that they can grow right beside obedience for a while. A concern can look like prayer. A complaint can look like honesty. A grudge can look like conviction. A fear can look like caution. A compromise can look like kindness.

And before long the roots are tangled. The church is still singing. Still gathering. Still using the right words. But underneath the surface, something is stealing water from the wheat. Trust is drying up. Prayer is thinning out. Joy is getting choked. Expectation is getting weaker.

And the servants wake up one morning and say, "Master, did you not sow good seed in your field? Where did this come from?"

That is not just a question about a field. That is the question faithful people ask when they are trying to obey God and still have to fight sabotage. Have you ever asked God that question? Have you ever looked at a major setback in your life or in this church and asked, "Why are there weeds still here when I am just trying to be obedient?"

The servants offer a solution. It is the human solution. "Do you want us to go and pull them up?" Let us fix it. Let us purge the critics. Let us burn the field down and start over. Let us force the kingdom to be pure right now.

And the Master looks at them and says the hardest word you will ever hear when you are living in the in between.

He says, "No."

He says if you pull the weeds now, you will destroy the wheat. Let them both grow together until the harvest.

That is the agony of the in between. God intentionally leaves us in a field full of weeds. He asks us to keep growing while the enemy is actively trying to choke us out.

## AHA — The Protective Forbearance of God

The servants ask for permission to rip the weeds out. They want immediate justice. They want to fix the field right now. But the Master says no.

Why does He stop them? The answer is buried in the dirt. Underneath the soil, the roots of the darnel ryegrass are braided tight with the roots of the good wheat. They are locked together. If the servants violently rip out the toxic weeds, they will tear the fragile roots of the genuine crop right out of the ground.

Here is the theological reversal. We look at the mess in the world and the visible church, and we think God is ignoring us. We assume His delay means divine apathy. But the Master's refusal to uproot the weeds is exactly the opposite. It is calculated, protective forbearance.

God sees the entire field. He knows our human discernment is terribly fallible. We do not know the difference between a toxic weed and a broken believer who is just struggling to grow. If God allowed a premature purge of every critic, every hypocrite, and every source of sabotage right this second, the collateral damage would destroy the church. He knows your roots are still green. He knows your faith is still taking hold in the soil.

The delay of the final judgment is not a sign that God has abandoned the field. The delay is the absolute proof of His mercy. He refuses to risk a single stalk of genuine wheat. God is not ignoring the mess. God is actively protecting you.

## WHEE — Experiencing the Gospel **\[NEW\]**

But I have to ask you something. If the weeds are this poisonous, if darnel is the one grass whose seed can actually kill you, then somebody still has to answer for it. You cannot just let poison grow in a field forever. The harvest is coming, and Jesus says the weeds get bundled and burned. There is a fire at the end of this field.

So here is the question that should keep every one of us up at night. If the standard is that pure, and the fire is that real, then how does any of us survive the harvest? Because if I am honest, I have not always been wheat. There have been seasons where the roots of my own heart got tangled with the very weeds I am preaching against. You have had those seasons too.

So how do the weeds get burned without us burning with them?

Watch what the Master does. Look back at the parable. Who sowed the good seed? Jesus says it Himself when He explains it in the house. "The one who sows the good seed is the Son of Man." That is Jesus. He is the Sower. The field is His. The wheat is His.

But stay with me. Because the Sower did something no farmer in Israel ever did. The Sower went down into the dirt Himself.

Jesus came into His own field. And the weeds He refused to uproot, the critics and the saboteurs and the religious leaders who called Him demonic, they grew right up beside Him. They tangled their roots around Him. And one Friday they did to Him what we wanted to do to them. They tore Him up out of the ground. They bundled Him like He was the weed. They handed Him over to the fire.

The Son of Man, the Sower, let Himself be treated like the *zizania* so the wheat would never have to be.

He was buried like a seed in the dark earth. And here is the gospel. The fire that belongs to the weeds fell on Him at the cross. The furnace Jesus warns about at the end of the field, He walked into it first, in our place. He took the judgment that the poison deserved so the harvest could be saved.

You are protected in the in between because He was not protected at the cross. You get to keep growing because He let Himself be cut down. The Master is not standing on the edge of the field watching you sweat. The Master came into the field, took the worst of it into His own body, and came up out of the ground on the third day, the first stalk of the harvest, the firstfruits from among the dead.

That is why He can say "let them grow together." He is not being passive. He already paid for the patience. Every day you grow in this weed-choked field, you are growing on ground that Jesus bought with His own blood. Amen?

## YEAH — Celebrating the Consequences, Anticipating the Results **\[NEW\]**

So let me tell you how this field ends. Because Jesus does not leave us guessing.

The weeds do not win. Hear me. The weeds do not win. The critic does not get the last word. The compromise does not get the last word. The leader who fell and broke your heart does not get the last word. The thousand paper cuts do not get the last word. Jesus says there is a harvest, and at the harvest everything gets sorted, and it does not get sorted by you. It gets sorted by the One who can finally tell the difference.

Listen to what He says happens to the weeds. "Gather the weeds first and bind them in bundles to be burned." Everything the enemy planted in this church, in this field, in your life, every poisonous thing he sowed while men slept, it gets gathered up and dealt with. Permanently. You have been begging God to do something about the weeds. He is going to do something about the weeds. Just not by your hand, and not before its time.

And then look what happens to the wheat. "Gather the wheat into my barn." The same field that felt like a battle becomes a harvest. The same plant that had to fight for water and fight for room and grow up next to poison gets carried into the Master's barn. Safe. Gathered. Home.

But I love how Jesus says it when He explains it in the house. He does not just say the wheat survives. Matthew 13:43. "Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father." Did you catch that? In the in between, the wheat and the weeds looked the same. From a distance you could not tell them apart. But at the harvest, all that hidden faithfulness, all that growing in the dark, all that refusing to quit, it does not just get rewarded. It gets revealed. The wheat that nobody could pick out of the crowd is going to shine like the sun.

So here is what I am anticipating. I am anticipating that the season you are in right now is not wasted time. Every prayer you prayed when nothing seemed to move, it counted. Every Sunday you showed up tired and gave God praise anyway, it counted. Every time you held the standard when it would have been easier to compromise, it counted. You were not just surviving the in between. You were being formed in it. The roots were going down even when the harvest was not coming up.

You are not on the outside looking in. You are wheat in the Master's field, growing on purchased ground, headed for the barn, and one day you are going to shine. That is the result I am anticipating. That is the consequence worth celebrating. The harvest is certain. The barn is real. And the Farmer has never lost a single stalk He intended to keep.

## MOVEMENT 3 — USE THE NET (The Close) **\[NEW framing of your Net draft\]**

Now Jesus is not done. He tells one more story in this same chapter, and it is the one I want to leave you sitting with this morning. He says the kingdom of heaven is like a net.

Hear the Word. Matthew 13:47–50.

"Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a net that was thrown into the sea and gathered fish of every kind. When it was full, men drew it ashore and sat down and sorted the good into containers but threw away the bad. So it will be at the close of the age. The angels will come out and separate the evil from the righteous and throw them into the fiery furnace. In that place there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth."

Look at how the net gets thrown. Jesus says the kingdom of heaven is like a dragnet, and a dragnet does not aim. You do not bait it. You do not pick. You drop it off the back of the boat and you drag it through the dark water and it scoops up everything the sea is hiding. Good fish, bad fish, the prize catch and the garbage, all of it comes up in the same haul. That is the grace of the gathering. The gospel goes out over the whole sea of the world and it does not check your pedigree on the way in. It takes the drunk and the deacon in the same pull of the rope. Amen? The net is wide open. The net holds everybody.

But the net is not the end of the story. Stay with me.

The net fills up and they drag it to the beach, and watch what these men do. They do not dump it and run. They do not sort it standing up in a hurry. The Bible says they sat down. Tristram, a man who studied the Holy Land, says he sat on the gunwale of the boat right alongside these fishermen and watched them go through the net by hand, one fish at a time, throwing back into the water everything too small and everything unclean. Picture it. The sun is coming down. The catch is glittering on the sand. And a man eases down onto the edge of that boat and he picks up one fish and he turns it over in his hands and he decides. Keep. He picks up the next one. Throw it back. He is not frantic. He is not in a panic. He is deliberate.

Here is what I need you to feel this morning. The judgment of God is not a wild swing in the dark. The judgment of God is a man sitting down on the shore, taking every single fish in His hands, and weighing what it is worth. We have spent this whole sermon afraid of a God who pulls weeds too violently. But that is not who He is. He does not panic. He does not rush. He sits down, and He takes every fish in His hands.

Now here is the word Jesus uses for the fish they throw away. In the Greek it is *sapra*. And you would think that means rotten, dead, stinking. It does not. *Sapra* means worthless. Unfit. Good for nothing. The commentators tell us it carries the weight of the old law, the legally unclean. Open your Leviticus. God said anything in the water without fins and scales is unclean to you, an abomination. So when that fisherman flips a fish back into the sea, he is not being picky. He is reading the law of God over that fish, and the law says this one cannot come to the table. This one has the form of a fish and the look of a fish, and it swam in the same water as the catch, and it is still cast out. It was never fit.

And I have to tell you what that means for this room.

The net of this church holds both kinds. Right now. This morning. The good and the *sapra* are in the same net, dripping on the same shore, and from a distance you cannot tell them apart. They sang the same songs. They sat in the same pews. They got pulled up in the same gospel haul. Jesus is the one who says it plain. Proximity to the catch did not make the unclean fish clean. And proximity to this altar will not make you His. The fisherman does not ask the fish what church it swam to. He turns it over in his hands and he asks one thing. Is it fit. Is it clean. Is it worth the table.

So Paul reaches across the centuries and grabs you by the collar in 2 Corinthians 13:5. "Examine yourselves," he says. Test yourselves. Do not test the person in the row in front of you. Test yourself, to see whether you are in the faith. The hands of God are going to turn every one of us over on that shore. So turn yourself over now. Do not measure your life by how close you sit to the catch. Measure it by the fruit.

## ALTAR CALL **\[NEW\]**

\[slow down here\]

The unclean fish and the clean fish came up in the same net. Only one of them was ever fit for the King's table. So which one is in your chest this morning?

I am not asking you to turn over the person beside you. I am asking you to turn yourself over, right now, in your own hands, while there is still time to do it gently.

Because here is the mercy in all of this. Right now the net is still open. Right now the field is still growing. Right now the Master has not sat down to sort yet. You are in the one window where a weed can still become wheat, where an unclean fish can still be made fit, because the Sower already went into the ground and the fire already fell on Him instead of you.

So come.

Come while the field is still growing. Come while the net is still open. Come before the sort.

If there are weeds tangled in your roots this morning, you do not have to rip yourself apart. Come let the One who knows the difference do the work. Come, you who are tired of fighting a thousand paper cuts. Come, you who have been faithful in the in between and nobody noticed. He noticed. Come, you who got pulled up in this net years ago and have never once let Him turn you over and make you fit. Come.

This altar is open. Come turn yourself over in the hands of God while His hands are still full of mercy and not yet full of the harvest.

Keep coming till the weeds let go. Keep coming till the roots get free. Keep coming till you know, not hope, but know, that when the Master sits down on that shore and picks you up and turns you over, He is going to look at you and say, "Keep. This one is Mine. Gather this one into the barn."

Let us pray.
